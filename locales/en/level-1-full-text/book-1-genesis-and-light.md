# Book I: In the Beginning and the First Light
*(Full Text Edition — Ages 9–10)*

---

## Part I: The Seven Days of Creation

### Chapter 1: The World's Beginning

Long, long ago, in the very beginning, there was not yet any earth. There were no clouds, no stars, and not even a single sun! Darkness covered everything everywhere. No animals ran about, and no birds soared through the sky. How could there be, when the earth itself did not yet exist?

And yet, was there already something then? Yes, indeed! There was a heaven—a world high above, far beyond even our stars.

In that heaven, the eye of the Creator shone like a sun. The little angels could not look straight into His radiant eyes, for that light was far too dazzling—brighter many times over than our sun, so that even a fleeting glance would blind them. Yet the greater archangels could look up for a brief moment into the Creator's eyes. They were even allowed to draw near His throne whenever they wished to speak with Him.

Throughout all of heaven, wondrous music echoed without ceasing. Flutes, lyres, and harps sang out together, blending with the long songs of the angels. No sooner had one great concert ended than a new one began. Some angels fastened golden stars onto the Creator's blue mantle, while others caught beams of light and shaped them into precious jewels. Everything was wondrously radiant.

Once, two great archangels knelt together in prayer before the Creator's throne. Rising to their feet, they flew down together through the heavenly heights, for His throne stood high and steep as a mountain peak. Suddenly, one of the two—whose name was Lucifer—paused in midair. He gazed upon the splendor of heaven and upon his own shining mantle, and thought to himself:

*"How wonderful it must be to be a god like that! My garment shines almost as brightly as the Creator's! Truly, it is bright enough for me to sit in the very place of God!"*

The very moment Lucifer thought this, a thin gray cloud drifted silently before his brow. It floated down toward his heart and left a dark stain upon his robe. Seeing the stain, Lucifer grew frightened and quickly spread his wing to hide it.

As he flew through heaven, he met Michael. Michael asked:

"Lucifer, what is the matter? Are you ill? Look—there is a stain upon your robe!"

"My heart aches just a little," Lucifer answered.

He flew hurriedly to the little angels and told them:

"Weave me a mantle as fiery red as flame. I need to cover something."

They did as he asked, and when Lucifer wrapped that cloak around himself, no one could see the stain any longer.

Lucifer stayed among the little angels and asked them:

"Would you like to help me build a golden throne? I will sit upon it and be your god. You are never allowed to approach the highest throne, but you may come to mine whenever you please."

Many angels were filled with dread when they heard this. Yet others loved Lucifer so much that they nodded in agreement. They stopped singing, stopped playing their instruments, and stopped fastening stars onto the Creator's mantle.

Michael witnessed what Lucifer was doing. Filled with dismay, the archangel went back to tell the Creator that Lucifer was building a throne for himself. The Creator spoke:

"Tell Lucifer to cast away his old heart. I will give him a new and radiant heart. If he is willing to do this, bring him to Me. If not, he shall have his throne—but not in heaven! If he refuses to listen to Me, take your sword and drive him out of heaven."

Thus spoke the Creator.

Michael carried every word of this message back to Lucifer. But Lucifer had already stirred up many angels to follow him; he wanted no new heart. All at once, the singing ceased. Voices shouted in wild confusion. Thunder rolled across all of heaven, and a fierce wind of fire burst forth.

Michael raised his sword high. From its blade flashed dazzling bolts of lightning. The archangel cried out in a mighty voice:

"All who are faithful to the Creator, stand on my side!"

Lucifer shouted back just as loudly:

"All who wish to enter a new heaven with me, stand on my side!"

So the spirits divided into two ranks. Michael's host stood above, and Lucifer's host stood below.

At once, Michael swung his lightning sword against the wall of heaven. Great thunderous blasts echoed everywhere, and a deep cleft opened wide. Lucifer and his followers fought back wildly, for they did not want to fall into the darkness below. But the Creator's light no longer shone upon them. All the glorious colors on their robes and wings grew pale and faded completely away. Their faces turned gloomy and ugly. Sharp claws sprouted from the tips of their fingers. They shrieked and groaned, yet in the end they had to give way before Michael and the angelic army. Lucifer and his followers were cast out of heaven and fell straight down into the pitch-dark abyss below.

From that time onward, there has been a dark world down below. Because it was freezing cold, the evil spirits gathered what little light they still possessed to kindle a tiny fire. They danced around it until the flame blazed up into a giant fire. They forged a throne for Lucifer and placed it right above the flames so he would always be kept warm. High above, Michael closed the cleft in heaven's wall once more, yet a scar remained etched there forever.

#### Storytelling Notes
- **An epic opening rhythm:** Read slowly and clearly through the opening lines to evoke the vastness of heaven and the music of flutes, lyres, and harps before the gray cloud appears.
- **Contrast between the two figures:** Keep Lucifer's voice somewhat low and guarded as he grows proud and tells his lie; make Michael's voice bright and decisive as he draws the lightning sword.
- **A quiet pause at the end:** Lower your voice into a deeper register when Lucifer's host falls into the cold abyss, and pause for 1–2 seconds at the detail of the scar on heaven's wall.

### Chapter 2: Michael and the Dragon

Down in the dark abyss, the evil spirits plotted together:

"Let us fashion a dragon and ride upon it to storm into heaven! With its strong, sharp teeth, it will tear the wall of heaven wide open!"

At the bottom of the dark abyss, they set to work creating a dragon. Day and night they hammered and polished thousands of razor-sharp scales for its body. Its tongue was like a licking flame, and its wings were like the wings of a bat. When it was finished, the dragon still had no spirit inside it.

Then one of the evil spirits shouted:

"I will be its spirit!"—and straightaway slipped down into the monster's belly.

The whole horde cheered, "Forward!" and the dragon shot upward, heading straight for the scar on heaven's wall. The evil spirits rode upon its back or clung close behind.

At that hour, a great multitude of angels were gathered around the Creator's throne, singing and making music. Suddenly there came a shrill, grating sound of scratching. Hissing and howling broke across the angels' song.

Michael saw the hideous dragon gnawing at the wall of heaven, carrying a whole swarm of evil spirits upon its back. With both hands, Michael gathered light from the Creator's throne and plunged his sword into that radiance.

By the time he reached the scar, one huge, sharp-clawed foot and the dragon's scaly, horned head had already poked inside. Lightning suddenly flashed from Michael's sword. Pinning the dragon firmly beneath his foot, Michael thrust his sword straight through it.

The evil spirits flapped their wings and scattered like a flock of bats. The dragon tumbled down into the abyss, coughed violently, and breathed its last. The spirit inside it crawled out, wailing miserably.

Michael called down into the abyss:

"O evil spirits! Stay quietly below! Who knows—perhaps one day the Creator will grant you a chance to make amends for what you have done."

The evil spirits growled and then fell silent, for the Creator had opened the gate of heaven. He spoke:

"Spirits of the abyss! Once you wished to establish your own kingdom! Now you have it, and below you must remain. Will you bow in obedience to Me?"

Through clenched teeth, they answered, "Yes." The gate of heaven closed. From then on, the realm above and the realm below were forever set apart.

#### Storytelling Notes
- **Rising tension:** Tell of the hammers forging the dragon's scales with brisk energy, then shift immediately to a startled tone when the scratching interrupts heaven's song.
- **The nobility of light:** Read slowly and solemnly when Michael scoops up light from the throne with both hands and dips his sword into it.
- **Echo of the promise:** Warm and soften your voice when Michael speaks of a future chance, then end crisply after the *"Yes"* spoken through clenched teeth.

### Chapter 3: The First Day of Creation

As the Creator sat upon His throne, He spoke seven words that rang out through all of heaven. At once, the seven colors of the rainbow appeared, shining in seven great rings around His throne. The angels gazed in awe at that radiance, both majestic and wondrously fair.

The Archangel Raphael spoke up and asked:

"Should we weave a mantle for the Creator out of these seven colors?"

The Archangel Gabriel answered:

"Perhaps a new great hall will be built of light!"

The Archangel Oriphiel—the angel of stillness—said softly:

"Those colors may be seven steps leading up to the Creator's throne."

The Archangel Michael only stood quietly silent, waiting for a sign from the Creator to show what was about to be done.

Then a wonderful miracle took place. Behind the rainbow, the majestic fire angels drew up a vast curtain of clouds, revealing a great hall no eye had ever beheld before. Within that hall lay tens of thousands of sleeping souls, countless as the stars in the sky.

And the Creator asked the angels:

"These are the sleeping souls of human beings. Will you join hands to build a Middle World—an earthly realm bridging Heaven and Earth—where they may awaken and live?"

All the angels joyfully answered, "Yes!"

The fire angels lowered the cloud-curtain and opened the gate of heaven. Outside it was freezing cold, pitch-dark, desolate, and empty. Then the Creator spoke, and His voice echoed across the night:

"Let there be Light!"

Light shone forth at once, blazing and sparkling in splendor. Darkness retreated into the abyss below. The fire angels drew flame from their own garments and sent it downward, warming the new world. The new earth bubbled, blazed, and flashed with light. Thunder rolled so mightily that the evil spirits in the abyss shrank back in terror. Looking down from on high, the angels' eyes shone like thousands of suns, glistening brightly in the radiant light of the first day of creation.

#### Storytelling Notes
- **Each archangel's tone:** Let Raphael sound eager, Gabriel bright and radiant, and Oriphiel calm and reflective, then pause briefly when speaking of Michael's silence.
- **The moment the cloud-curtain opens:** Soften your voice gently when introducing the tens of thousands of sleeping human souls.
- **The resounding command:** Speak *"Let there be Light!"* in a full, resonant voice, then carry the closing paragraph forward with lively, glowing momentum.

### Chapter 4: The Second Day

On the second day, the Creator spoke: "Let us make the air!"—for in the new world there was still no air at all.

The angels of the air gathered around the Creator. They began to blow winds and beat their mighty wings. Yet when the currents of air fanned the earth-fire already burning fiercely, the flames leaped flickering upward and flared with violent brightness. Huge firestorms erupted. They whirled round and round from above to below, howling wildly. Many angels were swept up into the spinning gusts and fell into great peril, needing help.

In that very instant, a great host of swift, weightless angels flew down and pressed everything heavy down below. Thanks to them, what lay underneath became like an ocean of fire, while above spread an ocean of air and light. Thus an upper realm and a lower realm took shape within the world.

Yet the human souls behind the curtain of clouds had no idea at all that a new world was being built for them. They still slept soundly on.

#### Storytelling Notes
- **Whirling storm rhythm:** Quicken your pace slightly as the firestorms spin from high to low so the children feel the tumult before the realms are separated.
- **A gentle ending:** Calm and soften your voice completely for the bright ocean of air above and the souls still sleeping peacefully behind the cloud-curtain.

### Chapter 5: The Third Day

In that world full of blowing winds and blazing fire, the wind swept huge, whirling firestorms high up toward the sky.

"Let us cool the world!" the angels said to one another.

With the Creator's help, they made water, and cool streams of water poured flooding into the world. From inside the flames, many voices called out:

"O water, we shall boil you up!"

Then everywhere rose a furious bubbling, a hissing roar, and a deafening sizzle.

The Creator spoke:

"Water must stay in a place of its own, and fire in its own place as well!"

The angels directed the water down into the deep hollows, while the light of the fire withdrew to the highest heights. Deep below, the waters gathered into seas. Beneath the ocean floor, a layer of solid earth began to form. That was the very first dry land, though it still lay hidden beneath the water.

And the Creator spoke: "Let green plants and herbs be made!"

At once the plant angels came and shaped the very first green shoots deep within the waters. Growing up from the dark, muddy seabed, the young sprouts yearned eagerly toward the light. Higher and higher they grew, until they reached the surface of the water.

One angel saw the green stems just emerging above the waves. That angel gathered light from heaven, wove it into a pure white crown, and set it atop a flower stalk. Those were the very first white lotuses and white water lilies.

Just as the angel was fashioning that wondrous blossom, a blazing fire angel swept past. Quick as lightning, the lotus angel spread its wings over the flower lest the fierce heat scorch it.

The fire angel called out:

"What are you hiding there? Nothing may be hidden here. That will not do!"

"I was afraid you might burn it up if I showed you," the plant angel answered softly.

"No, I would never burn anything so lovely! Oh!" the fire angel cried with delight. "What a beautiful crown of stars! Fire will do it no harm."

Before long, countless blossoms just like it opened in full bloom, adorning the whole surface of the sea.

The plant angel thought to itself: *"If only solid earth rose up above the water, surely a glorious earthly garden could be planted upon it."* So it flew all the way up to the Creator's throne.

Mighty angels stood guard around the throne, with lightning flashing brightly from their mantles. One of the guardians asked in a thunderous voice:

"What does a plant angel seek in this place?"

"I must ask the Creator a question. I do not know how to carry on my work down on the earth below," the plant angel replied.

At once they parted to let it pass. Reverently, the plant angel asked:

"O Creator, could You lift the earth up above the waters? We plant angels long to let herbs and all kinds of flowers grow beneath the open sky!"

The Creator spoke gently:

"Your thought agrees with My own will. So shall it come to be!"

With His right hand, He pressed deep down into the ocean floor. On both sides, high hills and great mountains rose upward, along with countless clear streams winding through the valleys. And by the Word of the Creator, the plant angels brought forth green herbs, myriad flowers, and trees across the whole face of the earth.

#### Storytelling Notes
- **Lighthearted, lively dialogue:** Use a shy yet loving voice for the lotus angel sheltering the flower with its wings, contrasting with the booming yet unexpectedly warm voice of the fire angel.
- **Majesty before the throne:** Deepen your voice for the thunderous question of the guardian angel, then soften immediately into gentle kindness as the Creator grants His blessing.

### Chapter 6: The Fourth Day

Down in the dark abyss, the fallen angels realized that something new was being built above them. Time and again, deep booms and rumbling thunder echoed down from the Middle World.

"What are they making up there?" they asked one another.

Lucifer sent several messengers flying upward, yet they could not come near that world blazing with light, because the radiance caused them pain. From afar, they saw only glimmers like star-flowers and countless tiny suns. The light flashed bright and then softened in a magnificent dance of colors.

The messengers reported all of this back to Lucifer. He said to himself:

"Who knows—perhaps later we too can lend a hand in building that world, and show what we are able to do."

In those early days, the brilliant light of the sun and the stars still lay hidden deep within the earth. Violent tempests and fiery whirlwinds raged continually. Plants and trees could not grow in peace.

Then the Creator spoke:

"O sources of light in the vault of heaven, shine forth!"

The great angels of light gathered all the fiery radiance scattered across the world and shaped it into one gigantic Sun. With a mighty heave, they lifted the Sun high up into the sky. The light gathered by the little angels was molded into thousands upon thousands of stars and raised far up into the heavens.

Afterward, with one majestic gesture, the Creator set the Sun in its circling course. With another gesture, He placed the stars along their rightful paths. Thus began their journey across the sky, and from that time onward there were day and night.

Because of this, the earth grew cooler. Plants and trees flourished in lush green, no longer hindered by scorching heat or whirling gales. Every creature fell into calm, harmonious order, anchoring its roots firmly in the steady ground. Ever since then, the flowers have always lifted their faces toward the Sun and the stars. They turn toward the shining light, knowing full well that the Sun is their loving mother, and the stars are their brothers and sisters. Every flower down on the earth has a star of its very own up in the sky.

Yet gradually the earth grew colder and colder. The soil became dry and hard. Many angels worried and said to one another:

"If things go on like this, soon no plants will be able to grow at all!"

Then the Creator spoke:

"Draw out of the earth everything that is hard, dense, and cold. I will shape one more heavenly light—and that shall be the Moon."

Obeying His Word, the angels used the strength He gave them to lift the Moon out of the earth and set it in the midst of the sky. This was the last of the great lights to be made. The Sun brings the earth light and warmth, pouring life into every living thing. The Moon, however, is a cold, lifeless world, causing the earth to grow ever firmer and more solid.

#### Storytelling Notes
- **Highlight the poetic bond of flowers and stars:** Read slowly and warmly through the passage *"the Sun is their loving mother... every flower down on the earth has a star of its very own up in the sky."*
- **Contrast Sun and Moon:** Keep your voice warm and brimming with vitality when speaking of the Sun, then let a cool, quiet stillness settle into the closing sentence about the Moon.

### Chapter 7: Edelweiss and the Mountain Flowers

At the close of the fourth day of creation, the Creator stood upon a heavenly cloud gazing down at the earth, with many angels standing close beside Him. Seeing the green trees and the vibrant blossoms, He praised the angels for helping weave such a splendid mantle for the earth.

The cloud drifted toward the high mountain ranges, yet there, lush meadows, blossoms, and tall trees could not flourish. On the high peaks lay only low shrubs, sparse herbs, and cold, bare cliffs. The young angels humbly asked:

"O Creator, may we go down to the earth once more to make flowers grow here too?"

He answered:

"Go then, and keep weaving the earth's mantle."

When the band of young angels reached the rocky mountains, the light of day was already fading. The sun was nearly setting.

"We can no longer make any green at all!" one angel cried out. "Without sunlight, it simply cannot be done."

Against the twilight sky, they caught sight of the gentle, silvery shimmer of the stars and said to one another:

"Let us make flowers out of starlight instead!"

Slender hands guided the starlight down onto the rocky ground. Leafy petals covered in soft silver down gently sprouted and arranged themselves into the shape of a star. In that very way, the edelweiss (the white star-flower of the rocky peaks) was born.

The next morning, when the pale blue sky brightened over the mountain range, the angels created the blue gentian—and to this very day, blue gentians still carry the color of the high sky. And out of the glowing crimson of dawn and sunset, they wove the richly colored alpine rhododendron.

#### Storytelling Notes
- **Twilight atmosphere in the high peaks:** Read lightly with a touch of wistfulness as daylight fades, then brighten eagerly when the angels discover the night starlight.
- **Rhythm in naming the three flowers:** Pronounce each name distinctly and unhurriedly—*edelweiss (the white star-flower of the rocky peaks)*, *blue gentian*, and *alpine rhododendron*—so children link each flower's hue to starlight, the open sky, and the glow of dawn and dusk.

### Chapter 8: The Story of Poisonous Plants

The Sun once gave all the flowers this gentle counsel:

"By day, open wide your blossoms and drink in my light. When night falls, fold your petals shut. The gentle Moon and the stars will keep watch over you. Yet on nights when thick clouds hide both moon and stars, and not a single ray of light shines down, never unfold your petals lest calamity strike. The spirits of darkness are always seeking a way to sow evil into the light of creation."

One pitch-black night, a dark shadow-spirit glided across the earth, prowling for a way to harm the plants. Coming beside a tiny woodland flower, it lowered its voice into a false, oily whisper:

"Poor little forest flower, how tiny you are! Open your petals so I may breathe into them. Then you will shoot up tall and bloom in grand splendor!"

The woodland flower kept its petals tightly closed and did not open.

Again the wicked spirit whispered:

"Open up, now! It is nighttime—no one can see. I will cover you completely with my own wings."

Slowly, the flower parted its petals just a crack. Instantly, the demon spat venomous poison straight into the heart of the blossom! Oh, what dreadful pain! The stem shuddered violently, and all its petals dropped raggedly to the ground as if scorched by fire. The evil spirit cackled with glee and vanished into the night.

The next morning, the angels of the morning dew flew down, bringing cool dew to bathe the meadows and woods. One of them came beside the woodland flower and asked in astonishment:

"Little forest flower, what has happened to you?"

"Oh, please pull me up by the roots! Please tear me out and throw me away!" the flower wept. "I failed to heed the Sun's warning and opened my blossom in the dark night. I am ruined, and I am burning with terrible pain!"

The angel stroked each leaf lovingly and spoke with tender kindness:

"I will not uproot you. Gather all that burning sap together into a berry. Keep the sap sealed safely inside it. Who knows—one day it may yet serve the world for good."

And so it came to pass. Today that woodland flower is called the deadly nightshade. The plant blooms with white petals. Its berries are dark purplish-black—very poisonous if eaten, yet able to be made into a precious medicine to heal sickness.

#### Storytelling Notes
- **Contrast the counsel and the temptation:** Use a warm, caring tone for the Sun's warning; in the midnight scene, drop into a sly, murmuring whisper as the wicked spirit tempts the flower.
- **Keep tenderness in the healing:** Do not linger grimly over the venom; instead pour gentle warmth into the dew angel's caressing hand upon the leaves and the hopeful counsel of the angel of the morning dew.

### Chapter 9: Why Roses Have Thorns

On another night, the evil spirit crept stealthily past a rosebush where a lovely, deep crimson velvet rose was blooming. When evening fell, the rose had folded its petals shut, yet its rich fragrance still drifted sweetly through the late night.

That perfume troubled the fallen evil spirit so greatly that he sneezed violently several times in a row. Grumpily he cursed beneath his breath:

"This blossom smells unbearable; I just want to shred it to pieces with my claws!"

Then he held his breath, crept close beside the flower, and whispered coaxingly:

"O sweet rose, open your petals! I will breathe upon you, and you shall have an even sweeter fragrance!"

The rose remained silent and kept its petals tightly closed.

Again the wicked demon hissed softly:

"Come now, rose, open your petals! I will blow upon you so you grow into a mighty, towering tree. Otherwise, your whole life long you will remain nothing more than a tiny bush!"

Yet the rose kept its petals folded firmly shut.

Suddenly, the evil spirit flew into a rage and slashed fiercely with his sharp claws along the stems and branches of the rosebush. He shook the branches furiously. Only when he saw he could do no further harm did he stalk off in a sulk and vanish.

The rose endured dreadful pain along its stems where the demon's claws had torn them.

Daylight returned. The wounds gradually scarred over, and out of those very scars sprouted sharp thorns. An angel of light walked past, smiled, and said to the rose:

"Rejoice in your sharp thorns! They are a keepsake reminding you of your steadfastness!"

Ever since that time, roses have always had thorns.

#### Storytelling Notes
- **A touch of gentle humor at the start:** Give a light, playful accent when the rose's fragrance makes the evil spirit sneeze over and over before losing his temper.
- **Emphasize steadfast courage:** Keep your voice calm and steady both times you describe the rose silently holding its petals tightly shut.
- **A proud, bright ending:** Read the angel of light's words affectionately, then pause for a beat before delivering the crisp closing line.

### Chapter 10: The Fifth Day

The Creator looked down upon the earth and spoke:

"It is too quiet upon the earth. Let us make the animals!"

He called down to the seas:

"O fish in the waters, receive life!"

In the deep ocean, life began to stir. First, the angels shaped the creatures with shells on the ocean floor: they fitted two shells together and placed a living being between them. Those were the clams and oysters, sleeping endlessly down upon the sea bottom.

The angels kept shaping and forming. They crafted creatures with small fins and gleaming silver scales—these were the fish. Fish have no eyelids, so to this very day they sleep with their eyes open. Then larger and larger sea creatures appeared one after another: sharks, whales, and dolphins. Many leaped high into the air and plunged with a splash back into the waves. Glittering goldfish, rainbow trout shimmering in every hue of the rainbow, and countless wondrously scaled fish such as silver-gleaming carp, perch, and gobies glided sparkling through the clear waters.

Next, the Creator called into the open air:

"O birds, spread your wings and soar, rejoicing across the sky!"

Then the angels fashioned the *"fish of the air."* Their bodies were smaller, yet their wings were broader and longer than those of the fish below. Instead of scales, they wore soft, brightly colored feathers. The schools of fish beneath the waves gazed up at the birds and cried out, *"We want to know how to fly too!"*—and leaped high out of the water.

Up above, certain birds looked down at the fish and declared, *"We know how to swim too!"*—and settled gracefully onto the water. Those were the geese, wild ducks, and swans, along with the white storks wading along the shallows. Swallows built their nests on cliffs and in trees, while titmice and little warblers wove their nests among the bushes.

A little sparrow perched at the water's edge boasted to the fish:

"We are quicker than you and work far harder! We build nests, lay eggs, and sit upon them to keep them warm. Then *cheep-cheep*, out hatch the baby birds!"

Yet the fish were quite content to be fish. One little fish answered the sparrow as it stooped to sip the water:

"Water holds us up far better than air. You must beat your wings nonstop or else you will fall! As for us fish, we only have to tighten our bellies a little—where our swim bladder rests—and down we sink; when we relax, up we float again!"

In the bright morning sunshine, as birds such as swallows, larks, and song-thrushes soared high into the sky, they heard the Lord's angels singing and making music. They tried to sing along with the heavenly melody and rejoice just like the angels. That is how birds learned their song. Later, many kinds forgot most of that ancient music, keeping only a few notes like *cheep-cheep* or *too-weet, too-weet*. As for those shy of flying up into the sunlight, they utter only the *caw-caw* of the crow or the *whoo-whoo* of the night owl.

And thus the fifth day passed.

#### Storytelling Notes
- **Rising from sea to sky:** Read softly and low for the clams and fish in the ocean depths, then lift and brighten your voice when the Creator calls the birds to spread their wings.
- **Banter between sparrow and fish:** Create a lively contrast between the brisk, chatterbox sparrow and the calm, unhurried fish.
- **Birdsong at the close:** Let the clear sounds *cheep-cheep* and *too-weet* ring lightly in contrast to *caw-caw* and *whoo-whoo*.

### Chapter 11: The Sixth Day

The next morning, when the angels of the morning dew came down to the meadows, they asked the flowers why they looked so sad.

The flowers answered:

"The birds pay us no heed at all. They only swoop right past and never stop to visit. We are rooted in the earth, and can only sway gently in the breeze."

The dew angels carried word back to the Heavenly Father. He gave them a cloud of many colors and instructed them: *"Carry it down to the earth and draw back the curtain of the cloud."* The angels brought the cloud down into the world. High in the open air, they pulled the curtain aside, and instantly thousands of butterflies fluttered out on bright wings.

Seeing this, the flowers cried out joyfully to one another:

"Look—children of the angels! Behold our little brothers and sisters come down from heaven! Their wings are just as lovely as flower petals!"

The blossoms seemed to beckon them close. The butterflies fluttered up to the flowers, stroked them gently, and asked for a drop of nectar. Gladly the flowers offered their sweet nectar, while the butterflies told them tales of all the wonders of heaven.

Yet deep in the forests and hidden among thick grasses, many flowers still grew where no butterflies could reach. They poured out their loneliness to the angels of the morning dew:

"The butterflies never find their way to us. We feel forgotten and all alone."

So the Heavenly Father sent down another heavenly cloud, glowing a warm brown. When the curtain of that cloud was drawn open, swarms of honeybees went humming and buzzing out across the whole earth. Each queen bee flew to alight upon a branch and whistled ever so softly. Though the queen's whistle was faint and gentle, every swarm knew its own queen and gathered closely around her. They sought out cozy homes in hollow trees or cavities in the earth and built their hives there. From that day on, even the tiniest, most hidden flower had friends to visit.

Once, a honeybee and a butterfly chanced to meet upon the same blossom. Full of pity, the butterfly said to the bee:

"Did our Heavenly Father have no colors left over for you? You are as dull brown as wood, and your wings are pitifully small. How can you possibly fly with wings like those?"

The honeybee smiled and replied:

"Do you see that tiny forget-me-not over there in the thick grass? Fly to it and come back here—I will wait for you."

Oh dear! As soon as the butterfly swooped down, its broad wings got tangled fast in the dense grass, and the bee had to fly in and help free it. Once it was out, the bee said again:

"Now let us race to that old linden tree bursting with fragrant blossoms over yonder!"

By the time the butterfly reached the crown of the linden tree, the honeybee had already arrived long before and flown off again. Laughing gently, the bee told it:

"My dear friend butterfly, your colors are lovely and your wings are splendid indeed. Yet now you see there is no need to pity me, do you not? Our Heavenly Father has arranged all things most wisely."

The butterfly felt quite ashamed for having boasted of its brightly painted wings.

And what of Sister Bumblebee in her thick, glossy black fur coat? She can fly even in chilly weather; whenever she bumps against a branch or twig, that plush coat cushions and shields her without a scratch.

Then the Heavenly Father spoke to the earth:

"O earth, let your lands come alive with every kind of creature!"

Together with the angels, He created all the animals upon the earth. At first the meadow frog lived in the water like a fish; given legs and losing its tail, it hopped onto land and croaked *croak-croak*. The creature with a slender body and four nimble legs was the lizard. The one carrying a protective shell upon its back was the turtle. The one hauling a tiny house upon its back without a single foot was the snail. Those living in grassy hills, burrowing tunnels underground and sitting upright, were the moles and field mice.

The mountain said, *"I want creatures upon my cliffs."* So chamois and mountain goats were created to dwell there. The deep forest said, *"I want to be a roof and home for many animals; within me they can dig burrows and build nests."* So there were squirrels, deer, hares, and foxes.

And who would graze all the lush green carpets of grass? Herds of cows, flocks of sheep, troops of horses, and goats. Strong bulls locked horns to test their might; horses with fiery eyes galloped swiftly across the plains, neighing boldly with manes streaming in the wind. And so every creature came to dwell across the earth.

Before creating the animals, the Heavenly Father already held within His mind the image of the human being. With every new creature they fashioned, the angels drew one step closer to that image. That is why many animals also have five fingers or five claws. Some creatures, like squirrels, bears, apes, and field mice, strive to stand upright like human beings. Truly, every animal carries some likeness to humankind.

#### Storytelling Notes
- **Poetic warmth at the beginning:** Read lightly and vividly when telling of the flowers' sadness and the thousands of butterflies fluttering out from the cloud-curtain.
- **Playful dialogue:** Bring out the butterfly's patronizing boastfulness in contrast to the honeybee's quiet poise as they fly to the *tiny forget-me-not* and the *old linden tree bursting with fragrant blossoms*.
- **A thoughtful hush at the end:** Slow your pace for the closing lines about the image of the human being held within the Heavenly Father's mind.

### Chapter 12: The Dove and the Little Lamb

One day, an evil spirit saw a snow-white dove perched upon a tree branch. That pure white plumage irritated him terribly. Putting on a honeyed voice, he tried to coax her:

"My lovely little dove, come fly down here to me!"

The dove tilted her head and answered:

*"Coo-coo, coo-coo,*
*I do not care for you!"*

Again the evil spirit called out:

"Come here, my dove, and I will paint your feathers with dazzling colors so you do not have to wear that boring white coat forever!"

He crept stealthily right up to the foot of the tree and was just about to climb up when the dove spread her white wings wide and soared away. Furious, he shook the tree where she had perched with all his might, yet he could not harm her at all.

Evening fell, the sun sank behind the mountains, and nearly all the animals were fast asleep. Only one mischievous little mouse was still scurrying about in the shadows. The moment the evil spirit spied it, he snatched it up and said:

"Now I too will make a bird of my own!"

He stretched the mouse's ears and four legs out long. Then he tore a flap of skin from his own body, fastened it onto the mouse's back, and stretched both its legs and tail into that skin. All night long, the mouse fluttered fearfully through the darkness. When dawn broke, it crept into the darkest corner of a hollow tree to hide. Too ashamed to fly out into the sunlight while the other birds sang, it became a creature of the night—and so the bat lives to this very day.

Another time, the evil spirit saw a little lamb out in the meadow. He tried to sidle up and lure it close, but the lamb backed warily away. So the evil spirit put on his softest voice and whispered:

"If you let me comb your fleece with this golden comb, every lock of your wool will turn to pure gold!"

Yet the little lamb leaped away and vanished in a flash.

Spiteful and angry, the evil spirit slunk into the deep forest. There he met a dog sniffing around for food. Pulling out a piece of devilish poisoned bait, he tossed it to the dog. Gulping the bait down greedily, the dog threw back its head and let out a piercing howl. Inside its belly, fire burned so fiercely that the hair along its neck bristled straight up. That creature turned into a wolf. The evil spirit drove the wolf bursting out of the woods straight toward the meadow among the flock of sheep. The fierce wolf charged into the flock and seized the very little lamb that had escaped the evil spirit's grasp. From that day onward, only blood can briefly soothe the burning torment inside a wolf's belly.

#### Storytelling Notes
- **Contrast good and evil:** Keep the evil spirit's voice low, slippery, and furtive; speak the dove's two-line rhyme (*"Coo-coo, coo-coo..."*) in a bright, crisp, decisive voice.
- **Gentle emotional pacing:** Tell of the mouse becoming a bat and the dog turning into a wolf in a quiet, compassionate tone, without trying to frighten the children.

---

### Chapter 13: The Creation of Human Beings

The Heavenly Father looked down across the whole earth and spoke to the angels:

"Let us create the human being!"

The angels flew forth and gathered a little of the finest essence from everything on earth. Hard stone was shaped into bones; flowing rivers became blood; the stars were formed into eyes; warm earth built the body; and the gentle wind gave breath. God rounded the head like the moon, and curved the arms, legs, and fingers radiating outward like rays of light. Just as the sun in heaven warms and nourishes every living thing, He placed a warm heart inside the human chest to sustain him with life.

When the human body was complete, a great archangel stepped forth from behind the cloud-curtain, bearing a sleeping soul, and laid it gently into God's hands. Then the Heavenly Father breathed His own living breath into the human being.

The angels in heaven flocked to behold this wondrous miracle: a human being had been created! His name was Adam—which means *"child of the earth."* In those days, Adam's body was not yet heavy and dense like ours today. He was light as an angel, walking softly over the new earth wrapped in a gentle cloud of light. In the evening when Adam slept, his guardian angel carried his soul back up to heaven. Therefore the human being knew no evil, never fell sick, never grew old, and did not yet know death.

As Adam walked across the earth, his heart overflowed with joy. Full of trust, all the animals came forward to greet him, and Adam gave each one its name: the eagle flew down with all the birds of the air; the lion walked beside the leopard; zebra, giraffe, rhinoceros, and camel came one after another, along with the bull, the horse, and the stag. Bringing up the rear came the tiniest creatures such as beetles and ants—and of course, last of all came the little snail. It had hurried with all its might, yet still ended up arriving at the very end.

And the Heavenly Father gave the human being a lovely garden. That was the Garden of Paradise. The angels wove a hedge of light all around the garden so that Lucifer and his followers could never slip inside to disturb the peaceful life within.

#### Storytelling Notes
- **A sacred atmosphere:** Read slowly and reverently where heaven and earth are woven together (stone to bones, rivers to blood, stars to eyes...) and at the moment the breath of life is breathed in.
- **Lighthearted warmth:** Let a gentle smile come into your voice at the picture of the little snail hurrying as fast as it can yet still coming in dead last.

---

### Chapter 14: The Seventh Day

When every work had been created and finished, the Heavenly Father looked upon all that He had made and saw that it was good. He entrusted the keeping of that new world to the ranks of the angels. The Elohim angels governed the sun and its radiant sunlight, along with the moon and all the countless stars. The Cherubim held rule over rolling thunder and flashes of lightning. Stone, water, air, and fire—every element had its own guardian ruler; plants and every living creature were likewise placed in the care of heavenly beings.

That was the wondrous divine order the Heavenly Father established for the Middle World. Yet time and again, the evil spirits from the dark world below still crept upward, trying to pull down God's Kingdom or spoil that good creation. That was the very origin of evil upon the earth.

To shield the human being from evil, the Heavenly Father gave Adam the peaceful Garden of Paradise. Humankind did not have to know the evil lurking out in the wider world. Adam lived an everlasting life, untouched by sorrow, sickness, or grief. Without ceasing, the angels glided softly down to earth and soared back up to high heaven, bringing divine light to the human being.

#### Storytelling Notes
- **A solemn, tranquil pace:** Even though this chapter is brief, keep an unhurried rhythm like the closing chord of the seven days of creation so children feel the harmony of the cosmos.
- **A quiet shift in tone:** Lower your voice gently when mentioning the evil spirits from below, then lift it into warm radiance on the closing line about the angels bringing divine light.

---

### Chapter 15: In the Garden of Paradise

Once, standing beside the water's edge watching the fish swim gracefully past, Adam caught sight of his own reflection shimmering beneath the surface. He thought to himself:

*"Oh, if only I had a companion so I would not be all alone like this!"*

That night, when his angel bore his soul up to heaven, the Heavenly Father read the longing inside Adam's heart and created a companion for him.

Hand in hand like brother and sister, the two walked together through the Garden of Paradise. Adam showed her all the wonders of creation. Beholding the exquisite beauty of every living thing, she gasped in wonder, *"E!"* and *"Ah!"* So Adam named her Eva—and fondly called her Eve.

The Heavenly Father pointed out one tree in the midst of the garden and warned them:

"You may eat the fruit of every tree in the garden, except this one tree alone. This is the Tree of Knowledge. Its fruit you must not eat."

In those days, human beings ate only fruit. There was no winter at all; the air remained forever mild and warm. Trees bore blossoms and ripe fruit at the very same time. All the animals were trusting and wonderfully gentle. Adam and Eve stroked them, and the creatures came right up to eat from their hands. Birds pecked at scattered seeds and circled playfully above their heads. Joy and peace filled the entire garden so completely that Adam and Eve never even glanced at the forbidden tree.

The angels had woven a hedge of rays of light all around the garden so Lucifer could never slip inside; each day they examined it closely to make sure there was no opening anywhere. Once, while strolling through the garden, Adam and Eve drew near the hedge of light and heard a strange sound. At that very instant, an evil spirit was struggling furiously to squeeze through the bright hedge, though in vain. Yet he caught sight of the two human beings inside and hurried back at once to tell Lucifer. From that moment onward, Lucifer plotted day and night to find a way to break into the Garden of Paradise.

Inside the garden, meanwhile, whenever Adam and Eve ate the fruit of the Tree of Life, they felt refreshed and renewed and faintly heard the music of heaven. Sometimes they climbed onto the backs of horses or deer and rode out to the Four Rivers. They dipped their feet into the cool water while fish swam round them, brushing fondly against their ankles or leaping into the air in gleaming silver arcs. Bright butterflies alighted lightly on their hands, their shoulders, and even upon their heads, looking as lovely as garlands of flowers crowned upon their hair.

#### Storytelling Notes
- **Innocent poetry:** Keep your telling light and joyful when she exclaims *"E!"* and *"Ah!"* and when butterflies perch upon their hair like flower wreaths beside the Four Rivers.
- **A subtle warning pause:** Lower your voice slightly when the two hear the strange sound beside the hedge of light, planting a gentle sense of suspense before the turning point in the next chapter.

### Chapter 16: The Fall

Just as serpents today love to live among hedges and thickets, in the beginning the serpent loved gliding along the hedge of light surrounding the Garden of Paradise.

One day by chance, Lucifer spied the serpent stretched out beside the hedge. He crept closer and whispered:

"Serpent! O serpent! Come over here to me! I have something to tell you! Look at the splendid wings of those birds up there. What have you been given? The Creator has not been fair at all! Look at the deer with its long legs made for bounding—must you go on crawling flat in the dust forever? Come to me, and I will change your lot into something far better!"

At first the serpent hesitated, yet soon it wriggled through a narrow crack. Lucifer immediately said:

"Open your mouth wide—I will slip inside your body and transform you!"

Lucifer slid straight inside the serpent, and instantly the serpent's skin shimmered in every dazzling color! Softly Lucifer urged:

"Glide into the Garden of Paradise! Inside there, I will make you even more wondrous still."

He steered the serpent straight into the heart of the garden, aimed right for the forbidden Tree of Knowledge, and whispered: *"Climb up the tree!"* So the serpent wound itself around the wooden trunk and slithered high into the leafy branches to hide.

Before long, Adam and Eve came walking past. They sat down to rest beneath the Tree of Knowledge, listening to the angels' music drifting down from high heaven. Suddenly a strange voice called out: *"Eve, O Eve!"* Startled and a little frightened, Eve stood up to see who was calling her name. The voice went on:

"Eve! Look at this lovely apple! Pick it! One apple from this tree tastes sweeter than the fruit of any other tree. If you eat this fruit, you will know what Good and Evil are. You will become just like the Creator!"

Frightened, Eve wanted to step back, yet the serpent kept talking on and on, coaxing and pleading relentlessly:

"Take it, take it now! Nobody is looking!"

Eve thought to herself: *"Surely I could try just one tiny bite."* She reached up, plucked the apple, took a bite, and handed it to Adam.

Alas! The bright cloud that had always wrapped around them dissolved at once and fell away. Their steps suddenly grew so heavy they could scarcely lift their feet. Birds flew up into the sky in alarm. A fierce wind sprang up, roaring wildly, and all the animals scattered in panic and bewilderment. Trembling with fear, Adam and Eve hurried to hide behind the trees.

Thunder boomed aloud, and the lightning of the Cherubim flashed across the sky. The voice of the Heavenly Father echoed clearly:

"Adam, where are you?"

"I am here, behind the trees."

"Why are you hiding?"

"O Heavenly Father, I am ashamed, because I am naked."

Then Adam and Eve had to step forth, and the Heavenly Father spoke in a voice of solemn majesty:

"Because you have eaten the fruit of the Tree of Knowledge, you must leave this heavenly garden. You must go down onto the earth. There you shall have to toil hard by the sweat of your brow, and you shall come to know sickness and death."

Scarcely had He finished speaking when a majestic angel of fire appeared. With a flaming sword, the angel led Adam and Eve out of the Garden of Paradise. All the animals cried sorrowfully and walked heavily behind to bid them farewell. The wind moaned sadly through the treetops; the flowers drooped their blossoms, and the angels wept.

The Archangel Michael and the faithful angels looked up and pleaded with the Heavenly Father:

"May we still be their guardian angels down upon the earth?"

The Heavenly Father granted their prayer—otherwise humankind would have been lost forever. And the Cherubim locked the gate of the Garden of Paradise with their flaming swords and stood guard before it from that day onward.

#### Storytelling Notes
- **Whispering temptation:** Lower your volume and draw out the rhythm slightly as Lucifer tempts the serpent and the serpent calls *"Eve, O Eve!"* to convey stealth and enchantment.
- **Sudden upheaval:** Quicken your pace right after *"Alas!"* as the wind rises and thunder rolls, then drop completely into quiet stillness during the brief exchange between Adam and the Heavenly Father.
- **Closing in love:** Read slowly and warmly where the animals bid farewell and Michael offers his faithful plea.

### Chapter 17: On the Earth

Michael walked beside Adam and Eve, guiding them down onto the earth. When evening came, the air grew cold. Shivering in the chill, Adam and Eve gathered branches and broad leaves to build a small hut for shelter, and braided broad leaves together to wear as clothing. They went out to pick berries for food. While gathering wild strawberries, Eve suddenly let out a piercing scream: a snake had startled and terrified her! All night long, wolves howled in the distance; bright-eyed night owls hooted again and again, and leather-winged bats circled squeaking *squeak-squeak* around the hut. Terrified, Adam and Eve did not dare step a foot outside.

The next morning—their second day upon the earth—Adam stepped onto a sharp thorn, drawing the very first drops of human blood. He found a strip of tree bark and tied it firmly around his foot for protection. The ground inside the hut was rock-hard and miserable for sleeping, so Adam and Eve gathered soft moss off the stones to make the very first bed.

Adam discovered a tree laden with lovely ripe red apples. He thought to himself: *"Perhaps Eve will cheer up when she has these!"* When he handed his wife the first earthly apples, Eve felt a sudden ache of longing for the lost Garden of Paradise and burst into tears—and those were the very first tears ever shed upon the earth. Those apples were nowhere near as sweet as the fruit of Paradise; once they had eaten them, Adam and Eve could no longer hear the music of heaven or the voices of the angels.

One evening, Michael appeared to comfort Adam and Eve:

"You have not lost heaven completely. Pray to God. When you pray, the thread of light connecting your souls to high heaven will never break. Night after night, that very thread of light will draw you gently back toward the light of heaven."

From that time onward, human beings learned to lift their voices in prayer toward the Creator. And the angel taught Adam how to offer sacrifice. Through the warm flame and the rising smoke drifting high into the sky, Adam prayed that God's Light would shine forever into the human heart.

#### Storytelling Notes
- **First bewilderment on earth:** Read slowly and with a faint shiver when describing the chill, the little hut of *branches and broad leaves*, and the wolves howling and owls hooting in the night.
- **Two moving milestones:** Gently underscore *the very first drops of human blood* upon the sharp thorn and Eve's *very first tears* beside the earthly apples.
- **A closing ray of hope:** Let Michael's words about the *"thread of light"* sound tender and reassuring, like a comforting lullaby closing the story.

## Part II: The Descendants of Cain

### Chapter 18: Cain and Abel

When Adam and Eve had lived upon the earth for some time, Eve gave birth to a son. His name was Cain. Eve said to Adam:

"Look at this child. The Lord has given him to me and placed him in my care. I will be a good mother to him."

Adam lifted the little boy in his arms and said:

"Look, Eve! Heaven's fire is blazing in his eyes! His soul passed through the fiery gates of the Garden of Paradise before he was born."

Cain had a strong and fiery spirit. He grew tall and sturdy. Even the wild beasts fled before him. When lightning flashed and thunder rolled, Cain shouted for joy. He wanted to catch the bolts of lightning! With every step he took, the earth trembled beneath his feet.

After a time, Eve bore another son and named him Abel. He was altogether different from Cain. Abel was gentle and delicate. Eve said to Adam:

"Look, Adam. All the radiance of heaven shines in his eyes. Surely his soul passed among the stars before he came into the world."

Abel had a mild and quiet heart. His body grew slender and small. Abel made friends with the sheep in the meadows. He played with them and became a shepherd.

One day, Cain brought home a wooden stick with a flat stone wedged into it. He said:

"Father, I have discovered something! I can turn the earth over with this. I shall call it a hoe."

He dug small holes in the soil and planted young saplings inside them. Cain loved turning the earth with his new tool. Whenever he came across a plant that pleased him, he pulled it up roots and all and planted it out in the field. With his hoe, he drew furrows across the earth and sowed seeds in them.

Abel, on the other hand, loved the flowers. He wove blossoms into garlands to wear upon his head. He moistened a finger and held it out so trusting butterflies could alight upon it. The birds were his friends.

Once, the stone in Cain's hoe broke. He waded down into the streambed to search for another stone. He struck stones against one another until they split apart. Choosing the sharpest piece, he wedged it into the handle of his hoe. Seeing him, Abel said:

"Dear brother Cain, you are covered all over in dust and dirt."

Hearing that, Cain leaped straight into the swift-flowing stream and swam across to the far bank! Cain had never learned to swim; he knew how to swim from the very beginning.

Cain helped Abel build a pen for the flock of sheep. He drove wooden stakes deep into the ground while Abel deftly wove pliable willow branches in and out between them, creating a fence of woven willow branches. When night fell, Abel guided the sheep into the pen so that they would be safe from wild beasts.

#### Storytelling Notes
- **Create a clear contrast between the two brothers:** Keep your voice strong and decisive when describing Cain chasing lightning or swimming across the stream; soften into a lighter, brighter tone for Abel.
- **Highlight the joy of discovery:** Read with eager excitement when Cain shows off his stone hoe, and with gentle serenity when Abel weaves flower garlands and holds out his finger for butterflies.

---

### Chapter 19: What Cain and Abel Dreamed

Each morning when they woke from sleep, Cain and Abel told each other their dreams.

Cain said:

"I walked through rocks and crevices, deep down, deeper and deeper into the heart of the earth, all the way to the fire inside the earth. At that fire, I lit a torch and carried it up to the surface. With this fire, I light up the darkness of the black night."

Abel said:

"I walked with the angels in the Garden of Paradise and searched for the golden apples, yet I found not a single one. In the middle of the garden, I saw a cloud. The cloud rose high above the garden and shone like the morning sunlight. Behind that cloud stood the tree that bears golden apples!"

#### Storytelling Notes
- **A slow, prophetic cadence:** Pause gently for one or two seconds between the two dreams so the children can picture Cain's descent into the depths of the earth and Abel's gaze rising toward heaven.
- **Contrasting tones:** Give Cain a deep, steady voice; let Abel's voice sound light and brighten at the final sentence.

---

### Chapter 20: Cain Conquers the Wolf

One morning, Abel went out to check on his flock. He saw blood on the ground near the pen and tufts of wool scattered across the earth. There was a wide hole where the fence of woven willow branches had been torn open. During the night, a wolf had come and killed one of the sheep.

Weeping bitterly, Abel showed Cain what had happened. Flames of anger blazed in Cain's eyes. He uttered a fierce threat against the wolf:

"Just you wait, wretched beast! I will not rest until I find you and punish you."

Abel crept into the pen where the trembling sheep huddled in a corner. He stroked and comforted them. Cain pulled up a wooden stake and ran straight into the forest. He pushed through the thickets and listened intently.

Suddenly he heard a sound coming from behind some tall boulders. With watchful eyes, he crept closer on tiptoe. Carefully peering over the edge of the rock, he saw a cave. Before the cave, a mother bear was playing with her cubs.

"You are far too brown," he muttered, and crawled onward.

A woodpecker was pecking *tap-tap-tap* against a tree trunk.

"How well it drills into wood," Cain hissed through clenched teeth. "Yet I will strike a blow harder still than that."

Night began to fall. Cain still had not found the wolf. He sat down to rest beneath a tree, but weariness washed over him, and before long he fell fast asleep.

A little while later, a dark shadow glided past. It was the wolf. The beast lifted its snout, sniffed the air, and crept silently closer to Cain. By chance, the wolf brushed its tail against some dry leaves. Cain sprang awake to meet a pair of glowing, hateful eyes. Leaping to his feet, he hurled his wooden stake, yet missed. The wolf escaped. Annoyed and furious, Cain returned home.

He decided to spend the night right inside the sheepfold, since the fence had not yet been mended. He said to Abel:

"If the wolf comes, I will strangle it!"

The sheep paced restlessly for a long while and did not lie down until well past midnight. The moon rose, yet Cain did not sleep. An owl hooted in the forest, yet Cain did not sleep. He stared through the woven willow fence into the night so that no dark shadow could escape his eyes.

What was that? Something was slinking stealthily along the edge of the forest. Now and then it stopped, crouched low to the ground, and crept closer. Cain did not sleep. The wolf prowled right up to the pen. At last it poked its greedy head through the gap in the fence. Cain seized it and held it fast. The sheep bleated in terror, yet Cain tightened his grip and strangled the wolf to death.

When morning came and Abel awoke, he hurried to the sheepfold. Cain was not there, and thankfully not a single sheep had been lost. Then he saw Cain stepping out of the forest. From afar, Cain called out loudly:

"I threw the wolf down into a ravine in the woods. Your sheep are safe now."

Abel mended the willow fence. That day, he felt almost afraid of his older brother, who had killed a wolf with his bare hands and flung its body into a ravine.

#### Storytelling Notes
- **Suspenseful refrain:** Drop your voice to a whisper and mark a clear pause at each of the three repetitions of *"yet Cain did not sleep"* before the wolf thrusts its head through the fence.
- **Quiet pause at the close:** Let your voice fall softly with unspoken awe in the final sentence as Abel looks upon his brother's terrifying strength.

---

### Chapter 21: When Eve Wept

One afternoon, Adam and Eve sat beside their hut, waiting for their two sons to return. Cain came walking across the field, his arms cradling bright golden apples he had just picked. He placed the apples in Eve's lap. Eve lifted one apple. The moment she looked upon it, tears welled in her eyes and rolled down her cheeks.

Startled, Cain asked:

"Mother, why are you weeping?"

She did not answer. Then Abel stepped close and asked gently in sorrow:

"Mother, tell us—why do these lovely apples make you weep?"

Speaking in a soft, tender voice, Eve began to tell the story:

"Apples like these, yet far more wondrously beautiful, once grew upon the trees in the Garden of Paradise. Your father and I lived there like the angels. We were allowed to eat every fruit in the garden. Only the tree standing in the middle of the garden were we forbidden to touch. Lucifer, that wicked spirit, tempted us to pick and eat an apple from that tree. He told us, 'You shall be like God.' We let him deceive us, and because of that we lost the Garden of Paradise and all its heavenly fruit!"

Cain asked:

"Where is the Garden of Paradise? I will find it and smash open its gates!"

Eve answered:

"No one can ever find that place, unless the Lord Himself calls them."

Abel asked:

"How may we please the Lord?"

Eve replied:

"By praying to Him and offering sacrifice. Fire and smoke will carry your prayers up to the Father in Heaven. That is what the angel of the Lord taught us."

Cain cried out eagerly:

"When I broke stones apart, I saw sparks fly out! I will try to kindle a fire. I will help everyone build the fire for offering!"

And so Cain kindled the very first fire. Abel brought dry little twigs to feed the flame. Yet Cain threw huge tree trunks onto the blaze until the fire roared furiously and set the nearby forest ablaze. Only then did Adam teach him how to pile up a stone altar so that the fire would burn more gently.

#### Storytelling Notes
- **Eve's longing memory:** Read slowly and warmly as Eve speaks of the fruits of the Garden of Paradise, contrasting with Cain's fierce eagerness to smash open the gates.
- **Rhythm of the fire:** Quicken your pace as the flames leap out of control toward the woods, then settle into a calm, measured voice when Adam teaches them to build a stone altar.

---

### Chapter 22: How Cain Slew Abel

One day, Abel said to Cain:

"Brother, let us offer sacrifice upon an altar just as Father Adam taught us."

Cain answered:

"You offer your sacrifice, and I will offer mine."

Cain built an altar out of massive boulders and piled a great heap of firewood upon it. Abel's altar was small, with only a little wood, for he was not strong like Cain.

"What should I offer as a sacrifice?" Abel asked his father.

"Offer whatever you love most," Adam answered.

That answer filled Abel's heart with dread. What he loved most was a spotless, snow-white lamb! Deep within his heart, he heard a voice whisper:

"Do not offer that one. Take a spotted sheep instead. Any sheep offered to the Lord will do."

Abel shuddered in fright. Was that not the voice of the evil spirit? He went into the pen, lifted his dearest little lamb in his arms, and brought it as his offering. Cain carried fruits from the field, placed them upon his altar, and lit the fire. Abel asked for a few glowing embers from Cain's altar to start his own fire. Filled with reverence, Abel knelt down and prayed from the depths of his soul:

> *"I thank You, Father in heaven high,*
> *Who created this beautiful world.*
> *I thank You for the bright sun*
> *That nourishes the grass, the trees, and the blossoms,*
> *And warms both wild beasts and humankind.*
> *I thank You for the cool water, the fresh air, and Mother Earth*
> *That faithfully sustain our bodies.*
> *Thank You for the spirit of life*
> *That You have breathed into me."*

The smoke from Abel's offering rose straight into heaven like a flower, while Cain's fire burned wildly. The flames whipped sideways in confusion. Thick smoke curled low along the ground and billowed into his eyes, making them sting.

Cain looked at Abel's offering and saw the smoke rising straight up high. Cain thought the Lord had rejected his gift. He felt as though a raging fire were burning inside his soul. A fierce heat surged straight to his head. Blinded with fury, he struck his younger brother a heavy blow. Abel collapsed to the ground, his final words still a prayer. He was dead.

Cain lowered his hand. Gazing in horror at the red blood flowing from Abel's head and soaking into the earth, he fell to his knees and wept bitterly:

"Abel, get up! Abel, my dear brother, wake up!"

Yet Abel did not move. His eyes, bright as stars, were closed forever.

Then Cain felt a piercing anguish so deep that he howled like a wild beast. Terror drove him headlong through the forest. He ran wildly through thorns, thickets, boulders, and deep ravines. A storm tore through the forest, and lightning flashed bright. Deep inside a rocky cave, he flung himself to the ground. Beating both fists against the stone floor, he cried aloud:

"O earth, split open and swallow me!"

Yet the earth did not open.

And now, the voice of the Lord sounded in his ears:

"Cain, where is your brother Abel?"

"I do not know. Am I my brother's keeper?" he groaned in reply.

"What have you done? Your brother's blood is upon your head. You shall live without rest, without home or shelter. You shall wander across this wide earth and never find peace."

Tears welled up in Cain's eyes, and he said:

"O Lord, my sin is too great to be forgiven. Where can I hide from Your face? Put me to death!"

Then the voice of the Lord spoke:

"No one shall be permitted to kill Cain. This mark shall protect you!"

And He set a mark upon Cain's forehead. From that day onward, Cain wandered across the face of the earth. Nowhere did he ever find rest.

#### Storytelling Notes
- **Poetic cadence in Abel's prayer:** Read the nine lines of prayer slowly, clearly, and solemnly, like smoke rising straight into the sky.
- **Restraint in tragedy:** Tell the moment Cain strikes swiftly and without dwelling on violence, then pour choked sorrow into his cry *"Abel, get up!"* and the roaring forest storm.
- **The voice of the Lord:** Speak with a deep, majestic resonance that softens gently when granting the mark of protection.

---

### Chapter 23: Adam Buries Abel in the First Grave on Earth

On the afternoon after Cain and Abel went to offer sacrifice, neither of them came home to Adam and Eve. So Adam went out to search for his sons. A thin trail of smoke guided him to the place of offering. Was that not Abel sleeping upon the grass? He bent down beside his son and saw blood upon the ground. What had happened here? Truly, Abel's body lay still and breathless right beside the altar!

Overwhelmed with sorrow, Adam stood beside the lifeless body of Abel—the first human being to die upon the earth. Adam seemed to hear a voice speaking down from the clouds above. It was the voice of the Cherubim angels who had once driven him from the Garden of Paradise with the words: *"Upon the earth you shall suffer death."*

For three days and three nights, Adam kept vigil beside Abel's body. He placed an offering upon the altar. When he led Eve to their son, she burst into tears and cried out again and again:

"Abel, dear Abel, get up! Do you not hear your mother calling? Can you no longer be the light of your mother's heart?"

Yet Abel could not answer. She covered her son completely with flowers and kept watch with Adam.

On the third day, Adam saw some ravens burying a dead raven in the soil. Then he remembered the angel's words from long ago: *"For earth you are, and to earth you shall return."*

So Adam took Cain's hoe and dug a grave right beside the altar, and together with Eve he laid Abel inside. He said:

"Your body belongs to the earth, yet your soul has returned to God."

After burying their son, the two felt comforted, and Eve said:

"We shall meet Abel again in heaven, high above the stars, when our time upon the earth comes to an end."

Abel's grave was the very first grave upon the earth.

#### Storytelling Notes
- **A solemn atmosphere:** Read slowly as Adam follows the thin trail of smoke and hears the words of the Cherubim echoing from the high clouds.
- **Turning toward peace:** Let Eve's weeping cries sound heartfelt yet restrained, then brighten warmly when she speaks of reuniting above the stars.

### Chapter 24: Seth, Cain's New Brother

In the evening, as Adam and Eve sat grieving and longing for Abel, an angel appeared and spoke:

"You shall have another son to take the place of Abel. The boy will be radiant like Abel and strong like Cain. His name shall be Seth. Then other sons and daughters will follow into the world, so that there may be many people upon the earth. The soul of an angel will dwell within Seth, and he will bring a great light to humankind."

At the very hour Seth was born, a flock of white doves flew in circles above the roof of Eve's hut. She heard the singing of the angels, rejoicing because one of their own had come down to earth.

When Seth grew into a boy, he did all that Abel had done before him. He tended sheep, built an altar, and offered sacrifice to the Lord.

One day, while Seth was kneeling in prayer before his offering, Cain happened to be wandering across the fields. He caught the scent of smoke and crept stealthily through a grove of trees. Seeing a boy praying in the meadow, he thought Abel had returned to life and walked toward him.

Seth started in alarm at the mighty stranger he had never seen before. Cain asked:

"Who are you? Who are your father and mother?"

"I am Seth, the son of Adam."

When he realized this was indeed his younger brother, Cain embraced and kissed him and burst out weeping:

"Oh, my little brother! My little brother!"

Then, just as suddenly as he had arrived, he let go of Seth and vanished in an instant. Abel's blood gave him no peace at all.

Time passed. Mother Eve gave birth to many more children, and the number of people upon the earth grew ever larger. They still lived near the Garden of Paradise. They aged very slowly and lived for hundreds of years.

#### Storytelling Notes
- **Healing light at the opening:** Read brightly when the angel brings the news of Seth and white doves circle above the roof of the hut.
- **The moment of encounter:** Give Cain a low, questioning tone that suddenly breaks open on *"Oh, my little brother!"* before quickening the pace as he vanishes.

### Chapter 25: An Angel Reveals the Book of Life to Adam

And then this came to pass: an angel of the Lord led Adam into a rocky cave. The angel showed Adam a book in which were written seventy-two Signs of Light. All the wisdom of the world was written within that book.

The angel taught Adam to read the signs in the book and then instructed him:

"Before you pass away, you must hand this book over to someone whose soul is filled with the light of God, so that the wisdom of the angels may keep shining upon the earth."

When Seth turned twelve years old, and Adam saw that he was a divinely blessed child, he took him by the hand and led him to the cave where the sacred book was kept.

The Book of Life was not written upon goatskin parchment; it was *Light written with Light*. When Seth gazed into the book, his eyes were dazzled. He could not read the signs written in the lettering of the stars.

Adam said:

"When I am gone, you shall be the servant of the Book of Wisdom. Show it to no one, lest it be misused for harmful magic. When you grow old, pass it on to one from whose eyes shines the light of the Garden of Paradise. If you find no one like that, seal the entrance of the cave shut. Then the book will remain safe for future ages."

#### Storytelling Notes
- **A sense of sacred mystery:** Slow down and give gentle weight to the phrases *"Light written with Light"* and *"the lettering of the stars."*
- **A voice of entrusted stewardship:** Read steadily, calmly, and clearly when Adam charges Seth to guard the secret and seal the cave entrance.

### Chapter 26: Adam's Death

Adam had grown very old and frail. The earth pulled him toward itself with ever-heavier weight, until his back bent low whenever he walked.

Knowing he would pass away before long, he said to Seth:

"My beloved son! Very soon my soul will return to the Lord. As for my body, it belongs to the earth. My son, will you fulfill one final request for me?"

"Anything that I am able to do," Seth answered.

"Go toward the rising sun. Let the angel of the Lord guide you to the Garden of Paradise, and bring back to me three seeds from the Tree of Life. Place them inside my coffin. From those seeds a tree shall grow that will bring blessing to all humankind."

Seth asked:

"Father Adam, will I be able to find the Garden of Paradise? Whom must I call upon to lead my way?"

Adam replied:

"The Garden of Paradise has been lifted high into heaven. You will never find it upon the earth. Call upon the name of Michael; he will guide you on your way."

Seth walked toward the rising sun until he drew near the cave of the Sacred Book. He stepped inside the cave to rest and fell into a deep sleep.

The Archangel Michael came to his side, lifted his soul up to heaven and into the Garden of Paradise. The fiery gate was guarded by the Cherubim angels, and lightning flashed from that gate. Michael sheltered Seth's soul beneath his wings, bore him through the gate, and brought him to the Tree of Life. Seth picked one apple, split it open, and took three seeds from inside. Then Michael carried Seth flying back through thunder, lightning, and fire, returning him to the cave.

When he woke, Seth found the three seeds lying right in his hand. He hurried back to his father Adam, where everyone had gathered in great numbers. News that Adam was nearing death had spread far and wide. Sons, daughters, grandchildren, and great-grandchildren—all had come to stay close beside Adam in this hour.

Bringing the three seeds, Seth placed them inside Adam's mouth. One final time, Adam gathered his strength and raised himself up. Then he stretched out his hands toward the angel of death sent to guide his soul.

Gently, the angel lifted the soul free from the body of the forefather of humankind. Then Adam's heart ceased beating, his breathing slowed, and his limbs gradually grew stiff.

Lucifer had been waiting for this moment. He thought to himself: *"Adam belongs to me. His soul belongs to my kingdom!"* He approached the angel of death to seize Adam's soul. Yet Michael was already there. Standing before him, the dark evil spirit had no power at all and was forced to flee.

Seth carried Adam's coffin into the cave where the Sacred Book was kept. He had read in the book that in an age to come, the coffin would be carried far away, to the land where the Savior, the Redeemer of humankind, would be born. He had also read that a tree would sprout from the three seeds. The wood of that tree would fashion three things: the staff of a Divine Leader, the pillars of a Temple, and the cross of the Redeemer.

From that time onward, Seth lived near the cave and became known to all people as a priest of the Lord. Everyone who saw the offerings Seth presented to the Creator felt the light of heaven pouring into their souls.

#### Storytelling Notes
- **The journey in sleep:** Read with a soaring, luminous rhythm as Michael shelters Seth's soul beneath his wings and passes through the lightning-guarded gate of the Cherubim.
- **Stillness and triumph:** Slow down solemnly as Adam greets the angel of death; turn sharp and decisive when Michael puts Lucifer to flight.
- **Prophetic resonance:** Let the three sacred things shaped from the Tree of Life ring out clearly, then soften into quiet peace on the closing line about the priest Seth.

### Chapter 27: The Sons of Cain

In those days, the earth was still a vast wilderness where wild beasts of every kind lived everywhere. Herds of wild horses and wild cattle grazed across the wide plains, belonging to no one. Bushes and leafy trees gave people shelter, for nobody yet knew how to build a house! People ate fruits from the trees and the open fields, just like the offering Cain had once brought to the Lord. Not a single hunter went out to hunt animals yet, for in those days no one ever ate meat.

And Cain fathered three sons, whom he named **Jabal**, **Jubal**, and **Tubal-Cain**. Thanks to these three brothers, many things upon the earth changed deeply, for the sons and daughters of Cain all loved to work.

#### Storytelling Notes
- **An unhurried, mythic opening:** Read slowly and clearly so 9- to 10-year-olds can picture the primeval earth before houses or hunting existed.
- **Emphasize the three brothers' names:** Pause slightly before naming **Jabal, Jubal, and Tubal-Cain** to open the saga of the inventive line of Cain.

### Chapter 28: Jabal, the Tamer of Animals

When Jabal was a young man, he had a wondrously strange dream. In his dream, he saw herds of wild horses galloping across the plains. His heart leaped with excitement as he watched their proud bounds and playful frolicking. In those days, no one had ever thought of riding a horse. Imagine Jabal's astonishment when, in his dream, a mysterious being drew near and told him to climb onto a horse's back! At that very moment, a magnificent white horse trotted gracefully past. As it swept by, Jabal sprang straight onto its back. Swift as the wind, the white horse carried him across the meadow. They reached a river, and the horse gathered itself to leap clear to the far bank. Jabal tried his hardest to hold on tight, yet his hands slipped, and he tumbled headlong into the icy water!

Jabal woke with a start and found himself lying asleep beneath a tree! Not far away, a herd of wild horses was grazing. Instantly he thought: *"I will catch a horse and ride it, just like in my dream! I will creep close to the herd and find a way to leap onto one's back. Yet I must be clever, for they are very watchful and swift."*

Hiding behind a bush, Jabal waited tensely until one horse stepped close to his hiding spot. A jet-black stallion with a glossy coat stepped forward. With a single bound, Jabal thought he was already seated upon its back! Yet the stallion reared high into the air, spun sharply around, and flung him tumbling to the ground before he could even grasp its mane. Embarrassed by his failure, Jabal got up while the entire herd whinnied shrilly and bolted away. A blazing determination flared in Jabal's heart. *"I must succeed!"* he told himself. From then on, the thought of catching and riding a horse never left his mind. Day after day, he roamed across the meadows and plains. Time after time he tried to approach the herds, yet their noses were far too sharp—every time they caught his scent and galloped away out of sight.

One day, after all his efforts had come to nothing, Jabal grew terribly thirsty and could find neither stream nor brook anywhere around. He saw a herd of cattle grazing and a little calf suckling at its mother's udder. Jabal had extraordinary strength. He seized a cow by her horns, twisted her head to one side, and wrestled her down onto the ground. He milked her udder and drank eagerly. How sweet and refreshing the milk tasted! Never before had any human being drunk milk! From that day on, Jabal often caught cows to drink their milk.

After some time, Jabal discovered a grassy meadow shaded by leafy trees, where the wild horses liked to come and rest. Early one morning, while the herd went down to drink water, he climbed into one of the trees and hid deep within the foliage. The wild horses returned; sensing no danger, they stood resting beneath the shade of the trees. Suddenly came a rustle of leaves, and quick as lightning, Jabal dropped straight down onto the back of a white horse standing right below! He flattened himself along the horse's back and wrapped both arms tight around its neck. Snorting in anger, the horse bolted wildly out across the field. It reared up on its hind legs, yet Jabal clung fast. Then it galloped madly past branches and rocks, leaping across deep gullies and rocky streams. When Jabal still did not fall, the horse flung itself to the earth and rolled onto its back. Nimble as a flash, Jabal knelt upon the horse's neck and pinned its head firmly to the ground, leaving its four hooves kicking helplessly in the air.

At last the horse grew exhausted and knew it had been mastered. As it lay panting and still, Jabal stroked its neck and spoke soft, soothing words. He patted its back and scratched gently behind its ears. Within a few days, the horse was tamed. Man and horse became friends. Sitting high upon horseback, Jabal rode far and wide across the earth.

Yet everyone was terrified of both horse and rider. Thinking they saw a monster, people fled in panic. The children screamed and ran crying to their parents:

"A monster! Look, a monster! It has six legs and two heads—it looks just as if a man is growing out of its back!"

Seeing everyone scatter in fright, Jabal burst out laughing and dismounted. He took a cord woven from reeds, tied the horse to a tree, and coaxed the people out of their hiding places. Cautiously they stepped forward, yet still kept their distance. One brave little boy, whom Jabal lifted in his arms, dared to stroke the horse's neck. Grown-ups too began gathering round. Suddenly the horse let out a whinny. Everyone shrieked again and bolted away! Jabal laughed aloud and explained that horses call out like that whenever they feel happy.

Nearby stood a few huts. Jabal noticed that all the children were pale and thin. One woman wept and lamented:

"This year we could find hardly any fruit. The whole village has had to dig wild roots and forest tubers just to get by from day to day."

Jabal said:

"I will tame a cow for you, and then all of you will have milk to drink."

They turned to look at one another, shaking their heads in doubt:

"Who knows what on earth he is talking about?"

Jabal tied his horse to a tree and asked a man standing nearby:

"Please watch this horse for me. I will be back this evening."

He walked up into the nearby hills, where he had once seen a herd of cattle grazing. From his hiding place, he dashed toward the herd and grabbed a cow by her horns. That very instant, a huge bull bellowed loudly and charged straight at Jabal. Letting go of the cow, Jabal seized the bull. A fierce wrestling match broke out. At last, Jabal pinned the bull's head down to the ground so powerfully that the beast had to give in and recognize who was master. Defeated, the great bull lumbered off, stomping sullenly away.

Now Jabal had to stalk the herd once again to catch a cow. Taming a cow was far easier than subduing a bull! He led the captured cow back to the village. Once more the children screamed at the top of their lungs:

"He is leading a horned monster back here!"—and every last child ran off to hide.

Yet the brave little boy **Grain**—who had just stroked the horse a moment before—still dared to step close. Jabal tied the cow beside the horse, stroked her gently, and began to milk her. Seeing streams of white liquid spurting from the cow's udder, Grain shouted out:

"White blood! Look, white blood!"

Jabal laughed and told the boy:

"This is milk. It is delicious and wonderfully nourishing!"

He milked some into his cupped palm, drank a taste, and offered it to Grain to try. The milk was so sweet and good that Grain asked for more. The boy turned back to call out to everyone:

"White blood—milk! It tastes wonderfully sweet and good!"

People crept cautiously closer. One by one, Jabal let the first person, then the second, then the third taste the milk. He asked for a bowl and filled it brimming with milk. Just as everyone stood crowded around the bowl, the cow suddenly let out a loud *"Moooo!"* Once again the whole crowd shrieked and scattered in every direction! Only Jabal, little Grain, and the man holding the bowl did not run; all three stood laughing heartily together.

Jabal said to the man holding the bowl:

"Keep this cow for yourselves. Every day she will give you sweet milk. Share the milk with the children and care for her well. I will come back and catch many more cows for you all. Strong as you are, why not go out into the meadow and catch one yourself to raise?"

Then Jabal mounted his horse and rode away. He thought to himself: *"Who knows—besides horses and cattle, perhaps I can tame other animals as well!"* A fierce longing welled up in his heart to do just that.

* * *

Once, Jabal came to a mountainous region. The people living there told him:

"Up in the mountains live horned demons! They climb clear up onto the high rocky crags. Nobody dares go up there."

Jabal replied:

"I dare. Let me climb up and see what sort of creatures they are."

Asking someone to look after his horse, he climbed up the rocky mountains. Suddenly he came upon a whole herd of mountain goats grazing and resting upon green patches of grass. They showed no fear at all. Toward evening, the whole flock began climbing in the same direction. Jabal followed them to a huge cave where they lay down to sleep. He went inside the cave and spent the night there too.

In the morning, Jabal noticed several goats walking over to a boulder and licking it. Discovering salt in the stone, he broke off a chunk and tucked it into his leather pouch. The goats stood quietly while Jabal milked them, and their milk had a mild, sweet taste. Several goats grew so fond of Jabal that wherever he went, they followed after him. He led them down into the valley, offering them a lick of salt now and then. That is how goats came to live with humankind. Jabal gave the herd of goats to the mountain people and taught them how to build pens and fences. Gradually, the mountain folk themselves caught wild goats to tame, for they treasured their sweet, wholesome milk.

Another day, while riding through the deep woods, Jabal heard dreadful yowling and fierce hissing. He saw a large wildcat fighting a tiny kitten. Chasing the big cat away, he scooped the little kitten up into his hands. He stroked its tangled, fluffy fur and slipped the tiny creature inside his coat pocket. When he visited the villagers to whom he had given the cow, he fed the little kitten some milk. Soon the kitten became Jabal's close friend, purring softly and rubbing against his legs. Wherever Jabal went, the cat followed. While Jabal and his horse rested, the cat went stalking mice. Yet one day, after visiting people who lived in huts roofed with leafy branches, Jabal called and called, and the cat did not come. Searching for her, he found her lying under a bush with seven tiny newborn kittens! From that day onward, there were house cats in the world.

On yet another occasion, Jabal heard the howling and whimpering of several wild puppies. Their mother must have left them, since they were no longer tiny. Jabal took one pup along and taught it to ride behind him on horseback. Whenever it slipped off, the puppy trotted cheerfully along beside the horse. Jabal taught it to fetch wooden sticks and do many other tricks. At night, the dog slept right beside its master and the horse.

One day Jabal fell asleep under the shade of a tree, his horse grazing nearby and the dog lying at his feet. A thief hoping to steal the horse crept stealthily closer. Suddenly the dog sprang up, barked furiously, and chased after the intruder. Catching up with him, the dog sank its teeth hard into the thief's leg! Jabal woke and realized what a watchful guardian friend he had beside him. From that day forward, Jabal tamed dogs for humankind. Beyond guarding homes, dogs also became great helpers to shepherds and goatherds.

#### Storytelling Notes
- **Action-packed climaxes:** Read briskly and decisively through the scenes where Jabal rides the bucking white horse and wrestles the wild bull, then soften your voice completely as he strokes and gentles them.
- **Lively dialogue:** Bring out the comic panic in cries of *"a six-legged, two-headed monster!"*, the cow's sudden *"Moooo!"*, and little Grain's delighted wonder at *"white blood!"*
- **A warm closing tone:** Tell softly and fondly of the mountain goats licking rock salt, the seven kittens tucked beneath the bush, and the faithful dog chasing off the thief.

### Chapter 29: Jubal Brings Music to Humankind

Another son of Cain was named **Jubal**. By day, while the bright sun shone overhead, Jubal's heart was always heavy and sad. Yet by night, when the stars sparkled above, Jubal grew wondrously joyful. Why was that? When night fell and Jubal sank into sleep, he always dreamed the loveliest dreams on earth. In his dreams, he stepped through heaven's rainbow gate, where the angels sang and played music. The stars rang out like chiming bells. Listening to the angels sing, he softly joined his voice with theirs. Yet the moment Jubal awoke, he forgot every single song!

In those days, humankind had no musical instruments, nor did anyone yet know how to sing. That was why Jubal felt so sorrowful. He often thought: *"If only I could sleep, dream, and sing forever in that heavenly home! How I wish I never had to return to this earth."* The next night, as he lay down to sleep, he pleaded with his guardian angel:

"Please carry me away from this dreary earth, where neither song nor music brings joy to my heart."

His guardian angel appeared at once and spoke:

"Dear Jubal, the Lord has heard your prayer. He feels pity for you and for all humankind. You, Jubal, shall become a great singer. You are granted permission to bring down to humankind the songs and melodies you heard in heaven. Bring them down as a comfort for the Paradise that was lost."

With those words, the angel handed Jubal a golden cup and told him to drink it dry. The moment Jubal drained the golden cup, the music of heaven and the majestic ringing of the stars echoed brightly in his ears.

When he woke, the music went right on resounding! Music vibrated through his whole body and soul, so that songs poured naturally from Jubal's lips. He walked among the people and lifted his voice in song. Meeting shepherds, he sang the shepherd's song; meeting farmers, he sang songs of the fields. The sun, the stars, and the moon rising at twilight—all were praised in music and sacred hymn. His singing brought comfort to every sorrowful heart. He sang to the children about how God created the world, how the little redbreast robin warbles its song, and how the swift horse gallops. People never forgot those songs. They sang along and passed them down from one generation to the next.

#### Storytelling Notes
- **Light and shadow contrast:** Keep your voice slightly subdued and dreamy during Jubal's daytime sadness, then brighten vividly at heaven's rainbow gate and the stars ringing like bells.
- **From solemnity to joyful flow:** Speak the guardian angel's words softly and warmly; from the moment Jubal drains the golden cup and wakes, let the storytelling cadence dance cheerfully, like melodies drifting across the countryside.

### Chapter 30: Tubal-Cain, the Inventor and Smith

The third son of Cain was named **Tubal-Cain**. He loved the earth, sturdy tree trunks, and caverns deep within rocky mountains. He said:

"I will find the hardest stone upon this earth and forge tools out of it."

He discovered iron. Iron ore was so hard that it could not be broken into pieces at all. He dreamed of softening iron with fire. Yet when he placed it in the middle of a huge roaring bonfire, the iron did not soften one bit. Using stone and clay, he built a furnace and stoked the fire so fiercely hot that the lump of iron finally grew soft and malleable. He punched a hole through the flattened piece of metal and fitted a wooden handle into it. In this way he crafted a hoe far sturdier and more lasting than the old stone and wooden hoes used up to then. He forged such hoes for the farmers. Taking his own tightly clenched fist as a model, he forged the very first iron hammer. That iron hammer was so hard that with it, Tubal-Cain could shatter any rock. He forged the first iron anvil and set it inside his rocky cave, turning that cavern into his smithy.

#### Storytelling Notes
- **A sturdy, grounded voice:** Read steadily, giving weight to each step of discovery: finding the ore, building the stone-and-clay furnace, softening the iron, and shaping the hammer after his clenched fist.
- **A firm closing beat:** Pause briefly on the final sentence as the cave becomes the first smithy, conveying the solid foundation of human craftsmanship taking shape.

### Chapter 31: The Sons of Cain and the First Houses

Once, Jabal lay sleeping atop a hill. He dreamed of a heavenly city filled with temples, tall towers, and houses where people came and went. Filled with delight, he gazed upon those remarkable houses and thought to himself: *"If only I could build houses like these upon the earth."* When he woke, he set right to work building houses out of stone, wood, and clay. They were tiny as toys. He worked tirelessly at them for many days on end.

Tubal stopped by, and after looking over the tiny toy city, he said:

"Brother, you should build these houses sturdy and spacious enough for people to live inside. I will forge tools so you can fell trees! Instead of using little scraps of wood, you must use whole tree trunks. Let me plan this out with you."

Jubal also climbed the hilltop and was delighted by the tiny houses. He said:

"You will need strong men to help you. I will sing them a house-building song. Then they will follow me here to help you."

* * *

A few days later, Jabal rode his white horse deep into the forest toward the cave where his younger brother Tubal-Cain worked. Smoke billowed thick from the cave entrance, and clanging hammer blows rang out so loudly that the horse shied in terror and refused to go any closer. Tying his horse to a tree, Jabal walked toward the cavern mouth. He started in amazement as two fellows smeared black with soot stepped out from inside!

"Is Tubal in there?" he asked.

The two helpers grinned broadly and pointed into the cave. Jabal heard the crackling roar of flames and the deafening ring of an iron hammer. Through the swirling smoke, he spotted the tall, shadowy form of his brother swinging a hammer down with tremendous force.

At last Tubal paused and wiped the sweat from his brow. Seeing Jabal, he burst out laughing:

"Don't you care for my fiery furnace—hot as the underworld?"

Jabal asked:

"Have you finished the tools I need for building houses?"

Tubal took up a pair of tongs and lifted a glowing red bar of iron:

"Here it is—almost finished."

He hammered swiftly along the front edge of the iron, then stepped outside and plunged it straight into a nearby stream to cool. Next he walked to a tree and cut off a thick, sturdy branch. Finally, he drove that branch hard through the hole in the blunt end of the iron head. That was **the very first axe**. Swinging mighty strokes with the axe, Tubal chopped straight into the trunk of the very tree whose branch he had just cut. Wood chips flew in every direction, and within moments the massive trunk crashed thunderously to the ground. Jabal gazed at the mighty felled tree and felt an unusual awe for the small iron wedge held in Tubal's hand.

Then Tubal brought something else out from the cave. He showed Jabal a long strip of iron lined with strange jagged teeth and said:

"With the teeth in my mouth, I can crunch up tiny splinters of wood. Now I have made teeth of iron that can bite clean through timber!"

The two brothers walked up to a fir tree and began to saw. Both of them sweated and struggled hard before they finally sawed through the trunk. The blade kept binding tight because its teeth were still thick and spaced unevenly. Jabal said:

"I prefer the axe."

Tubal replied:

"I will forge newer, better saws still."

Then Tubal's helpers began forging many more axes for Jabal.

That very day, Jubal brought along ten strong men eager to help Jabal build a house. Soon the ring of chopping wood and the crash of falling trees echoed across the forest. By the third day, they had felled enough timber for the first house. Straight, slender trunks were sharpened at one end and cut into poles twice the height of a grown man. Two men lay stretched head-to-toe along each trunk as a measure so the logs would be cut to the right length. The first house Jabal built was a **round house**. The logs were driven deep into the ground to form a circle. They gathered **palm fronds, meadow grass, and river reeds** growing round a nearby lake to thatch the roof. Yet before the roof was finished, they invited the priest **Seth** to come and bless the house.

Seth spoke to the crowd gathered round:

"This house shall be a Little Paradise, a Garden of Paradise in miniature, where evil may never enter. Make a place of offering right in the center of the house so we may hold a consecration service. And place a fresh green branch atop the peak of the roof in remembrance of the tree in the Garden of Paradise."

When all was made ready, a great crowd gathered around the house. Jabal, Jubal, and Tubal helped Seth kindle the sacrificial fire. As the smoke curled high into the air, Jubal joyfully lifted his voice to sing the song of consecration:

> The house upon the earth is raised upright.
> May no flame ever burn it down,
> May lightning from the sky never strike this place,
> May a kindly spirit dwell within the home,
> Driving far away all lurking dangers
> And sheltering this warm hearth against the floods.
> Peace abide within this dwelling place,
> Blessing rest upon this house,
> Upon all who dwell within,
> And all who come in and go out.

As Jubal sang, the smoke of the offering rose straight up to heaven. Waiting until the sacrificial fire burned low and the final embers sparkled inside the hearth-pit, Jabal and the builders thatched the roof tight with reeds and meadow grass. Yet nobody knew whom this house would belong to. When the house stood complete, Jabal spoke:

"The very first house upon the earth must be dedicated to the Lord. I give this house to His priest, Seth."

From that day onward, Seth lived in that house and kept it as a holy place. People called it **the House of God**. In later days, Jabal and his builders went everywhere building houses, yet never was a single house roofed completely before offering a sacrifice of consecration and celebrating **the roof-raising blessing with a fresh green branch fastened** atop the peak of the roof.

#### Storytelling Notes
- **Pace shifting from dream to labor:** Begin softly with the toy-like model on the hilltop, shift to lively smoke and clamor inside the smithy cave, then match the crisp rhythm of axes felling timber.
- **Read the consecration hymn with quiet reverence:** Pause a breath after each line of blessing to evoke the sacred stillness around the offering fire and the custom of the roof-raising blessing with a green branch.

### Chapter 32: The Three Knives

The priest Seth said to Tubal-Cain:

"Could you forge for me a keepsake out of pure gold shaped like a ray of sunlight? When I offer sacrifice, I will thrust it into the soil so that the fields too may receive a blessing. Make an iron handle for it as well, so I may hold it fast."

He handed Tubal a nugget of gold he had found in the mountains. With great skill, Tubal and his smiths forged a **golden knife**. From that time onward, whenever he offered sacrifice, Seth prayed for God's blessing and asked the sun to warm the seeds and the earth. As a sign of that prayer, he pressed the golden knife into the ground. Only after this was done were the plow and the hoe allowed to turn and till the soil.

* * *

Once, Jubal lay sleeping beneath a large bush. In his dream, an angel appeared playing music upon a heavenly instrument. It looked like a hollow tube with several small holes along its side. Jubal asked the angel:

"Could I also blow an instrument like this? How I love it!"

The angel pointed toward the bush:

"Take wood from these very branches and fashion that instrument."

Jubal woke up. He broke a branch from the bush, went to Tubal-Cain, and told him his dream. Tubal said:

"Let me forge a silver knife. Using it, we can hollow and carve that instrument from wood."

With wondrous skill, Tubal forged a **silver knife** and used it to carve **the very first wooden flute**. That flute was identical to the heavenly flute in his dream.

* * *

Meanwhile, one of Tubal-Cain's apprentices secretly forged an **iron knife**. He thought to himself: *"Here is a tool that is sure to serve many uses."* When finished, he hid that knife carefully inside a rocky crevice in the wall of the cave.

#### Storytelling Notes
- **Give three distinct colors to the three knives:** Speak with solemn dignity for the sunbeam-shaped golden knife; light and clear for the silver knife and the wooden flute; then drop low, slow, and foreboding for the iron knife hidden in the rock crevice.
- **Hold brief silences between the three parts:** Pause for about two seconds at each section break so the children feel the contrast between gold, silver, and iron.

### Chapter 33: Jubal Makes Music for the Animals

Jubal saw another wondrous instrument in a dream. When he described it, Tubal-Cain fashioned a wooden **harp**, strung with sheep gut that had been twisted, stretched taut, and dried in the sun.

Jubal sat beneath a tree in the forest and plucked the strings. Curious little mice crept cautiously out to hear the music and danced, then after a while stood perfectly still to listen. A wildcat came along and spotted the mice, yet the harp sounded so sweet that it forgot the mice altogether and stretched out flat at Jubal's feet; not a single mouse ran away. Two hares heard the music and hopped over to sit beside the cat, pricking up their ears and swaying in time with the melody.

A fox slunk into the clearing. Seeing the little creatures, it drooled hungrily: *"I'll dash right in and make a three-course feast!"* Yet the sound of Jubal's harp soothed its appetite, making its bushy tail wag gently to the beat until the fox too lay down beside the two hares.

The bushes rustled, and out stepped a wolf! Sharp eyes darting over the gathered creatures, the wolf calculated silently: *"Shall I catch the fox, the cat, or the hares?"* Yet the music slipped into its ears; even though it raised a paw to swat at its ear as if brushing away a fly, the wolf still had to lie down, rest its chin upon its front paws, and shut its eyes to listen.

Branches snapped and crackled, and a great bear came lumbering out of the forest. Hearing the harp, old bear stood upright on its hind legs and danced into the clearing, swaying back and forth for a long while before plopping down peacefully onto its belly. All the animals lay gathered around Jubal, listening to the music until gradually they fell fast asleep. Jubal smiled and thought to himself: *"Back in the Garden of Paradise long ago, all the creatures lived gently together just like this."* When dusk settled over the woods, he stood up and left the forest.

When the mice woke up, it had already grown pitch-dark; spotting the wildcat, they darted straight down into their burrows. Next the two hares awoke, glanced nervously around, and bounded into the bushes. The wildcat yawned and stretched, saw the fox and the wolf, and slipped quietly into the shadows. The fox yawned and opened its eyes, caught the scent of wolf and bear, and asked: *"What on earth am I doing here?"*—and slunk away at once. The wolf scratched its nose: *"What? Have I been sleeping beside a bear?!"*—and bolted off at full speed.

Only old bear slept straight through until the next morning before waking. Blinking bewilderedly around, it exclaimed: *"My word, what a marvelous dream I just had—mice, hares, a fox, a wildcat, a wolf, and even a man making music! What a wonderful dream! How I wish I could dream it all over again."* Then the bear ambled contentedly back to its den.

#### Storytelling Notes
- **Slow down the pace:** Keep an even, soothing tone from the moment the mice dance until the bear settles down to sleep, letting the forest grow still beneath the harp's melody.
- **Humor at dawn's awakening:** Quicken the tempo as each animal wakes in fright and darts off into the night, then draw out the bear's puzzled, dreamy voice in the morning light.

### Chapter 34: Jubal Plays Music for Humankind

**Naamah**, one of Cain's daughters, wove a long white cloak as a gift for Jubal. Clad in white and riding his white horse, he looked like a messenger of the Lord. When night fell, he liked to rest by the edge of a forest or atop a hill, playing his flute beneath the twinkling stars while no wild beast ever harmed him. One night, he halted upon a hilltop without knowing there was a village nearby. His horse lay down beside his feet. As Jubal played his flute, a little girl inside one of the huts suddenly woke up, nudged her little brother, and whispered:

"Listen! A night-bird is singing."

Her little brother whispered back:

"No, it isn't. That must be an angel! I have never heard any bird sing like that before. Let's go outside and listen, sister."

Their parents were fast asleep and did not know the two children had stepped outdoors. Music drifted down from the hilltop, where a white figure could be seen. Not daring to climb the hill alone, the two children went back inside to wake their parents:

"Listen! There is music drifting down from the hilltop."

Listening in amazed reverence, the parents took their two children by the hand and slowly climbed the hill. Halfway up the slope, they stopped; beneath the moonlight and starlight, the figure clad in white stood out clearly. Pure, gentle melodies rang through the stillness, moving the whole family to sink to their knees and bow their heads. Before long the entire village awoke and came hurrying to gather at the foot of the hill, none daring to climb higher lest they disturb the music.

Then Jubal lifted his harp and began to sing. His music carried far into the night until the break of dawn. He mounted his white horse, raised a hand in greeting, and rode down the far side of the hill. Everyone bowed respectfully; soon he disappeared behind the dark trees. The villagers built a stone altar upon the very spot where he had sat and sung, offering a sacrifice of thanksgiving to the heavenly power that had spoken through Jubal's music.

Along his wanderings, Jubal often met children, played music for them, and taught them simple songs. Whenever he rode away, children ran after him until late dusk, begging for just one more song. Even the youngest began humming along, while the older ones whittled **bamboo flutes and reed pipes** of their own. And so music and song filled the life of humankind.

#### Storytelling Notes
- **A quiet, starlit night:** Begin softly and gently, keeping the whispered wonder between the two small siblings, then shift to hushed reverence as the villagers kneel at the foot of the hill.
- **A bright, joyful close:** Let your voice brighten cheerfully when the children run along behind him learning songs and carving bamboo flutes and reed pipes.

### Chapter 35: Of Wicked Companions and Evil Deeds

One day, Tubal-Cain and most of his workers left the cave to mine iron ore. Two men stayed behind to tend the fire and finish forging a hoe. The older smith, named **Kenos**, gripped the glowing red iron bar with tongs upon the anvil, while the other swung the hammer, sending sparks showering everywhere. One hammer blow slipped off the anvil and struck Kenos hard in the side. Flying into a rage, Kenos punched his companion squarely in the face; the other dropped his hammer and struck Kenos on the neck. The two grappled furiously across the floor of the cave. Just as his strength was failing, Kenos's eyes fell upon the iron knife tucked in the rocky crevice. He snatched it up, lunged forward, and stabbed his companion to death!

Once the deed was done, Kenos stood frozen in horror. Seeing blood spreading across the floor, he dropped the knife and fled. Anger still boiled in his veins, while in his ears he seemed to hear the mocking laughter of evil spirits ringing behind him. Kenos ran desperately for his life through the forest; storm winds howled through the treetops, lightning flashed in the distance, and heavy rain poured through the pines and oaks.

Stumbling on for a long while, Kenos came upon a cave and collapsed onto the stone floor. Through clenched teeth, he muttered: *"He was always provoking me. Now he got what he deserved."* Without a shred of remorse in his heart, he dropped his head upon his arms and fell asleep.

The following evening, Tubal and his workers returned. *"What happened here?"* he asked when he saw no smoke curling out and found the forge fire dead cold. Stepping inside the cave, one of the men tripped over a lifeless body. A cry of terror rang out; in the dark they could not recognize who it was, so they carried the body outside. One man drew the knife out from the victim's chest and spoke sorrowfully:

"Kenos has stabbed him to death!"

Tubal gazed at the bloodstained knife for a long while and then said:

"I forged the golden knife for the priest Seth to offer sacrifice. The singer Jubal keeps the silver knife. Yet this iron knife has brought calamity. Should I bury it deep in the earth or cast it down into an abyss?"

Then he thought: *"Long ago Cain killed Abel without needing any weapon. The knife is innocent. Kenos is the murderer, and he has fled."* He plunged the knife into the soil several times to wipe away the blood, then tucked it back inside the rocky crevice.

#### Storytelling Notes
- **Mounting tension:** Pick up the pace during the glancing hammer blow and the struggle on the cave floor, then stop short at the moment Kenos stabs his partner.
- **Gloom and sorrow:** Lower your voice heavily as Kenos flees unrepentant through the storm, and read Tubal's reflections on the three knives with quiet sadness.

### Chapter 36: Kenos in the Forest Cave

On the first night Kenos slept inside his forest cave, an evil spirit crept up beside him and whispered:

"Go! Slip back to Tubal's cave and take that knife. With it, you will hold power over humankind and slay beasts to eat meat and satisfy your hunger!"

Early the next morning, Kenos sneaked back toward Tubal's cave, hiding behind boulders and trees until he saw the master and his helpers go down to the stream to wash their faces as they did every morning. Quietly he slipped into the empty cave and felt around in the darkness—the knife was still sitting in the rocky crevice! He imagined the drops of blood upon the cave floor were whispering his name and clinging to his heels. Quickly grabbing the knife, he sprinted away into the woods.

Suddenly a fierce wolf with wild, streaked eyes blocked his path! Kenos leaped at it, stabbed the wolf dead, and drank its warm blood. He dragged the carcass back to his secret cave, struck stones together to kindle a fire as Tubal had taught him, roasted the wolf meat until he had eaten his fill, and spread the wolf's hide for a bed.

Word of the murder reached the priest **Seth**. He summoned the people to offer a sacrifice of atonement inside **the Sacred House**. While the sacred flame blazed high and prayers rose upward, Jubal plucked his harp and the priests and priestesses stepped in a sacred dance around the altar.

Meanwhile, Kenos prowled near the village and spied from afar the crowd of people heading toward the Sacred House for the service. Not far away, he met a young man lounging lazily upon a bearskin. Asked why he had not gone to the ceremony, the fellow answered:

"I am far too lazy; only yesterday I finished chopping down a tree."

Delighted to meet someone avoiding the service, Kenos sat down, drew out his knife to show off, and said:

"Come with me, and I will teach you how to use this. Maybe I can even get you one!"

Tempted by the promise, the fellow followed Kenos into the forest. Together they killed a wild boar and roasted its meat for days on end. From then on they went hunting together, striking terror into all the beasts of the woods with hearts full of cruelty, sparing neither hind nor stag—killing not just for meat, but for the cruel pleasure of chasing and slaughtering.

Sometime later, Tubal dismissed an apprentice who was always cursing and blaspheming the name of the Lord while at work. Grumbling and wandering across the fields, he was discovered by Kenos and his companion sleeping among damp leaves. Birds of a feather, they invited him back to their cave. Now there were three of them.

#### Storytelling Notes
- **Chilling contrast:** Speak the whispers of the evil spirit and the calling bloodstains in a cold, eerie murmur, sharply contrasting with the solemn ceremony of atonement at the Sacred House.
- **A rough, hard closing beat:** Keep your tone crisp and sober as the two men hunt for sport and draw the third outlaw into their band.

### Chapter 37: How Jubal Escaped Great Danger

One evening, Jubal entered the very forest where those wicked men lived. When night fell, he was surprised to see firelight flickering among the trees. Drawing closer, he saw three men crouching around a fire.

"Greetings!" Jubal called out.

Startled, all three sprang up and vanished into the dark bushes. Suspecting nothing, Jubal laughed at their timidity, sat down beside the fire, and called out cheerfully:

"Come back, forest travelers! There is plenty of room by the fire for all of us."

Yet they dared not step out for fear Jubal would give them away, so instead they plotted to kill him. As the three crept stealthily closer, Jubal began to pluck his harp. Through the quiet night woods, the music sounded so wondrously beautiful that they forgot their wicked plot, stepped out of hiding, and lay down beside the fire. The hearts of the two younger men softened before that heavenly music; it felt as though a light kindled within their souls, and tears slipped silently down their cheeks.

Yet the murderer Kenos ground his teeth together fiercely. Once more the evil spirit whispered in his ear:

"Look—he is casting a spell over your two companions! Smash his harp! Silence that singing throat of his!"

"May I spend the night here?" Jubal asked.

Kenos nodded craftily, led him deep inside the cave, spread out a bearskin for him to lie upon, and then they all lay down. Right beside his own place, Kenos stuck his dagger upright into the earth.

Just as Jubal was drifting off to sleep, an angel appeared and warned him:

"Jubal, play your harp! Do not stop your hands until all of them are fast asleep, for among them is one who harbors evil designs against you."

Jubal held his harp close and played a gentle, soothing lullaby. Only when he was sure all three men were dead asleep did he finally lay himself down to rest.

Early the next morning, the cheerful singing of birds woke Jubal. He set out gladly upon his journey while the three ruffians still slept soundly. As he walked through the woods, he sang and played his harp; flocks of birds flew ahead of him from tree to tree, guiding him safely out of the forest.

#### Storytelling Notes
- **Suspense easing into gentleness:** Keep your voice low and measured while the three men lurk in the dark, then warm and soften as the two younger men weep beside the fire.
- **The angel's warning and daybreak:** Read the angel's message urgently yet softly, maintain a steady lullaby cadence, and brighten brightly with the birds leading the way at dawn.

### Chapter 38: The Death of Seth

As long as **Seth** lived among humankind, he upheld them with his light and his teachings. He passed on to them great wisdom drawn from **the Book of Adam**. Seth was the forefather of the priests—those who offered sacrifices to the Lord on behalf of the people. Everywhere, people reverently brought sacred offerings and served the Divine Beings. In those days, wicked folk were still very few; they lived by hunting in the dense forests of the high mountains, and now and then crept down to steal from the huts of the peaceful people.

As time went by, more and more new houses sprang up across the plains. Herds of tame cattle, goats, and sheep grew ever larger. While they worked, the people often sang the songs they had learned from **Jubal**—praising the wondrous works of the Creator. Everywhere there were smithies like the forge of **Tubal-Cain**, where smiths crafted farming tools and implements for building homes. A few had also learned from Tubal-Cain the art of making musical instruments; they fashioned harps, horns, and bamboo flutes.

When Seth grew very old and knew that his final day was not far off, he went looking for someone to whom he could entrust the Book of Adam. Yet search as he might, he found no one whose soul-light was strong enough to stand before the dazzling Light of the book. With a heavy heart, Seth hid the book deep inside, sealed the entrance of the secret cave tight, and before long passed away. Humankind mourned him with deep, unending sorrow. In their grief, the priests prepared a farewell offering and asked one another sadly:

"Now that Seth has left us, who will tell us the will of the Lord?"

#### Storytelling Notes
- **Opening pace:** Keep a warm, bright voice as you describe the lively plains, the forges, and Jubal's songs.
- **Quiet close:** Lower your voice to a solemn tone as Seth seals the cave; let the final question linger softly with wistfulness.

### Chapter 39: How Enos Brought Idol Worship to Humankind

Long before, **Enos** had been taught by Seth how the Lord formed human beings from the earth and breathed sacred breath into them to give them a soul. Yet Enos became a scoffer and joined the band of the wicked. Arrogantly he declared:

"I myself will fashion a living creature and give it the breath of life!"

He molded a clay figure with a fierce, terrifying face. Setting the statue upon an altar, he kindled a fire and breathed upon it. That false sacrifice at once drew an evil spirit to the spot. The evil spirit slipped inside the statue and seized power over Enos and his followers. They began to serve that evil spirit and practice dark sorcery.

And so evil spread wider and wider among humankind. Wicked bands came down from their mountain lairs and committed countless crimes. Carrying long knives—which they called swords—they threatened and slew anyone who stood against them. Evil grew without ceasing; many practiced dark arts that brought harm and chaos. The age of Enos witnessed four harsh changes: the mountains hardened completely into stone, barren deserts appeared, human beings lost the image of God, and the evil spirits no longer feared humanity. The idol worshipers painted jagged black marks across their foreheads as a sign that they were true companions of Enos and servants of the devil.

For a long time, only a faithful few continued to offer sacrifices alongside the priests of the line of Seth. Jubal's songs were heard no more; the sound of flutes and harps fell utterly silent. Deep sorrow enveloped the angels in heaven, and their weeping over the corruption of humankind rose all the way up to the Lord.

#### Storytelling Notes
- **Dark tone:** Read Enos's boast full of pride; from the moment the evil spirit enters the statue, drop your voice into a low, heavy rasp.
- **The four changes:** Pace out each of the four changes in Enos's time clearly before turning to the weeping of the angels.

### Chapter 40: The Light of Goodness Returns to Earth Through Enoch

The Creator chose the great and courageous soul of **Enoch**, and turned Enoch's eyes to look down upon the earth. Enoch saw thick darkness closing in around humankind. Up from the earth rose curses, cries of grief, bitter complaints, and the clamor of war. He spoke and answered:

"The shining image of humankind is besieged by the dragon. Cries of agony and torment echo up from the abyss. O Lord! Send me down to earth to save humankind with light from Your own Light. I dare to take upon myself the task of winning them back for Your kingdom! I will fight against evil upon the earth!"

As soon as Enoch finished speaking, God the Father bestowed upon him light from His own Light.

Before long, Enoch was born upon the earth as a tiny baby. His soul shone so brightly that his parents' house was filled with warm radiance. A servant girl hurried out into the fields to bring his father the joyful news that a son had just been born.

When the father reached home, he gasped in wonder to see flocks of snow-white birds circling around his house. Higher and higher they wheeled until they vanished into the deep blue sky. Right at the doorway, a radiant beam of light shone toward him. Stepping inside, he saw a little babe resting quietly beside his mother. The child's eyes shone like the sun. Tenderly, the father lifted his son into his arms.

At that very hour, an old man arrived at the house—an old man who in days long past had known Seth! A dream had foretold Enoch's birth to him, and joyfully he said to the child's parents:

"Rejoice with me! In your son, the light of Seth has returned to the earth once more! Now I can die in peace, knowing a new messenger of God has been sent to humankind. As long as these feet can still walk, I will go everywhere proclaiming that Enoch has been born—the greatest priest since Seth!"

#### Storytelling Notes
- **Radiant tone:** Give Enoch's pledge heroic resolve; when the snow-white birds appear, open your voice to a clear, luminous brightness.
- **Overflowing joy:** Let the old man's voice tremble gently with emotion, yet ring out warmly in his final proclamation.

### Chapter 41: Enoch Makes His First Offering

When Enoch was still a boy, evil spirits tried many times to creep near and harm him, yet all their schemes came to nothing. His guardian angel always shielded him from every danger.

When he grew into a young man, Enoch entered a city where crowds of idol worshipers lived. He saw the multitude dancing wildly around a towering idol and heard blasphemous words pouring from their mouths.

A holy wrath suddenly welled up in Enoch's heart. His voice boomed like thunder, making the clay idol sway and tremble violently. With one mighty shove, he toppled the statue onto the stone pavement, where it shattered into pieces with a deafening crash. The idol worshipers shouted in fury; many scattered in panic, while others stared at Enoch in terror. He called out loudly:

"Wipe away those jagged black marks from your foreheads! Your demon has fled. The Light of God will live again among humankind. Bring firewood! We will kindle an offering and lift up our prayers!"

The people were so overwhelmed by Enoch's mighty presence that they could do nothing but obey him. They hurried to fetch firewood, and soon the sacrificial flame blazed up upon the very stone pedestal where the idol had stood. Turning toward the fire, Enoch spoke sacred words. Everyone gathered there marveled at his courage and wiped the black marks from their foreheads. Great numbers of people drew close around that new offering.

From that day onward, Enoch journeyed from place to place, helping human souls find their way back to the kingdom of heaven.

#### Storytelling Notes
- **Burst of power:** Speed up slightly for the wild dancing, then gather strong, resonant force as Enoch topples the idol.
- **Brief pause:** Pause briefly after the crashing statue before shifting to a solemn voice beside the fire.

### Chapter 42: Enoch Finds the Cave of the Sacred Books

One day, while Enoch was sitting beside a little stream to rest, a majestic archangel suddenly appeared before him. At first, he thought to himself, *"Could my hour of death have come?"*

"No, it has not," the angel replied gently. "I have been sent by the Lord to show you the cave where Seth sealed the Book of Adam."

The angel led Enoch to the entrance of the cave. Enoch broke open the sealed stone wall and stepped into the sacred chamber. It was a vast cavern flooded with light, its rocky walls sparkling and flashing with countless crystal rays. The radiance pouring from the book was so blinding that he was forced to turn his face away.

Turning aside, Enoch caught sight of a casket. The angel told him:

"Here rests the body of Adam. This mortal frame is kept for future ages, and one day it will be carried to another place."

Then the angel taught Enoch how to read the book in which all the wisdom of the world was written. Enoch remained inside the cave for a very long time. His body needed not a bite of food nor a drop of water, for he stood within the Light of God and was nourished by the Lord Himself. When he returned to humankind, Enoch's soul overflowed with wisdom and holy light.

#### Storytelling Notes
- **Mystical atmosphere:** Keep your voice slow and gentle, like a flowing stream, to evoke the dazzling crystal cavern.
- **The casket:** Read the angel's words calmly and clearly to evoke solemn reverence for what lies in the future.

### Chapter 43: Enoch's Journeys

Once, while walking through a dense forest, Enoch suddenly heard loud shouting and boisterous laughter ringing out. He thought to himself, *"That sounds like a fierce and rowdy band. Should I avoid them and turn down another path?"* He stood still for a moment, then told himself, *"Light must shine into the darkness. Whoever they may be, I will walk straight up to meet them."*

He strode toward the forest clearing where the rough voices came from and discovered a band of forest robbers. They were sitting in a circle around a campfire, roasting meat. Enoch saw that every single one of them had jagged black lines painted across his forehead. In a deep, resonant voice filled with quiet power, he greeted them.

The robbers were so astonished and awed that not a single one dared oppose him. They stopped their shouting and jeering and invited him to sit by the fire. None of them had ever seen Enoch before, so they wondered silently among themselves, *"Who is this fearless traveler who knows no dread and dares to share a fire with a band of robbers?"* Without saying a word, the robbers went right on chewing their meat and gnawing on bones.

Enoch gazed steadily into the flames and began to speak:

"In the beginning, the Creator fashioned heaven, earth, and human beings out of light and fire. All things came into being through Him. From Him, all life springs forth. Everything that grows upon the trees in the deep forest, creeps upon the earth or soars through the air, and everything that gives light and warmth to humankind—all of it has its life in God."

Filled with wonder, the band of robbers listened as Enoch told of the seven days of creation. Never before had they heard anything like it. One after another, they tossed their bones aside, wiped their mouths with the backs of their hands, and settled down comfortably by the fire.

When Enoch spoke of the angels, one of the men listening suddenly challenged him:

"Doesn't God stand on the side of the angels, while the devil stands on the side of humankind? An old sorcerer taught me that! God drove us out of the Garden of Paradise and pushed us into the devil's realm. So we might as well serve him in whatever way pleases us!"

Calmly, Enoch replied:

"Human beings stand between God and the devil just like a flower standing between light and darkness: with its blossom, it opens wide to welcome the light; and with its roots, it holds fast to the darkness so that it may stand straight and firm!"

With powerful words, Enoch went on praising the angels and the works of the Light, until one by one the robbers quietly wiped away the jagged black marks from their foreheads. So absorbed were they in listening that they did not notice the fire dying down. When one of them piled on fresh wood and the flames leaped up brightly, they all turned their clean foreheads toward the fire. Enoch stood up, reached into his garment pocket for a pinch of fragrant frankincense resin, cast it into the flames, and spoke the sacred words of offering.

Seeing the sacrifice and remembering all the wrongs they had done, tears of shame and repentance suddenly streamed down the faces of those men. Earnestly they pleaded with Enoch:

"Let us follow you! We do not want to stay in this robbers' den anymore!"

The next morning, they went down to the river to bathe and cleanse both body and soul. From then on, Enoch always traveled with a company of companions. Day by day, the crowd following him grew until it became an entire tribe. At last, Enoch decided to settle down in one place. He chose the land at the foot of the mountain near the cave where the Book of Adam was hidden. From that time onward, he lived in that cave, while his followers built their homes all around and diligently plowed and planted the good earth. Everyone was deeply happy to live close beside Enoch.

#### Storytelling Notes
- **Contrast the voices:** Make the robbers' speech blunt and gruff; keep Enoch's voice warm, deep, and glowing like fire as he tells of Creation.
- **Parable of the flower:** Read slowly and clearly distinguish the two halves—*"with its blossom... opens wide to welcome the light; with its roots... holds fast to the darkness."*
- **Tears of repentance:** Soften your voice with a touch of tenderness as they wipe away the black marks and weep beside the frankincense smoke.

### Chapter 44: The Mountain of God and Its Priests

More and more people came to listen to Enoch's teachings. Every day, he climbed the hill above the cave to offer sacrifice at the altar and tell the people about the kingdom of heaven.

Throngs of people pitched their tents until the hillsides were covered. Enoch's words poured the warmth and light of God into their souls. They lived together in harmony like brothers and sisters of one family. It is told that in Enoch's days, the fields yielded richer harvests of grain and the trees blossomed and bore fruit far more abundantly than before. Evil slowly departed from human hearts, and the angels carried many glad tidings from earth back up to heaven.

Yet Enoch thought quietly to himself, *"I will not stay upon the earth forever. Humankind must learn to see the Light of God for themselves without me. From now on, I will climb the mountain to offer sacrifice only once every two days."*

Step by step, Enoch taught the people how to walk in the Light of God on their own without him beside them. He gave them prayers for morning and for evening, for hours of labor and times of rest.

#### Storytelling Notes
- **Warm, unhurried pace:** Read calmly and peacefully through the passage about abundant harvests and brotherly harmony on the hillside.
- **The teacher's foresight:** Lower your voice gently and calmly when Enoch resolves to help the people stand on their own.

### Chapter 45: Enoch's Ascension into Heaven

After a year had passed, Enoch came to the people only once every three days. As months and years rolled on, he appeared less and less often, until he climbed the mountain only on the seventh day of each week. By then there were many other priests—called the *"Descendants of Seth"*—guiding humankind alongside him, yet their offerings did not carry the power of Enoch's.

Then came the time when Enoch appeared only once a month. On those days, a vast multitude gathered, honoring him as a messenger of God. When his tall figure, robed in pure white, stood solemnly performing the sacrifice at the mountaintop altar, the whole sea of people fell completely hushed. When he lifted his voice to speak the sacred words, the mountain wind carried his voice echoing far down into the valley. Not a single soul went home without becoming a better person.

At last came the time when Enoch appeared only once a year. After seven such years, he told the people that he was soon to leave them forever:

"The call has come to me that I am soon to ascend into heaven. For the last time I say to you: practice and fulfill upon the earth those deeds that lead to heavenly light."

The crowd could not understand his words; many thought he had just told them a parable. Yet lo, behold! When the offering ended, he did not walk back toward his cave as usual, but turned straight toward the towering mountain range crowned with ice and white snow! Realizing that Enoch was truly parting from them forever, a long procession of people set off behind him.

After walking some distance, Enoch turned back and said:

"Return home, good people! Even when I have departed from you, I will still be near you in your offerings, if only you remember me."

Many turned back with heavy hearts and tear-filled eyes. Enoch climbed higher and higher toward the steep, jagged crags. Some way behind him, a steadfast band who could not bear to part from him still followed on. He paused and looked back once more, and when they reached him, he urged them:

"Turn back, my faithful friends! The earth needs your strength, for once Enoch is gone, a terrible, pitch-black darkness will descend. Become Warriors of the Light!"

With those words, he climbed into the white snow and ice of the highest peaks. Nearly all who had followed turned back down the mountain. Only one old man, nearly exhausted, sought shelter from the biting wind among the rocky crevices. From there he saw a few people stumbling after Enoch up onto the glacier.

Snow began to fall. In the fiery red glow of sunset, the old man watched Enoch's figure grow faint and blend into the whiteness of the mountain. He saw one after another of that faithful group sink down into the deep snow. They were freezing to death.

The red glow of evening faded, and the stars came out across the sky. Suddenly, a winged white horse swooped down from a radiant cloud toward the earth! Vigorous and shining like a mighty youth, Enoch mounted its back. He waved and stretched out his arms toward his dying companions. And behold! Rising out of their icy graves, twelve souls soared upward toward him and clung fast to the winged horse's tail! With powerful beats of its wings, the horse sprang straight up into heaven and vanished among the stars.

The next morning, several men climbed to the high peaks in search of their missing companions. Upon the glacier, they saw several frozen blocks of ice that they mistook for the bodies of their friends. Among the rocky crevices, they found only the frail old man. Lifting him onto their shoulders, they carried him back down to the valley. Once he had recovered his strength, he told of Enoch's ascension into heaven. From that day forward, he traveled from place to place recounting everything he had seen, so that all who loved Enoch might know how he had been carried up into heaven.

#### Storytelling Notes
- **Widening intervals:** Gradually slow your pace as Enoch's appearances grow rarer, evoking the feeling of an approaching farewell.
- **Tragic grandeur on the snowy peak:** Quicken slightly as the icy wind and snow rise; let your voice catch softly as the faithful followers collapse.
- **Climax of the ascension:** As the stars come out and the white horse descends, lift your voice high and soaring toward the starry sky.

## Part III: Noah's Ark

### Chapter 46: Old Methuselah

As evening came on, old Methuselah sat before his hut. His son Lamech walked up and said:

"Dear Father, I have brought you fruit from the fields: dates, apples, and pears. May they give you strength!"

"Sit beside me, Lamech, to help the time pass," old Methuselah said. "My legs have grown far too old and weak now; today they can carry me no longer. Tell me the news. How are the people living down in the valley?"

Lamech began to tell him:

"Today, while I was working in the fields, a band of young men came marching past. They mocked me and my work, and threw stones into my fields. I turned away, so they aimed stones up into the trees to ruin the fruit. Look, Father, many of the figs and pears have been bruised and battered."

"Oh, this sinful generation!" Methuselah cried out. "What more must my old eyes live to see? There was a time when people looked up to Enoch. He went before us, leading us up the mountain, and when he offered sacrifice, the angels leaned down from heaven to bless all who prayed. Oh, Lamech, how dark the world has grown now!"

"Yes, Father, it is true," Lamech answered. "People dream up all manner of evil. Out from the huts in the valley come fierce, roving gangs. They roam across the countryside, plundering the fields and slaughtering the farmers' animals to eat their meat. They have built many stone houses and call the place a city. They demanded to have a king. The worst among them—one whom everyone fears—has set a crown upon his own head. A traveler who saw it with his own eyes told me so today."

While Lamech spoke, old Methuselah ate a little of the fruit. Looking at a bruised apple, he said:

"Humankind no longer walks in the ways of God. The world has grown corrupt. Even the fruit no longer tastes as sweet as it did in days of old. The grain in the fields has shriveled thin and hollow. Unless a new Enoch comes, the earth will fall completely into sin."

#### Storytelling Notes
- **A slow, deep voice for old Methuselah:** Convey his advanced age and frailty, along with his deep sorrow as he remembers the days of Enoch.
- **An earnest, down-to-earth tone for Lamech:** Bring out clearly his worry over the youths pelting the orchards with stones and the gangs building a stone city.
- **Pause at the close:** Rest for a quiet moment after the words *"a new Enoch"* before moving on to Chapter 47.

---

### Chapter 47: Where Is the House of the Righteous?

Up in heaven, the Creator chose a mighty soul and spoke to him:

"Go down to the earth, where your name shall be Noah. Upon the earth, strive to lead humankind toward the Good. I will be with you."

Then God called the angel of birth and instructed him:

"Guide this soul into a home upon the earth where goodness still shines. This soul will renew My ways in the world below."

The angel of birth set out to find a home to place that soul within. Invisible to human eyes, the angel visited house after house. Yet wherever he went, only darkness, falsehood, deceit, and hardened hearts met him. The angel feared he would have to return to heaven and tell God the Father that upon the earth there was no home for a good soul.

While flying over the mountain country, however, the angel suddenly spied a house radiating peace and goodness. Stepping inside, he heard old Methuselah telling Lamech and his wife about the days of Enoch:

"Yes, that is how it used to be: every evening, upon many mountains, sacrificial fires blazed up. People turned toward God with grateful hearts for the blessings of fruit, grain, and water. In those days, every kernel of wheat was plump and sweet, not dry and hard as it is now. Mother Earth gladly gave good nourishment to humankind, because they walked upon the path of Light."

For a long while, the angel stood listening to old Methuselah. He saw how the old man's words brought light into Lamech's soul, and how Lamech's wife sat listening in quiet reverence. Then the angel of birth knew for certain: *"In this house, the soul of Noah can become a child."* The angel returned to heaven and reported all that he had discovered throughout his journey across the earth. Lamech's house was truly the house of the righteous.

#### Storytelling Notes
- **Light and dark contrast:** Drop your voice to a heavy, shadowed tone as the angel encounters deceit in the world, then open into a bright, warm glow when he enters Lamech's home.
- **Highlight the wheat image:** Clearly contrast the *"plump and sweet"* kernels of old with the *"dry and hard"* grain of today so nine- and ten-year-olds feel the link between gratitude and nature.

---

### Chapter 48: Noah's Birth

Not long afterward, as Lamech was returning from the fields one day, he saw a great flock of white doves wheeling around his house. The birds soared in wider and wider circles above the roof. The wondrous sight made Lamech stop in awe and watch their flight. A servant woman came hurrying out from the house toward the fields:

"Lamech, hurry home! Your wife has just given birth to a baby boy!"

As Lamech drew near the house, the gleaming white doves were still circling overhead. Stepping inside, he found the whole dwelling bathed in light. He gazed at his son and lifted the baby into his arms. Lamech knew that a mighty heavenly soul had descended to dwell within this child. Old Methuselah lifted his eyes toward the heavens and spoke:

"His name shall be Noah. Through him, the earth shall be saved from ruin."

#### Storytelling Notes
- **Wide, soaring rhythm:** Read smoothly, letting the image of the white doves circling ever wider above the roof linger gently.
- **Solemn prophecy:** Slow down and place clear emphasis on old Methuselah's naming of Noah at the close of the chapter.

---

### Chapter 49: The Child Noah's Anger

Noah grew up in the quiet house upon the hillside. Aged Methuselah often told him stories about the holy times of long ago. One day, the two of them sat in front of the hut looking down into the valley. Little Noah asked:

"Grandfather, look at that black smoke rising from the valley! It spreads across the land like a dark dragon."

Methuselah answered:

"That smoke rises from the wicked city, where people worship idols."

Noah asked:

"What is idol worship?"

Methuselah explained:

"Noah, my boy, you know how God created our beautiful world. We bring Him offerings of thanksgiving, just as the first people upon the earth used to do. But the evil spirits of darkness want to pull humankind away from God. At night while people sleep, the demons slip wicked thoughts into their hearts. Before long, people no longer want to serve God. Instead, they build statues in the likeness of evil spirits and bow down to worship them."

As old Methuselah finished speaking, a flash of anger flared in Noah's eyes. He clenched his fists and cried out:

"When I grow up, I will go into that city and smash those idols to pieces!"

One day, Noah was playing beside a stream. He was digging little channels in the meadow and guiding the water through them. While he played, a gang of rowdy youths came walking up from the direction of the city, carrying sacks stuffed with stolen fruit over their shoulders. They called coaxingly to the sheep grazing in the meadow where Noah was playing. When the trusting animals trotted over, the boys began pelting them with stones. Struck and hurting, the sheep bleated piteously and fled back to their shepherd, Noah. Setting their sacks down, the bullies marched toward Noah where he stood in the midst of his flock. They were full of spite and looking for cruel sport. Their leader raised a long stick and struck at the trembling animals.

Like a young lion, Noah sprang forward, seized the boy, and with extraordinary strength hurled him straight into the stream! Bawling and dripping wet, the boy scrambled back onto the bank. Two others rushed at Noah to grab him, but one after the other, both went flying into the water! Meanwhile, the rest of the cowardly gang snatched up their sacks and bolted.

Noah turned back to his sheep. His whole body was trembling. Tears welled up in his eyes. He threw himself upon the ground and sobbed bitterly. He—who could never bear to harm even a tiny beetle—had just fought with other youths! After a while, the sheep grew calm again and lay down huddled close around their gentle shepherd. The stream babbled on as before, and Noah's tears slowly dried.

That evening, Noah told Methuselah and Lamech what had happened during the day. Both of them were amazed to see that the boy had grown so strong. Methuselah told him:

"Do not fear the wicked folk from that city. Protect the weak. Stand brave against evil, and God will always be at your side."

#### Storytelling Notes
- **Shift Noah's emotion:** Use a strong, decisive voice when he vows to smash the idols and flings the bullies into the stream; then soften completely with a catch in your throat as he weeps beside his sheep.
- **Warm counsel at the end:** Read Methuselah's words slowly to emphasize courage walking hand in hand with gentleness.

---

### Chapter 50: The City of a Hundred Idols

Many years passed, and Noah grew to manhood. One day, old Methuselah said to Noah:

"Last night I dreamed that we ought to go into the city. Tomorrow, let the two of us visit the city of the idol worshipers together."

"Yes, that is a fine idea!" Noah replied. "Perhaps we can keep some of the people from doing evil!"

The next day, grandfather and grandson entered the city—a place neither of them had ever set foot in before. The gatekeeper greeted them with a sneer:

"Why do you two wear such solemn, pious faces? Can't you cheer up a little when you come into our city?"

As he spoke, he smirked and flung the dregs of his wine over them. Old Methuselah whispered softly:

"Stay calm, Noah. Now is not the time to confront them!"

They walked deeper into the city. Everywhere they looked, men and women stood loitering in idle clusters. Nobody was working. Men fought one another over stolen food. Children were throwing stones at an old man, while grown-ups stood around clapping and jeering whenever a stone struck him.

Noah could hold back his anger no longer. He wrapped his cloak around the old man to shield him and thundered:

"Cowards! Have you forgotten that God gave you hands to work and to pray?"

Mocking laughter answered him, yet Noah's bold words and fearless stance made the crowd draw back, and no more stones flew. Noah said to the old man:

"Come with me if you wish to leave this city!"

The old man replied:

"You have saved my life. They would have stoned me until I lay bleeding in the dust. They often do that for sport."

And the old man followed his protector.

Walking onward, they passed a house from which came the pitiful sound of children crying and moaning. Noah stepped inside and found four wretched children. The eldest told him:

"Our father and mother were killed in a brawl. We have no food, and tonight we must leave this hut. A little while ago two men came to drive us out. They threatened that if we are still here tonight, they will beat us and throw all four of us out into the street."

Noah said:

"Come with me, children. I will give you food and shelter."

He lifted the youngest into his arms, and the other three trailed close behind.

Before long, Noah and Methuselah reached a great square where a crowd stood packed in a ring. Even from afar, high flames could be seen leaping upward. Men with blackened faces were dancing around the blaze. Noah asked someone in the crowd what was happening. The man answered:

"We are burning alive those who dared speak against the king."

Noah asked:

"What did they say against the king?"

The man replied:

"One fellow said, 'The king ought to see to it that the people work. This idleness cannot go on forever.' And a bold woman dared to say that the king should care for the sick instead of staging war drills all day long! Anyone who criticizes the king is put to death."

Noah asked further:

"And who are those black-faced dancers?"

"They are the king's servants. Day and night they stand guard beside him to protect him and carry out his commands. Right now they are dancing around the stone idol—the statue everyone who comes here must worship."

Then old Methuselah whispered to Noah:

"Let us go home, my boy. Today we have seen more than enough evil."

Together with the four children and the old man, they left the city. Noah's heart was weighed down with sorrow at all they had witnessed. He said to Methuselah:

"I will never take a wife and raise children if their future must meet with corruption like this."

Noah did not return to the city again. The four children grew up to become faithful helpers in his household, and the old man became a gentle shepherd.

#### Storytelling Notes
- **Mark the three stops in the city clearly:** The mocking gate, the street corner where he rescues the old man and four orphans, and the burning square, keeping the pacing crisp and clear.
- **Restrained tone for Methuselah:** Speak softly both times the grandfather urges Noah to keep calm and turn homeward, balancing Noah's righteous anger.
- **Warm stillness at the close:** Soften your voice as you tell how the four children grew up loyal and true and the old man became a gentle shepherd.

---

### Chapter 51: The Command

One night, Noah woke at the sound of his name being called. Raising his head from his bed, he listened. Once more the voice called out:

"Noah, do you hear Me?"

Then Noah knew it was no human voice, but the voice of God calling him. He answered:

"Yes, Lord, I am listening."

The voice spoke on:

"Noah, the crimes and wickedness of humankind rise up to Me like a heavy, murky cloud of smoke. Say to all who will listen: 'Turn away from evil! Put an end to your wicked deeds, lest God send judgment to destroy you.' Carry this word to all humanity. Cry it aloud so they may hear!"

The next day, when Noah met old Methuselah, he told him of the revelation. How amazed he was when Methuselah said that he too had heard the very same words! Once again, grandfather and grandson made ready to set out for the villages and cities to fulfill the Lord's command and warn the people.

At this same time, at the Lord's command, Noah took a wife named Namoa. God spoke:

"From your family, good people shall fill the earth."

Namoa bore three sons, and they were named Shem, Ham, and Japheth.

#### Storytelling Notes
- **Sacred night atmosphere:** Lower your volume as the voice sounds in the night, keeping a deep, resonant solemnity for the words of God.
- **Warm shift at the end:** Let a gentle gladness enter your voice when naming Namoa and their three sons, Shem, Ham, and Japheth.

---

### Chapter 52: The King with the Black Crown

Noah and Methuselah went far and wide calling upon people to walk in God's ways, yet few would listen. They came a second time to the City of a Hundred Idols. This time, there were no guards at the city gate. Crowds were surging into the city that day to watch the king hold court. Once every week, the king held judgment to punish anyone who displeased him.

From afar, Noah saw the king seated in the square. Towering beside him stood a great idol. The king wore a black crown and a cloak as red as blood. A group of people lay prostrate before him upon the stone pavement, bowing their heads before king and idol until their foreheads touched the ground.

Suddenly, the king pointed his sword at the kneeling people. Executioners leaped forward and struck off their heads, and the heads rolled down to the foot of the throne. At that same moment, frenzied dancers pounded furiously upon drums, raising a spine-chilling din. Still more prisoners were dragged before the king.

Noah parted the crowd and strode straight toward the king with the black crown. He stood tall and fearless before the idol and raised a booming voice:

"Stop! Cease! O sinful people, what are you doing? I stand before you as a messenger of God to proclaim: Give up worshiping these demon faces! Cast off these evil ways! Kneel before the Lord—the One who guides the sun across the sky and bids the stars rise and set!"

He snatched a heavy mallet from one of the drummers and rained down mighty blows that shattered the stone idol, sending shards flying in every direction!

The king roared at his men to seize the blasphemer and put him to death. Like a pack of wild beasts, they charged at Noah. Calmly he lowered the mallet and stood completely still, gazing serenely ahead. His attackers reached out to grab him, only to yank their hands back with agonized shrieks. Noah was wrapped in a halo of radiant light whose brilliance and heat seared their hands the moment they touched him!

Pale as death, the king stepped down from his throne and leaped onto his black warhorse. In his frantic haste, the black crown tumbled from his head and rolled across the ground. Overcome with terror, he spurred his horse and fled.

Uproar broke out through the crowd. Many screamed and bolted from the square. Others stood rooted to the spot, waiting breathlessly to see how this drama would end. When the noise died down, Noah spoke again:

"Turn away from idol worship. Worship the Lord—the Creator who made the high heavens and the wide earth."

Yet their hearts had grown too hardened by evil to listen. They turned their backs and walked out of the square. Even so, not one of them dared come near Noah.

Only a small group took Noah's words to heart. They drew near to him and asked:

"Master, your words are good and true. What must we do?"

Noah answered:

"Follow me out of the city, for the Lord is soon going to destroy it."

So Methuselah and Noah left the city, followed by that small band in whose hearts a spark of goodness still glowed. Outside the city gate, a few turned back, not yet ready to leave behind their sinful way of life. The rest walked onward behind Noah and Methuselah.

#### Storytelling Notes
- **Dramatic climax:** Build a rapid, pounding rhythm through the drums and executions, then ring out boldly with Noah's cry of *"Stop!"* and the smashing of the idol.
- **Calm power:** Drop into a remarkably quiet, composed tone as Noah lowers the mallet inside his shining halo, contrasting sharply with the panic of the king dropping his crown.

---

### Chapter 53: Raphael Guides Noah to the Book of Life

After that journey into the city, Noah's soul was weighed down with heavy care. Then the Archangel Raphael appeared before him and said:

"I have come to you at the Lord's command to help heal the earth."

The angel helped Noah understand the laws of life and the meaning of death, and taught him to discern between good spirits and evil spirits. Raphael guided Noah to the cave where the Sacred Book—the Book of Life—was kept, the very book that Adam had once passed down to Seth, and Seth to Enoch. Now the book was opened to Noah.

What did he discover within the wisdom of that Sacred Book? Noah learned how to heal many sicknesses. He learned to fathom the powers of the sun, the moon, and all the stars. He could gaze clearly through every event of the earth's past, present, and future.

#### Storytelling Notes
- **Deep, mystical stillness:** Read slowly and lucidly through the sacred lineage *Adam – Seth – Enoch – Noah*.
- **Pace the three layers of wisdom:** Give clear weight to the three gifts Noah learned—healing sickness, reading the sun, moon, and stars, and seeing past, present, and future.

---

### Chapter 54: The Command to Build the Ark

When Noah's soul was steeped in the holiness of the book, the Lord Himself spoke to him:

"Noah, I have decided to destroy the sinful race of humankind upon the earth! Great rains will pour down, and floodwaters will rise. For forty days and forty nights, the waters above the heavens and from the deep abyss below will burst open and stream forth. Water will cover the earth and the mountains of the old world. But you, Noah, together with your family, will be saved and brought into a new world. Build a great ark of cedar wood. Divide it into many chambers inside, and seal every seam tight with pitch. High up, make a window, and in one side, make a door. The ark must have three decks: a bottom deck, a middle deck, and an upper deck. Bring every kind of living creature on earth into the ark, a pair of each kind. Take plenty of food with you. When the time comes, go inside the ark with your three sons and your household, so that you may be saved alive for the new world."

When the Lord had finished speaking, Noah called his three sons, Shem, Ham, and Japheth, and told them what the Lord had commanded. The three brothers were filled with joy at being able to serve the Lord by building the Ark.

Together with all the men of the household, they climbed a nearby mountain called Cardinon. There they felled the giant cedar trees growing on the mountainside. Just as they had fitted together the first heavy beams and planks, old Methuselah passed away. Before his soul left his body, he spoke:

"Now I can close my eyes and depart in peace and comfort, for I have seen the building of a Temple for a new and better world."

By *"a Temple,"* he meant the Ark, which was being built to the exact measurements the Lord had given Noah.

#### Storytelling Notes
- **Clear, majestic voice for the Lord:** Speak every detail of the Ark's design distinctly, as though sacred blueprints were being placed straight into the hands of the master builder.
- **Serene farewell:** Slow down and soften into a warm tone on Methuselah's final words when he calls the Ark *"a Temple."*

---

### Chapter 55: Shem, Ham, Japheth, and the Animals

Before the command came to build the Ark, Shem used to follow the birds, for he loved them dearly. He found eagles' nests high up on rocky mountain cliffs and watched how the young eaglets learned to fly. One day he brought a baby eagle home and tamed it. When it grew up, after every soaring flight over the mountains, the eagle always returned to Shem's side. Shem learned the whistle and call of every bird in the forest. He could summon cuckoos, woodpeckers, and countless songbirds to come near. They pecked seeds right out of his hand. A wild wood pigeon often perched upon his shoulder, too.

Ham, on the other hand, was a daring young man of extraordinary strength. He tamed wild beasts, and above all, the lions. One day, as he was walking through a thicket, a lion sprang out to pounce on him. Quick as lightning, Ham dodged aside, and the lion crashed headlong into a tree trunk! Ham leaped onto the lion's back, pressed his knees firmly against it, and gripped its neck tightly. He squeezed the beast's neck so hard that it had to open its jaws and gasp for breath. Little by little, he loosened his grip to let the lion breathe. The lion grew so gentle that it followed at Ham's heels like a faithful dog. From that day on, wherever Ham went, the lion was always at his side.

Youngest brother Japheth loved the life of a shepherd and herdsman. He drove the sheep and cattle out to pasture and tended them with care. He tamed a mighty bull that pulled his plow and turned the soil across the fields.

#### Storytelling Notes
- **Three tones for the three brothers:** Use a light, clear voice for Shem calling the birds; a swift, forceful pace as Ham subdues the lion; and a gentle, steady rhythm as Japheth plows the fields with his bull.
- **Bond between humans and animals:** Highlight the natural trust between the three brothers and the animals before the day they board the Ark.

---

### Chapter 56: Will the Ark Be Destroyed?

The people of the City of a Hundred Idols noticed that something strange was happening up on Mount Cardinon. Yet no one knew what was being built there. Some mocked:

"Noah is building a giant coffin for himself! He and his whole family mean to bury themselves alive in it!"

Five men plotted together:

"Whatever it is, burning that huge wooden box to ashes would be a devilish bit of fun."

One evening, the reckless band crept up the mountain path to carry out their plan. Their ringleader carried an earthen pot filled with glowing coals. The others hauled bundles of dry kindling and straw. Little did they know that every night Ham left his lion sleeping beneath the Ark to stand guard—for Noah feared that wicked men from the city might try to destroy their work. Neither did they know that Shem's eagle chose the highest beam atop the Ark as its roost each night.

Through the still night, the five dark figures crawled stealthily toward the Ark. Every now and then they paused to listen. Not a sound stirred. Both the lion and the eagle were fast asleep. Inside the Ark, Shem, Ham, and Japheth lay sleeping on beds of straw alongside the workmen. The five men crouched behind a thicket. Leaves rustled softly. A thin wisp of smoke rose from the pot of glowing embers and drifted on the gentle breeze toward the Ark.

Half awake, the lion brushed its nose with a paw and sneezed. Only a few paces away, the pile of embers was about to be dumped beneath the dry timber. The ringleader had already lifted glowing coals out of the pot. A bundle of straw burst into flame!

Suddenly a thunderous roar shattered the night! The lion leaped straight through the flames and pinned the arsonist down. The others bolted in terror. From the highest beam, the eagle swooped into the air with a piercing shriek and chased after the fleeing men. It clawed at their faces and pecked at their shoulders until they screamed in pain, convinced that some dreadful monster was hunting them down.

Inside the Ark, Shem, Ham, and Japheth woke with a start at the alarm. They heard the commotion and smelled smoke. Looking beneath the Ark, they spotted the burning coals and quickly shoveled sand over them to put out the fire. Then they saw the lion holding its heavy forepaw pressed firmly upon the chest of the wicked ringleader. When Noah arrived, he stroked the lion and gave thanks to the Lord that the Ark was unharmed. From that night onward, the three brothers took turns keeping watch over the Ark through the night.

#### Storytelling Notes
- **Build suspense in the quiet night:** Lower your voice and slow the pacing as the five dark shadows creep closer, the thin wisp of smoke drifts on the breeze, and the lion sneezes.
- **Burst into action, then soften:** Let your voice surge with the lion's roar and the eagle's shriek, then shift to a warm, grateful tone as Noah strokes the lion and thanks the Lord.

---

### Chapter 57: Gathering the Animals

When the Ark was finished and every seam was sealed tight with pitch, Noah said to his youngest son:

"Japheth, my son, you are the friend of the great beasts, of the cows, the bulls, and every creature that walks or creeps upon the earth. Go and bring back one pair of every kind. Build a wide enclosure with a sturdy fence and gather them inside it. Soon we will lead them into the Ark, into the bottom deck."

Japheth asked:

"How will I know which animals to choose?"

Noah replied:

"God has given me a sign that the angels will help us choose and guide the animals. As you draw near, whichever creature gently lies down before you, bring that one along."

So Japheth set out to gather the great creatures of the earth.

To Shem, Noah said:

"You are the friend of the eagle and all the birds. Call one pair of every kind of bird to come here. They can perch in the treetops of the nearby woods until the time comes for us to let them fly into the uppermost chambers of the Ark."

Shem asked:

"Father, what will give us light inside the Ark once all the windows must be closed tight?"

Noah answered:

"Go up into the mountains. Inside a rocky cave, you will find the wondrous, radiant jewel Anoa; that precious stone will shine and give us light inside the Ark."

Shem turned toward the mountains, softly whistling to summon the birds as he walked. One afternoon, he came upon a rocky cave glowing with a mysterious light. There he found the precious radiant jewel Anoa, which gleamed in his hands like a block of crystal. As he made his way back down into the valley, great flocks of birds circled overhead and followed him. Whenever he sat down to rest, the birds settled in the treetops; whenever he walked on, they rose into the air again and flew toward the Ark. Only the wild hens and the partridges clucked and waddled along the ground in a line behind him. In this way, Shem reached the woods beside the Ark. All the birds perched among the branches, whistling and singing the whole day through. Not one large bird harmed a smaller one; they all sat happily singing together on the boughs, just as in the days of old in the Garden of Paradise.

#### Storytelling Notes
- **Clear rhythm of instruction:** Keep a calm, steady voice for Noah's directions to Japheth and Shem, then drop into a touch of mystery when Shem discovers the radiant jewel Anoa.
- **Joyful ending:** Bring lively warmth to the image of the wild hens and partridges waddling behind him and the flocks singing together in peace.

---

### Chapter 58: Entering the Ark

Now the Ark stood complete, and every crack was sealed tight. Shem, Ham, and Japheth came to ask Noah:

"Father, how should we bring the animals into the Ark, now that everything is ready?"

Noah gave his instructions:

"First, carry the provisions aboard. Even though the animals will sleep through most of the long voyage, they will still need food. Then Japheth will lead the heaviest creatures down into the bottom deck. Ham will bring his animals into the middle deck. When all of them are inside, open the roof window for Shem so the birds can fly in."

Noah's sons and the household set to work carrying abundant food aboard—first grain, figs, ivy leaves, and all kinds of fruit. Noah himself brought along grapevines, fig trees, and olive trees to plant in the new world.

When that was done, it was Japheth's turn to lead the animals aboard. Broad beams were laid down to form a wooden gangplank. First Japheth led in the bulls, the cows, and the livestock. He chose only young elephants, young wild oxen, and young giraffes, for the full-grown ones were far too large to fit through the Ark's door.

Soon the gangplank was alive with twisting, wriggling motion as reptiles—snakes, salamanders, and lizards—slithered into the Ark. And the strangest wonder was that not a single creature harmed another! They lived side by side in gentle peace, just as in the Garden of Paradise. The snake forgot it had venomous fangs. Even the porcupines, large and small, tried their best to keep their quills lying sleek and flat. Soon the bottom deck was full; the animals lay down and quickly fell into a deep sleep.

Next, Ham led the lions, panthers, leopards, wildcats, and all the wild forest beasts into the Ark. They too lay down quietly on the middle deck.

Then Noah opened the roof window, and Shem whistled to call the birds. They flew in two by two, exactly as he called each pair. Had they all rushed in at once, they would have crowded and crushed one another and broken their wings!

As the sun set, Noah said:

"Close up the Ark for today. Tomorrow, you must come with me on one last journey. I have received a command to carry Adam's casket into the new world."

Just as Noah was about to pull the door shut, something fluttered around his head. Two bats squeaked in fright, begging to come inside. Noah chuckled:

"Aha! You don't quite know whether you belong to the mice or the birds, do you? Come on in!"

Scarcely had he spoken when two night owls appeared. Noah nodded knowingly:

"Hmm, you're afraid the other birds will peck your feathers out if you sit beside them, aren't you? Hop up onto my shoulders."

Happily, the two owls perched one on each of Noah's shoulders, while the two bats clung tightly to his arms.

He climbed up to the bird deck. Most of the birds were already fast asleep. He tucked his nighttime guests into a quiet corner.

"At last we can close the door!" Noah said.

But look! A cluster of snails had crept up the doorframe and were clinging right to it. They had only just arrived! Noah gently carried them down to the bottom deck and then locked the door.

#### Storytelling Notes
- **Busy yet peaceful rhythm:** Keep a lively pace while loading provisions and leading the beasts up the gangplank, then slow gently when the snake forgets its venom and the porcupines flatten their quills.
- **Gentle humor at the close:** Let a playful warmth shine through as Noah chats with the bats and night owls on his shoulders, followed by mild surprise at finding the snails on the doorframe.

### Chapter 59: Adam's Casket

For the very last time before the flood, Noah and his sons left the Ark and walked to the rocky cave where Adam's body had been laid to rest.

Guided by an angel, they found the place—a hidden cavern deep beneath the earth. With hearts full of awe and reverence, the three brothers lifted the casket onto their shoulders and carried it back to the Ark, just as their father directed. Upon the casket itself they set the precious radiant jewel Anoa, so that its gentle glow spread through the dim shadows inside the Ark.

When everything had been settled safely inside the Ark, Noah knelt down and prayed to God:

"Lord, we have finished the work just as You commanded us. Please be with us according to Your holy will."

#### Storytelling Notes
- **Solemn, hushed atmosphere:** Read slowly and lower your voice to evoke the stillness of stepping into a deep subterranean cave.
- **Light and prayer:** Give a soft, lingering emphasis to *"its gentle glow,"* then pause for a quiet beat before Noah's prayer of trust.

### Chapter 60: The Ark Is Sealed and the Rain Begins to Fall

All those who had helped build the Ark stepped inside: Noah's sons, their wives, and their entire households.

Standing on the threshold, Noah gazed out to bid the old world one last farewell, as though still hoping someone else might come running in time. His eyes swept all along the horizon, yet not a single human figure appeared. Slowly, he pulled the heavy wooden door shut.

Before long, a white cloud drifted close to the Ark. Out of the cloud a hand reached forth and traced a seal upon the door, locking it fast until another sign should be given.

#### Storytelling Notes
- **Gaze of farewell:** Slow your pace as Noah scans the horizon and gently shuts the door, conveying a quiet sorrow.
- **Sacred seal:** Speak softly and reverently as the white cloud approaches and the hand traces its seal upon the wooden door.

### Chapter 61: The Great Flood

For days, black clouds had been rolling inland. Strange, eerie thunder rumbled near and far. Suddenly the rain burst down in torrents, sending people scrambling for shelter inside their houses, in rocky caves, and beneath the trees. They cursed fiercely, shaking their fists at the sky and roaring:

"Do you mean to drown us all down here?"

Yet the rain poured without ceasing through the night, and when water began flooding into every house and hut, sheer terror fell over everyone. At first they climbed onto the rooftops of their homes and up into the treetops. Many frantically lashed beams and planks together to build small rafts.

Still the rain streamed down, and the waters rose higher and higher. Amid flashes of lightning and booming thunder, desperate wails echoed on every side. Whoever could escape scrambled hurriedly toward the mountains. Hungry crowds huddled there together, among them the King of Sin and his followers.

When the rain slackened for a brief moment and a shaft of sunlight broke through the clouds, they saw the Ark drifting past. Driven by the wind, the Ark glided close to the sinking City of a Hundred Idols. Loud cries rang out:

"Noah, Noah, save us! We will listen to you now. Save us!"

But their repentance had come too late. The fierce wind swept away all their cries and pleas.

A few strong swimmers managed to reach the Ark. Clinging tightly to the wooden seams, they pounded their fists against the hull and shrieked:

"Noah, open the door!"

Yet their voices could not pierce the thick timber walls, and their pounding echoed within only like the soft, muffled footsteps of the animals moving across the wooden floor.

In that hour, Noah gathered his family together to offer a prayer of thanksgiving. With a choked voice he said:

"My heart is heavy and my eyes are full of tears for the terrible judgment that has fallen upon sinful humankind. May God have mercy upon their souls, since He had to destroy their bodies."

The next day a mighty tempest arose, and the Ark was tossed about by towering flood waves. A violent earthquake shook the old world. Mountains and dry land collapsed. Wooden rafts shattered, hurling those clinging to them into the churning waters. Upon the mountains, the remaining survivors huddled close together; many in despair threw themselves straight into the sea.

Upon the peak of the highest mountain sat the King of Sin wrapped in a tiger skin, gripping a sword in his right hand. Before him stood his followers—the driving rain had washed away the black soot marks from their faces. As the waters climbed to the very summit, those wicked men shoved back into the flood anyone else who tried to scramble up. Frantically they hauled stones and piled them higher, hoping to raise the mountaintop just a little further.

Night fell, and the king's guards crouched around his feet. He barked at them to jump into the water and drove them back with his sword until one after another vanished into the surging flood. Yet the very last guard fought back and wrestled with the king to the bitter end. He wrenched the bloodstained sword away and flung it into the waves; a moment later, the black crown fell in after it. Still the two struggled as the water rose up to their knees. All at once, locked tightly together, they lost their footing and slipped silently beneath the rolling waves. The last human bodies of the old world sank deep to the bottom of the ocean.

#### Storytelling Notes
- **Build with the rising flood:** Let the tension swell from the first downpour and the defiant shouts to the late, desperate cries for rescue.
- **Hushed compassion:** Lower your voice when the pounding on the hull sounds *"like the soft footsteps of the animals,"* and speak Noah's prayer with tender grief.
- **Tragic, solemn close:** Give clear weight to the bloodstained sword and the black crown falling into the sea, then pause in deep silence.

### Chapter 62: Distress in the Ark

While the earth quaked beneath the raging storm and gigantic waves slammed across the sea, Noah and his family also endured great distress inside the Ark.

People and animals lost their footing and were flung hard against the wooden walls. All the creatures woke up. Frightened groans and whimpers echoed throughout the Ark. Only the snails, the moles, and the field mice slept soundly on!

The pair of giraffes were terribly miserable because their heads kept bumping thump-thump against the ceiling. Japheth sawed a hole in the ceiling so they could poke their heads up into the middle deck. When Ham was walking across that second deck in the dark, he bumped straight into a head and a neck with no legs anywhere below them! Only after a moment did he realize it was the heads of the two young giraffes poking up into his deck.

Shem, Ham, and Japheth had their hands full calming the animals. The snakes were terrified when first a cow and then a donkey stepped right on top of them. Japheth gathered the snakes into a corner and told them to curl up neatly together.

When Ham went over to the lion, it was growling because the wolf lay right beside it. Ham stroked the lion's mane to soothe it, and gave the wolf's ear a firm tweak when it bared its fangs. At that, both beasts settled down.

The loudest uproar of all, though, came from overhead. Clucking, quacking, whistling, and shrill chirping filled the bird deck. Shem scattered grain and seeds for them to eat; busy pecking up the seeds, the birds soon forgot all their fear.

Coming to the ducks, Shem saw that all through the storm they had stayed quietly huddled in a corner. He stroked their smooth feathers and spoke gently to them. The ducks thought to themselves:

*"The other birds always want to peck our feathers. Human beings stroke us. How kind human beings are!"*

Once the storm passed, the three brothers fed all the animals. Before long, every creature sank into a deep slumber and did not wake again until they set foot in the new world.

#### Storytelling Notes
- **Shift to a warm, playful tone:** Add a mischievous sparkle when mentioning the snails, moles, and field mice sleeping soundly right through the storm.
- **Comic timing:** Pause in surprise when Ham bumps into *"a head and a neck with no legs anywhere below them,"* then use a sweet, innocent voice for the ducks' quiet thought.

### Chapter 63: The Raven's Flight and the Dove's Message

A steady wind carried the Ark eastward for more than forty days. One day, Shem climbed down from the upper deck to the middle deck and said to Noah:

"Father, how quiet it is! The rain has stopped falling. Surely the great downpour is over!"

Noah climbed to the top deck and listened. There was no sound of rain anymore. The skies had truly cleared! Noah said:

"Bring me the ladder. I will open the window and look outside."

He could see nothing but water—endless water and murky gray mist. Shem said:

"Father, let a raven fly out. It can travel far and look all around. Perhaps it will bring us back news."

Noah released the raven. It circled near and far above the waters, then flew straight off into the distant haze and never returned. It was the very first creature to find the new world!

Next, Noah sent out a dove to search for dry land. A few hours later the dove came back, for she could find nowhere to rest her feet. Noah latched the window and said:

"We must wait patiently for the Lord's command. He will give us a sign."

Seven days later, he sent the dove out once more. Toward evening, the dove returned. Look, see! In her beak she carried a freshly plucked olive branch! Then Noah knew the waters had drained from the earth. Yet where had the little bird found that branch? She had flown all the way to the Mount of Olives, where in days to come the city of Jerusalem would be built and so many great events would unfold.

Seven days later still, Noah sent out another dove. This time, the dove did not come back again. Then he knew for certain that the moment to behold the new world was near, yet he waited quietly for a sign from God.

#### Storytelling Notes
- **Rhythm of waiting:** Brighten your voice when Shem announces the rain has stopped, slow down into vast stillness when gazing out over the misty waters, and speak Noah's call to patience with calm assurance.
- **Wonder of the olive branch:** Exclaim softly on *"Look, see!"* and adopt a reverent tone for the mention of the Mount of Olives and future Jerusalem.

### Chapter 64: The New World

One day, a jolt shook the Ark, as though it had touched upon something solid and firm. Yet Noah did not open the door right away, for he knew the ground must dry before he could let the animals out. Soon afterward, in the night, God spoke to Noah:

"The time has come. Leave the Ark and bring all the living creatures out with you."

The next morning Noah swung the great door wide open. The whole green, fresh new world stretched out before his eyes! He told Shem to set the birds free. Shem opened the window and released the eagle first. What a magnificent sight it was as the noble bird soared high into the sunlit blue sky! Then thousands of bright-winged birds of every color fluttered up into the air behind it.

Ham let out the beasts of the middle deck. The lion and its family sprang bounding into the open air, bursting with joy. Because the animals were still gentle and tame, the peace of the Garden of Paradise lingered upon the earth for quite a long time.

Last of all, Japheth led out the bulls and the great beasts. The snakes glided away into the bushes. The mice scurried straight into the nearest burrows. And the snails, naturally, came out dead last—in fact, a few of them clung stubbornly to the Ark for several more days before finally making their way down!

Noah walked through the entire Ark to make sure every last creature had left. Up on the top deck, he found the two night owls and the two bats still tucked inside a dark corner. Knowing they wanted to wait until nightfall before flying out, he smiled and left them to rest in peace.

#### Storytelling Notes
- **Moment of opening the door:** Pause briefly before *"The whole green, fresh new world..."*, then open your voice into radiant wonder as the eagle soars into the blue sky.
- **Playful touch:** Slow down with a gentle chuckle for the snails clinging on for days and the owls and bats lingering in their dark corner.

### Chapter 65: The Offering of Thanksgiving

Together with his sons, Noah built a stone altar right beside the Ark. There was not a single person in the whole large family who did not joyfully help carry stones to raise the very first altar of the new world.

The first fire was kindled—a flame of thanksgiving. As everyone knelt around the altar thanking God for saving them from the flood, a glorious rainbow arched across the sky.

Deep within his heart, Noah heard the voice of God speaking:

"I accept your offering. From this day forward, I will never again send a great flood upon the earth. Live according to My commandments. That rainbow in the sky is the sign of the covenant between us."

Hearing those words echo in his heart, Noah solemnly proclaimed them aloud beside the altar. Shem spoke:

"Let me be a priest of the Lord, so that the offerings may always be kept sacred in the new world."

#### Storytelling Notes
- **Joyful yet solemn:** Sound bright and eager while the family carries the stones, then settle into warm reverence as the flame of thanksgiving burns beneath the rainbow.
- **Words of the covenant:** Speak every line of God's promise and Shem's steadfast vow clearly and unhurriedly.

### Chapter 66: The Devil in Noah's Vineyard

Upon the slopes of Mount Ararat, Noah planted the trees he had brought from the old world: fig trees, olive trees, and almond trees. He also planted grapevines to start a little vineyard.

While he was planting the vines, Satan crept up and asked:

"What are you sticking into the ground there?"

Noah replied:

"I am planting a vineyard."

The devil asked again:

"And what on earth will grow out of those dry little sticks?"

Noah answered:

"Grapes are very sweet, both fresh and dried. From their plump fruit, people press a juice that gladdens the human heart."

The devil slunk quietly away. A few days later, while Noah was away, he slipped back bringing a lamb, a lion, a monkey, and a pig. He slaughtered the lamb so its blood poured over the roots of the vines, and then one after another he killed the lion, the monkey, and the pig, soaking the vineyard soil deep with their blood.

The devil curled his lip in a sly sneer:

"When human beings drink the first cup of wine, they will be as gentle as a little lamb. The second cup will make them feel as mighty as a lion, strutting and boasting: 'I am king: no one can match me!' By the third cup, they will grow drunk and stagger about, playing foolish tricks like monkeys, no longer knowing what they are doing. And at last, if they drink still more, they will spill wine all over their clothes and wallow on the floor like pigs!"

With a harsh chuckle, he muttered to himself:

"So I have managed to smuggle into the new world something strong enough to drag humankind back onto my path after all."

#### Storytelling Notes
- **Contrast the two voices:** Give Noah a simple, honest warmth when he speaks of the grapes, and give Satan a low, raspy, slyly probing tone.
- **Progression of the four cups:** Shift from mildness (*lamb*) to boastful pride (*'I am king...'*), foolishness (*monkey*), and squalor (*pig*), letting the lesson of self-control linger lightly.

### Chapter 67: Shem and the Angels

Noah remained living inside the Ark, while his sons and their families built houses at the foot of Mount Ararat. One day Shem came to Noah and said:

"Father, an angel of God has told me to lift Adam's casket onto my back and follow wherever he leads. Adam must have a grave in the new world, too."

Noah replied:

"Fulfill everything the angel has commanded you. I will stay here and make the offerings."

So Shem hoisted Adam's casket onto his broad, strong shoulders and walked down from the slopes of Mount Ararat into the valley.

Walking beside him, the angel guided Shem across a long journey all the way to a hill that in later times would be called Golgotha. When Shem arrived there, the earth opened of its own accord before him. He lowered Adam's body into the ground. As soon as the earth had received the body of the first father of humankind, the ground closed tightly back together just as before.

Then the angel spoke to Shem:

"Through Adam, death came to humankind. In days to come, when humanity turns wicked once more, God will send His Son as the Savior. From this very spot of earth, He will bring Life and Light to the world."

Shem built a small hut nearby, and from that day forward, for the rest of his life, he served the great mystery that the angel had revealed to him beside Adam's grave.

#### Storytelling Notes
- **Pilgrim's cadence:** Keep a steady, unhurried, quiet pace matching Shem's footsteps as he carries the casket alongside the angel to the hill of Golgotha.
- **Closing cadence of Book I:** Pause gently before the final revelation, and give a warm, luminous clarity to *"Life and Light"* as a bridge opening toward what comes next.
