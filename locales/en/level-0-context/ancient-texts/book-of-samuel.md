# Books of Samuel

---

## Chapter I: The Call at the Temple of Shiloh and the First King

**1.** In the hill country of Ramah lived a boy named **Samuel**, dedicated by his mother to serve at the Temple of Shiloh under the guidance of the aged priest Eli. In those days, sacred revelations were rare; one night before the lamp in the sanctuary had gone out, young Samuel lay sleeping when he heard a voice calling his name: *"Samuel! Samuel!"* He ran quickly to Eli's chamber and answered: *"Here I am; did you call me?"* Eli told him he had not called and bade him go back to sleep.

**2.** The call echoed a second time and then a third; at the third time, Eli understood that it was a call from heaven, and instructed the boy that if he heard his name again, he should answer: *"Speak, for Your servant is listening."* Surely enough, the voice called once more, and from that night onward young Samuel grew into a faithful prophet, none of whose words ever fell to the ground.

**3.** When Samuel grew old, all the people gathered and asked that a king be appointed to rule over them like the nations around them. The Most High directed Samuel to a handsome young man of the tribe of Benjamin named **Saul**, taller than anyone else from the shoulders upward. Beneath the morning star outside the city gate, Samuel poured a flask of fragrant oil upon Saul's head, marking him as the first king chosen to shield the nation from its foes.

---

## Chapter II: The Shepherd Boy David and the Harp in the King's Palace

**1.** In the early years of his reign, King Saul won many great victories, yet triumph gradually filled the king with pride, leading him to follow his own will rather than keep to the upright path. Therefore the aged prophet Samuel left the palace in sorrow and quietly took his horn of oil to the small village of **Bethlehem** to visit the household of Jesse.

**2.** Jesse brought seven tall, handsome sons one after another before Samuel, yet the prophet shook his head at each, looking not upon outward stature but into the depths of the heart. Asking whether any son remained, Samuel heard from Jesse that only the youngest, **David**, was out in the pastures tending the sheep. When the ruddy-cheeked boy with bright, keen eyes came in from the fields, an inner prompting spoke: *"This is the very one!"* Samuel took the horn of oil and anointed David in the midst of his brothers.

**3.** Not long afterward, King Saul's heart became troubled by a heavy melancholy that left him irritable and sleepless. His attendants summoned young David—known alike for bravely guarding his flocks against lions and bears and for his gifted playing upon the harp—to serve at court. Whenever darkness settled over the king's mind, David took up his harp and gently played melodies of serene peace; at once Saul's soul grew calm and the gloom departed.

---

## Chapter III: The Smooth Pebble and the Giant Goliath

**1.** The Philistine army marched out to invade the land, drawing up their battle lines on a hillside facing King Saul's troops across a valley. Out from the enemy ranks strode a giant champion from Gath named **Goliath**, standing six cubits and a span tall, wearing a bronze helmet, clad in heavy scale armor, and bearing an iron-tipped spear heavy as a weaver's beam. Morning and evening for forty days, he stood in the valley roaring his challenge for one man of Israel to step out in single combat, striking terror into the entire army so that none dared answer.

**2.** Early one morning, young David was sent by his father with parched grain and cheese to check on his older brothers at the camp. Hearing Goliath's arrogant taunt, David felt no fear and asked King Saul's leave to go out and fight him, telling how while keeping sheep in the fields he had rescued lambs bare-handed from the jaws of lions and bears.

**3.** Saul tried dressing the boy in his own bronze armor, iron helmet, and belted sword, yet after taking a few steps David felt weighed down and encumbered, and asked to lay them all aside. Carrying only his familiar shepherd's staff, he walked down to the brook, chose **five smooth pebbles** for his shepherd's pouch, and with his **leather sling** in hand stepped forward into the valley.

**4.** Seeing an unarmed youth advancing toward him, the giant Goliath burst into scornful laughter and curses. David answered clearly and boldly: *"You come to me with sword and spear and javelin, but I come to you in the name of the living Most High!"* As Goliath charged furiously toward him, David ran swiftly to meet him, reached into his bag, drew out a single pebble, fitted it into his sling, whirled it, and let it fly. The stone struck straight into the giant's forehead and sank into his brow, and Goliath crashed face down motionless upon the earth. Seeing their champion fallen, the entire Philistine army fled in panic.

## Chapter IV: Friendship with Jonathan and Mercy in the Cave of Engedi

**1.** After that victory, the soul of Prince **Jonathan**—son of King Saul—was bound at once to the soul of David as closer than brothers; Jonathan took off his own royal robe, belt, and sword and gave them to his friend. Yet when King Saul heard the people singing louder praise for David's deeds than for his own, he fell into a mad jealousy, hurling his spear to kill David and leading troops to hunt him across mountains and wilderness.

**2.** Throughout those bitter months as a fugitive among rocky crags and deserts, David twice held King Saul's life within his grasp, yet steadfastly refused to strike. The first time, among the rocky cliffs of **Engedi**, while David and his companions lay hidden deep inside a vast cave, King Saul entered that very cave alone to rest, unaware of their presence. David's men urged him to strike down his enemy, yet David crept softly forward and silently cut off only a corner of the king's robe.

**3.** When Saul left the cave and had walked some distance away, David called out from the cave mouth, bowed low, and held up the piece of cloth for the king to see: *"Look, my lord king, upon the corner of your robe in my hand! I could have slain you, yet I would not lift my hand against the anointed one."* Hearing this, King Saul wept aloud, acknowledging David's righteousness to be far nobler than his own. Still, jealousy flared again in Saul's heart until the fierce battle on **Mount Gilboa**, where both King Saul and Prince Jonathan fell in combat. Hearing the tragic tidings, David tore his garments in grief and sang an immortal lament mourning the first king and his dearest friend, whose love had surpassed every earthly bond.

---

## Chapter V: Bringing the Ark to Jerusalem and the Promise of a Kingdom

**1.** When all the tribes united to acclaim **David** king over the whole land, he marched to the stronghold of **Zion**—the capital city of **Jerusalem**—and made it the heart of the kingdom. Soon afterward, King David held a joyous procession to bring the **Ark of the Covenant** out of obscurity into the royal city, amid blasts of trumpets, ringing harps, and the king himself dancing with wholehearted gladness before all the people.

**2.** Dwelling in a warm palace paneled with cedar, King David felt troubled that while he lived in splendor, the Ark of the Covenant still rested beneath tent curtains, and longed to build a magnificent Temple of stone and gold. Yet a prophetic word counseled him that because David's hands had fought in many bloody wars, the peaceful Temple would be built instead by the son who should succeed him.

**3.** In return, the Most High made an enduring promise to David: the throne and lineage of David's house would stand firm across generations, kindling hope for a kingdom of lasting justice and peace for all peoples. In his closing years, David gathered vast stores of gold, silver, cedar timber, cut stone, and the plans for the Temple, handing them to his son with a solemn charge to walk in integrity of heart.
