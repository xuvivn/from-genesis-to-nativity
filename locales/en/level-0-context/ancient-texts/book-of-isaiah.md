# Book of the Prophet Isaiah

---

## Chapter I: The Live Coal Touching the Lips in the Temple

**1.** In the year King Uzziah died, the prophet **Isaiah** beheld a majestic vision inside the Temple: the Most High sat enthroned high and lifted up, the train of His radiant robe filling the entire sanctuary. Above the throne stood six-winged **Seraphim**—with two wings covering their faces, two covering their feet, and two with which they flew—calling out to one another in echoing praise that shook the doorposts to their foundations while the Temple filled with incense smoke.

**2.** Beholding that holy splendor, Isaiah cried out in dismay: *"Woe is me! For I am a man of unclean lips, dwelling among a people of unclean lips, yet my eyes have looked upon the Most High King!"*

**3.** Immediately one of the Seraphim flew to him, holding in tongs **a glowing live coal** taken from the altar, and gently touched it to Isaiah's lips, saying: *"Behold, this burning coal has touched your lips; your guilt is taken away and your shortcomings are cleansed pure!"* Directly afterward, hearing the question: *"Whom shall I send, and who will go for Us?"*, Isaiah answered steadily: *"Here am I; send me!"*

---

## Chapter II: The Great Light and the Child Emmanuel

**1.** In an age when the nation stood threatened by war and turmoil and the royal court trembled before the northern empires, Isaiah was entrusted with bringing a timeless sign of peace to all who kept steadfast faith.

**2.** He lifted his voice to proclaim a resonant prophecy: *"The Most High Himself shall give you a sign: Behold, a maiden shall conceive and bear a son, and shall call his name **Emmanuel**—meaning The Most High is with us."*

**3.** Looking out toward the darkened northern land of Galilee that had endured deep sorrow, his song of hope broke forth like dawn: *"The people who walked in darkness have beheld a brilliant light; upon those who dwelt in a land of deep shadow, light has shone brightly! For unto us a child is born, unto us a son is given; authority shall rest upon his shoulder, and his name shall be called Wonderful Counselor, Mighty One, Everlasting Father, **Prince of Peace**."*

---

## Chapter III: A Shoot Rising from the Stump of Jesse

**1.** After fierce storms felled the proud cedars until only humble stumps remained resting quietly in the earth, a new hope sprouted green shoots directly from that lowly root.

**2.** The prophetic song painted a portrait of the world restored to primal harmony: *"A shoot shall spring forth from the stump of **Jesse**, and a branch out of his roots shall bloom and bear fruit. The Spirit of wisdom, understanding, counsel, and upright reverence shall rest upon him. He shall not judge by outward appearances nor decide by hearsay, but shall judge the poor with true righteousness."*

**3.** *"Then **the wolf shall dwell with the lamb**, and the leopard shall lie down beside the young goat; the calf and the young lion shall graze together in one meadow, **and a little child shall lead them**! The lion shall eat straw like the ox; the nursing infant shall play beside the cobra's den and come to no harm. Nowhere on all the holy mountain shall any hurt or destroy, for the knowledge of goodness shall fill the earth as the waters fill the sea."*

---

## Chapter IV: The Prayer That Saved Jerusalem from Siege

**1.** During the reign of the upright King **Hezekiah**, Emperor Sennacherib of Assyria swept down with a mighty host, captured nearly all the surrounding fortresses, and laid tight siege to the capital city of Jerusalem, sending arrogant taunts claiming no power could deliver the city from his grasp.

**2.** Hearing those grim threats, King Hezekiah tore his royal robes, put on sackcloth in grief, entered the Temple to spread the mocking letter out before the altar in prayer, and sent messengers to seek counsel from the prophet Isaiah.

**3.** Isaiah sent back a steadying word to the king: *"Do not fear the boastful words of the invader! That distant emperor shall not shoot a single arrow into this city, nor raise a shield before its walls, nor cast up a siege ramp against it, but shall turn back along the very road by which he came."* That very night an unforeseen calamity struck the besieging camp, scattering tens of thousands of soldiers and forcing the Assyrian king to withdraw hastily at dawn back to Nineveh, leaving Jerusalem safe and untouched.
