# Early Nature Legends

---

## Chapter I: The White Flower on the Rocky Peak, the Butterflies, and the Honeybees

**1.** At the dawn of the world, when green herbs and trees already clothed the plains while the high rocky mountain peaks still stood bare and icy, young angels flew down intending to adorn the summits with colorful blossoms—but twilight had already fallen, and the golden sunlight had faded too low to mix leafy greens. Looking up to see gentle silver stars twinkling across the night sky, they reached out, caught falling starlight in their hands, and wove it into **a pure five-petaled white flower** blooming amid the frosty mist—so that high upon the alpine peaks there ever grows a white star-flower carrying the light of heaven.

**2.** When wildflowers bloomed across the meadows yet sighed sadly because the birds flew straight up into the high clouds and rarely paused to chat below, a brightly colored cloud floated down from the sky and transformed into **thousands of fluttering, delicate butterflies** drifting onto every flower cup to sip nectar and whisper tales of heaven.

**3.** Yet many tiny blossoms tucked deep inside thorny thickets remained beyond the reach of broad butterfly wings, so a warm brown cloud brought forth **the industrious honeybees**. A gaudy butterfly once boasted scornfully of the brown bee's short little wings; yet in a race to gather nectar from a blossom deep within a dense thicket and a distant old linden tree, the butterfly caught its wings in the brambles while the humble little bee had long since finished gathering its honey—proving every small creature bears its own quiet gift.

## Chapter II: The Plant Bearing Healing Poison and the Thorns of the Rose

**1.** In those early days, all herbs and plants received gentle counsel from the sun: open your petals wide by day to welcome the light, and close your blossoms tightly to sleep safely by night—especially on pitch-dark nights when thick clouds veil moon and stars, lest darkness work you harm.

**2.** One ink-black night, a white bell-shaped forest flower yielded to a tempting whisper promising she would grow into the tallest tree in the woods if only she unclosed her petals. Instantly Darkness poured burning poison into her heart, leaving her petals drooping in pain. At dawn the angel of morning dew passed by to comfort her, advising the flower not to despair, but to gather every drop of that searing sap and lock it safely inside **dark purple-black berries**—both as a warning sign so wild creatures would not eat them by mistake, and so wise human hands in ages to come might transform that very sap into **a precious medicine to heal sickness**.

**3.** Another night, Darkness crept up to a **crimson velvet rosebush** breathing sweet fragrance, tempting the roses to open in the dark, yet the rose held her petals tightly shut and refused to listen. Furious at failing to deceive her, Darkness lashed out with sharp claws, raking long gashes down the rose stems before slinking away. Next morning beneath the warm sun, the scars along the rose stems hardened into **sharp thorns**, standing forever as proud armor guarding fragrance and beauty against every intrusion.

## Chapter III: The Dove and the Bat, the Lamb and the Wild Wolf

**1.** Among all creatures in the early world, the **snow-white dove** perched upon high boughs kept her heart pure and turned ever toward daylight. When Darkness tried to lure her down with promises of painting her plumage in dazzling artificial colors, the dove shook her head, cooed a firm refusal, and beat her wings upward into the bright sky. Enraged, Darkness snatched up a small mouse scurrying in a dark cave, stretched out its ears and toes, and spread black leathery membranes between them for fluttering wings—bringing forth **the bat**, which dares fly only at dusk and shrinks from the sunlight.

**2.** In that same dawn of creation, the **pure white lamb** in the meadow refused Darkness's tempting golden comb and bounded swiftly to safety; meanwhile, a hungry dog lost deep in the forest swallowed a fiery poisoned bait set by Darkness that scorched its belly, made its ruff stand stiff on end, and lengthened its fangs into the **wild wolf** howling through the midnight woods. Ever since those early days, blossoms and creatures standing between light and shadow silently remind humankind to tell uprightness from deceit and turn away from temptation.
