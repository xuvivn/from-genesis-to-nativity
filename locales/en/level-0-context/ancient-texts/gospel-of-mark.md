# Gospel According to Mark

---

## Chapter I: The Call in the Wilderness and Dawn on the River Jordan

**1.** The beginning of the glad tidings sprang from the ancient prophecy written long before: *"Behold, I send My messenger ahead of you to prepare your way; a voice cries out in the wilderness: Make straight the path ahead."*

**2.** Even so, **John** appeared in the lonely wilderness, dressed in camel's hair with a leather belt around his waist, eating locusts and wild honey, and proclaiming a baptism of repentance beside the **River Jordan** to prepare human hearts for renewal. Throngs poured out from all Judea and Jerusalem, confessing their wrongs and stepping down into the river for the washing of water.

**3.** Often John reminded the crowds that one far mightier than himself was soon coming behind him, whose sandal straps he was not worthy to stoop down and untie: John washed people only with river water, whereas the Coming One would renew human beings from the deepest spring of spirit.

**4.** In those very days, **Jesus** walked down from the little village of **Nazareth** in Galilee to the banks of the River Jordan and stepped into the flowing waters to be baptized by John. The instant Jesus rose up from the water, **the heavens tore open**, the Spirit **descended like a dove upon him**, and a voice resounded from high heaven affirming: *"You are My beloved Son; in you I am wholly well pleased."* After that dawning hour beside the river, Jesus entered forty days of quiet solitude in the wilderness surrounded by gentle beasts before beginning his teaching.

---

## Chapter II: The Carpenter of Nazareth and the Steadfast Death of John

**1.** Through the quiet years of growth before going forth to teach across the villages beside the Sea of Galilee, the townspeople of his home village **Nazareth** knew Jesus well as **a humble village carpenter** working steadily in his woodshop amid the neighborhood, dwelling simply beside his mother Mary and kinsfolk in honest daily labor.

**2.** Meanwhile, John's fearless, upright voice along the River Jordan reached all the way to the royal court. Because he dared speak plainly to King **Herod**—the ruling son governing Galilee and the eastern shore of the Jordan—concerning unlawful wrongdoing in the palace, the king had John arrested, bound in chains, and locked deep inside the dungeon of a grim fortress.

**3.** In truth, King Herod stood in awe of John, knowing him to be a just and holy man, and sought to protect his life even though John's words left him deeply troubled. Yet on the king's birthday, at a lavish banquet for his high officials and military commanders, a rash oath sworn before the guests was seized upon by John's enemies to demand the prisoner's head that very night.

**4.** King Herod was filled with deep sorrow, yet unwilling to break his boastful oath before his banquet guests, he sent an executioner down to the prison to put John to death. Hearing the tragic news, John's disciples came to claim their master's body and lay it to rest with honor—closing the steadfast life of the forerunner just as the light of dawn spread outward across the earth.
