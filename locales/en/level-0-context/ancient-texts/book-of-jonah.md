# Book of the Prophet Jonah

---

## Chapter I: The Flight Across the Stormy Sea

**1.** A call came to **Jonah**, son of Amittai, saying: *"Arise and go to **Nineveh**, that great city, and cry out a warning to them, for their injustice has risen up to high heaven."*

**2.** Yet Jonah had no wish to go to that foreign city; instead he rose and fled in the opposite direction toward Tarshish. Going down to the port of Joppa, he paid his fare aboard a merchant ship about to set sail across the open sea, and went down into the lowest hold where he fell fast asleep, hoping to escape his calling.

**3.** Soon a violent gale burst over the ocean, tossing the waves into fury until the vessel groaned as though about to break apart. Terrified, the sailors threw the cargo overboard to lighten the ship and cried out each to his own god. Finding Jonah still fast asleep below deck, the captain shook him awake and urged him to call upon his own God, in case He might spare their lives.

**4.** As the tempest grew wilder still, the sailors cast lots to discover whose fault had brought this calamity upon them; the lot fell straight upon Jonah. No longer hiding the truth, Jonah confessed plainly that he was fleeing from the Maker of sea and dry land. Calmly he told the crew: *"Lift me up and cast me into the sea, and the waters will grow quiet for you, for I know it is on my account that this great storm has overtaken you."* The sailors rowed with all their might to reach shore, yet the surging waves defeated them; at last they begged forgiveness, lifted Jonah, and lowered him into the raging sea. Immediately the sea ceased its fury and lay smooth and still.

---

## Chapter II: Three Days and Three Nights Inside the Great Fish

**1.** To preserve Jonah's life in the deep ocean, a **great fish** swam up and swallowed him whole. Inside the belly of the fish, Jonah stayed **three days and three nights**.

**2.** Out of the dark depths at the bottom of the sea, while seaweed wrapped around his head and the roots of the sunken mountains loomed in his spirit, Jonah poured forth a heartfelt prayer of repentance, recognizing that his life was lifted back from the abyss through mercy and vowing to keep his word faithfully.

**3.** On the third day, the great fish swam close to land and cast Jonah out safely onto the dry sandy shore.

---

## Chapter III: The Repentance of Nineveh

**1.** A second time the call came to him: *"Arise, go to Nineveh, that great city, and proclaim to its people the message I tell you."* This time Jonah obeyed and set out at once.

**2.** Nineveh was a vast metropolis requiring three full days' journey to cross on foot. Jonah entered the city, walked a single day's journey in, and proclaimed boldly: *"Yet forty days, and Nineveh shall be overthrown!"*

**3.** An extraordinary thing came to pass: the people of Nineveh from the greatest to the least believed that warning at once. They proclaimed a fast and put on coarse sackcloth, from high nobles down to the humblest folk. When word reached the palace, the **King of Nineveh** himself rose from his throne, laid aside his royal robe, clothed himself in sackcloth, and sat down in ashes; then he issued a royal decree throughout the city commanding every living creature—**from human beings down to cattle and sheep**—to fast, turn away wholeheartedly from violence and cruelty in their hands, and seek the path of goodness.

**4.** Seeing the sincere remorse and changed deeds of that vast city, the Most High relented from His wrath and spared Nineveh to live in peace.

---

## Chapter IV: The Withered Vine and the Lesson of Compassion

**1.** Far from rejoicing that the city was spared, Jonah grew bitter and deeply angry! Walking out to the eastern side beyond the city walls, he built a rough shelter to sit under the blazing sun and watch what would become of the "enemy" city, grumbling inwardly that he had fled toward Tarshish at the start precisely because he knew the Most High is ever gracious, slow to anger, and quick to forgive.

**2.** That night, a miraculous **gourd vine** sprang up rapidly to spread cool shade over Jonah's head, delighting his weary heart. Yet at dawn the very next morning, a small worm crawled out and gnawed the root of the vine so that it withered and died. As the sun climbed high, a scorching east wind beat directly upon Jonah's head until he grew faint and wished to die, complaining bitterly once more over the loss of his shade.

**3.** Then a gentle yet piercing question echoed within Jonah's soul: *"You feel pity for a **gourd vine** for which you never labored or made grow, which sprang up in a single night and perished in a single night; and should I not have compassion on **great Nineveh**, where dwell more than a hundred and twenty thousand innocent little children who cannot yet tell their right hand from their left, and countless blameless creatures besides?"* That quiet question remained hanging in the air, opening the human heart beyond every narrow boundary of prejudice.
