# Book of the Prophet Jeremiah

---

## Chapter I: The Call in Youth and the Solitude of Speaking Truth

**1.** In the quiet priestly village of Anathoth, young **Jeremiah** received the call to become a voice for truth in a time torn by upheaval and division. When Jeremiah timidly protested that he was still too young and knew not how to speak, a hand reached out gently to touch his mouth with the command: *"Do not be afraid before their faces! I have put truthful words into your mouth; this day I appoint you over the nations to uproot and tear down what is false, before planting and building anew on solid ground."*

**2.** For forty relentless years under weak and haughty kings alike, Jeremiah stood alone at the gate of the Temple urging rulers and citizens to turn away from idol worship, cease oppressing the poor, the orphan, and the widow, and stop placing false trust in stone walls outside while their hearts remained full of deceit.

**3.** For daring to speak unwelcome truth while false prophets lulled the court with empty promises of *"Peace, peace!"*, Jeremiah was mocked by his own townsfolk, struck in the face by the chief officer, locked in the stocks at the city gate, and branded a traitor when he counseled the city to humble itself and avoid needless bloodshed before the might of Babylon.

---

## Chapter II: The Burned Scroll and the Deep Mire Cistern

**1.** Undaunted even when barred from entering the Temple, Jeremiah dictated all his warnings to his loyal scribe Baruch upon a leather scroll, to be read aloud on a great feast day to awaken the people. When the scroll was brought into the king's winter apartment and read before the hearth, after every three or four columns were read, the king coldly sliced them off with a scribe's knife and threw them straight into the glowing brazier until the whole scroll turned to ash. Yet Jeremiah calmly dictated a second scroll, fuller still than the first.

**2.** When Emperor Nebuchadnezzar's Babylonian army closed in to besiege Jerusalem, enraged officials persuaded the king to seize Jeremiah and lower him to die slowly inside a **deep dry cistern** in the prison courtyard. There was no water in the cistern, only **thick, heavy mire** rising waist-high as the prophet sank into the cold mud.

**3.** Thankfully, a kindhearted foreign court official named Ebed-Melech heard of his plight and hurried straight to the king to beg for his rescue. Gathering worn rags and cast-off garments, he tied them to the ends of ropes lowered down into the deep pit, kindly bade Jeremiah pad the ropes softly beneath his arms, and together with thirty men hauled the prophet safely out of the mire.

---

## Chapter III: Sorrow on the Day the City Fell and the New Covenant Within the Heart

**1.** What Jeremiah had foretold with aching heart at last came to pass: after eighteen months of starvation and siege, the walls of Jerusalem were breached. Babylonian troops poured into the city, set fire to the glorious Temple, tore down palace and ramparts alike, and carried most of the citizens far away into exile in Babylon.

**2.** Sitting weeping amidst the desolate ashes of his ruined homeland, Jeremiah felt no bitterness or hatred, only deep lament for his people's fate—while sowing a sturdy seed of hope for their return after seventy years of exile.

**3.** Even amid the darkness of collapse, a luminous promise shone forth concerning the age when houses of brick and stone would stand no more: *"Days are coming when I will make a **new covenant** with them—not like the covenant carved on stone tablets which they broke. I will place upright law deep within their inward being, and **write it directly upon their hearts**, so that from the least of them to the greatest, every soul shall know goodness from within."*
