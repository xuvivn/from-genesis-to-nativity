# The Book of Enoch

---

## Chapter I: The Book of Life in the Crystal Cave and the Age of Clay Idols

**1.** Throughout his years keeping alive the flame of uprightness after Adam's passing, **Seth** wrote down the sacred knowledge of heaven and earth's creation, the names of all creatures, and the promise of light into the **Book of Life**. When old age weakened him and he saw human hearts beginning to divide toward violence, Seth concealed that precious book safely deep inside a **crystal cave** high on a mountainside and sealed the cave mouth shut, awaiting a soul pure enough in a later age to unseal it.

**2.** Surely enough, humanity soon entered a spreading age of darkness under the sway of arrogant scoffers such as **Enos** and his deluded followers. People no longer lifted their hearts toward the sky of light; instead they kneaded clay with their own hands into grotesque idols, set them upon high pedestals for worship, and marked dark charcoal streaks across their own foreheads as a sign of separation from the primal light. Cities swelled with crowds, yet overflowed with greed, envy, and oppression of the weak.

---

## Chapter II: Enoch's Teaching and the Parable of the Sunward Flower

**1.** Amid that dark night of idol worship, baby **Enoch** was born beneath the cooing songs and circling flight of white doves above the rooftop. Growing up in purity and extraordinary courage, as a man Enoch walked straight into the city where crowds bowed before clay statues, lifted his voice like rolling thunder to awaken human conscience, and shattered the lifeless, hollow idols.

**2.** Guided by an angel, Enoch found and unsealed the crystal cave, receiving once more the **Book of Life** of forefather Seth to read deeply into the mysteries of the heavens, the courses of sun and moon, the four winds, and the moral law governing the soul.

**3.** Traveling through forests and hills to teach uprightness, Enoch once crossed paths with a fierce band of robbers who lived by plunder under cover of night. Instead of wielding sword or curse, Enoch stood calmly beside their forest campfire and told them the **parable of the flower**: *"A human being upon the earth is just like a flower: with dark roots it must cling firmly in the cold black soil to stand fast in earthly life, yet with bright petals it ever reaches upward and opens wide to welcome warm sunlight from heaven! Never let your soul sink buried forever in dark soil and forget to look upward toward the light."* Hearing those gentle yet piercing words, the chief and his entire band wept, laid down their knives, and turned to walk the path of goodness.

---

## Chapter III: Ascending the Snowy Peak and the Radiant Jewel on the Ark

**1.** For many years guiding gentle folk to dwell in harmony along the slopes of the sacred mountain, Enoch inscribed all wisdom concerning justice, mercy, and cosmic order on ancient scrolls and handed them down to his son **Methuselah** and his descendant **Lamech**, urging them to keep faith alive before the coming cleansing flood upon the earth.

**2.** When his earthly mission reached fullness, Enoch bade farewell to his family and climbed up onto the **gleaming snow-white peak** piercing the clouds. Within a dazzling halo of light atop that high summit, together with faithful souls who walked beside him, Enoch was borne aloft in a chariot of light straight up among the stars, never tasting decay within the earth.

**3.** Generations later, when aged **Methuselah** grew frail on the eve of the Great Flood, all the wisdom of the Book of Life passed to his righteous grandson **Noah**. As Noah built the three-decked cedarwood ark against a darkened world, the healing angel **Raphael** came and placed in his care **the radiant jewel Anoa**—a pure gem shedding soft, warm light by its own power throughout all forty days and nights of storm-darkened skies, illuminating the inside of the wooden ark so Noah's household and all living kinds sailed safely across the boundless waters toward a new dawn.
