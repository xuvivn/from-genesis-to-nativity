# Ancient Chronicles of Mesopotamia and Egypt

---

## Chapter I: Young Abram and the Furnace of King Nimrod

**1.** In ancient Mesopotamia under the proud rule of King **Nimrod**—who raised a seven-tiered throne for himself and rallied peoples to build the Tower of Babel against heaven—court astrologers beheld in the night sky a **strange bright star** swallowing four stars at the four quarters of heaven, foretelling the birth of a child who would overthrow the worship of idols. Terrified by the omen, King Nimrod ordered all newborn boys hunted down and slain.

**2.** Meanwhile, in the home of the idol-carver Terah, infant **Abram** had just been born; his mother carried him secretly away to hide for years inside a **secluded cave amid the forest hills**. Stepping out from the cave for the first time to gaze upon the wide sky, Abram saw the blazing sun by day and wondered whether the sun was Lord of the universe. Yet at dusk the sun sank out of sight, yielding to the moon and countless twinkling stars through the night; and at dawn the moon grew pale and vanished before morning. From that moment young Abram saw clearly: sun, moon, and stars alike obeyed a higher turning order—there must surely be **one invisible Most High** governing them all!

**3.** Returning to tend his father Terah's shop of clay and wooden idols, one day when no one was around, Abram took a stout stick and smashed all the smaller statues standing on the shelves, leaving only the biggest idol upright and placing the stick in its hand. When his father hurried back in dismay demanding what had happened, Abram answered calmly that the idols had quarreled over the food offerings until the big statue grabbed the stick and battered the little ones! Furious, Terah snapped that lifeless statues of wood and clay could never lift a stick to fight; smiling gently, Abram asked his father in return: *"If you know so well that they have no life or sense, why do you bow down and worship things shaped by your own hands?"*

**4.** Hearing that a youth dared smash idols inside the capital, King Nimrod raged, had Abram bound, and hurled him straight into a **vast lime-kiln furnace** roaring with fire for seven days and nights. Tens of thousands watched breathlessly expecting Abram to turn to ashes; yet an angel descended from heaven and transformed the flames into a **cool, gentle breeze like a blooming garden**. Abram walked unharmed out of the furnace before Nimrod's astounded court, then quietly led his household out of the proud city beneath the starry sky toward the Promised Land.

## Chapter II: The Trial of Glowing Coals and the Two Lions of Egypt

**1.** Centuries later in the palace of Egypt, after baby **Moses** had been drawn from the Nile by Princess Bithia and raised at court, one day as the king held the toddler on his knee, the child playfully lifted Pharaoh's golden crown from the king's head, placed it on his own, and tossed it down to the floor! Court magicians gasped in alarm and warned the king that this omen foretold the child would grow up to seize Egypt's throne, urging his immediate execution.

**2.** A wise counselor suggested testing whether the toddler had acted in innocent play or calculating ambition: set before the boy **a dish gleaming with pure gold** and **a dish heaped with glowing red coals**; if he reached for gold, it proved royal ambition, whereas touching hot coals proved mere childish innocence.

**3.** Just as little Moses began stretching out his hand toward the dish of gold, an unseen angel gently nudged his hand aside so he touched the **glowing red coals** instead. Startled by the sudden burn, the baby snatched back his hand and popped his finger straight into his mouth, scorching the tip of his tongue—leaving Moses slightly slow of speech for life, yet convincing Pharaoh he was completely harmless and sparing him to grow up inside the palace.

**4.** Ancient chronicles also tell of the day Moses and his brother Aaron returned to the palace to demand freedom for the enslaved people: guarding the mighty palace gates stood **two fierce lions** chained on watch, tearing to pieces any uninvited stranger who dared approach. Yet when Moses calmly raised his shepherd's staff and touched them lightly, both raging lions **tucked their tails, crouched meekly to the floor, and wagged in welcome like tame house dogs**, clearing the path for the brothers to stride straight into the throne hall before the dumbfounded guards.

## Chapter III: The Seven Heavens of Sinai and the Dream of the World Tree

**1.** Old traditions relate that during the forty days Moses spent atop **Mount Sinai**, vision carried his spirit up through **seven luminous heavens** to behold the moral order of the universe: where the **Scales of Justice** ever weigh each truthful thought and merciful deed, and where every stream of light springs forth from uprightness of heart before taking shape as the Ten Commandments carved upon twin stone tablets carried down to humanity below.

**2.** And in the closing days of **King David's** reign in Jerusalem, as the aged king lay resting peacefully beside his old harp, in a deep twilight dream he beheld a wondrous vision of his lineage's future: out of his own heart rose a great trunk parting into **two flourishing green branches climbing high across many centuries**—one branch bearing the name of his son **Solomon**, signifying royal wisdom, and the other bearing the name of his son **Nathan**, signifying pure, guileless innocence—until on a distant winter night far in the future, those two branches arched inward and wove together into the **World Tree** bursting into snow-white blossoms to welcome a new dawn of light for all humankind.
