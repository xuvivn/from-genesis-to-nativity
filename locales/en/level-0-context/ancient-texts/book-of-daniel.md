# Book of the Prophet Daniel

---

## Chapter I: Purity at the Royal Court and the Statue in the King's Dream

**1.** When the city of Jerusalem was conquered, many noble youths—handsome, quick-minded, and eager to learn—were carried off to the capital of **Babylon** to be trained in the language and literature of the court of Emperor **Nebuchadnezzar**. Among them was young **Daniel**, along with his three close companions **Hananiah**, **Mishael**, and **Azariah** (known at the Babylonian court as Shadrach, Meshach, and Abednego).

**2.** The king ordered that the students be served rich delicacies and choice wine from the royal table. Yet Daniel resolved to keep his heart pure and not defile himself with such luxurious fare; he asked the chief official to let the four of them be tested for ten days on **simple vegetables and plain water**. After the ten-day trial, the faces of all four youths looked healthier and fairer than all who dined on royal meats and wine, and their wisdom shone ten times brighter than that of all the enchanters in the empire.

**3.** Not long afterward, Emperor Nebuchadnezzar dreamed a strange dream that left his spirit deeply troubled, yet upon waking he could not recall what he had dreamed and demanded that his sages tell him both the dream itself and its meaning. Facing the threat that every scholar in the court would be put to death, Daniel prayed through the night and received clear light in a vision.

**4.** Standing before the solemn throne, Daniel recounted accurately what the king had beheld in his sleep: a **dazzling colossal statue** whose head was of pure gold, its chest and arms of silver, its belly and thighs of bronze, its legs of iron, and its feet partly of iron and partly of clay. Then suddenly **a stone cut without human hands** rolled down, struck the feet of iron and clay, crushed gold, silver, bronze, and iron alike to dust scattered on the wind, and grew into a great mountain filling the whole earth. Daniel explained that the four metals stood for four successive earthly kingdoms, and at last all fleeting power would pass away before a righteous kingdom enduring forever. Astonished, the king appointed Daniel chief over all the wise men of Babylon.

---

## Chapter II: The Three Youths in the Furnace Heated Seven Times Over

**1.** Some time later, King Nebuchadnezzar erected a giant golden image sixty cubits high on the plain of Dura and commanded that whenever the horn, flute, and lyre sounded, all officials throughout the empire must fall down and worship the image; whoever disobeyed would instantly be cast into a **blazing fiery furnace**.

**2.** Daniel's three friends—**Shadrach, Meshach, and Abednego**—stood firm and refused to bow before the golden idol. Brought before the furious king, all three answered serenely: *"The Most High whom we serve is able to deliver us from the blazing fiery furnace; and even if He does not, we will never bow down to your golden image, O king."*

**3.** Blazing with wrath, the king commanded the **furnace to be heated seven times hotter than usual**, and had the three youths bound tightly and thrown straight into the flames. So fierce was the heat that the fire killed the very soldiers who carried them to the mouth of the furnace. Yet the next instant, King Nebuchadnezzar leaped from his throne in amazement and asked his counselors: *"Did we not cast three men bound into the fire? How is it I see **four men unbound walking unharmed in the midst of the flames**, untouched by fire, and the fourth looks like a messenger from heaven?"*

**4.** Hastening near the furnace door, the king called the three youths to come out. To the astonishment of the whole court, the fire had left no mark upon their bodies—**not a single hair on their heads was singed, their cloaks were unchanged, and not even the smell of fire clung to them**. From that day on, the king issued a decree honoring the faith of those three young friends.

## Chapter III: The Handwriting on the Wall at Belshazzar's Feast

**1.** Many years later under King **Belshazzar**, the ruler held a lavish banquet for a thousand nobles, and arrogantly ordered the gold and silver vessels looted from the Temple of Jerusalem brought in so his guests might drink wine from them while praising idols of gold, silver, bronze, iron, wood, and stone.

**2.** Amid the noisy revelry of the feast, suddenly **the fingers of a human hand** appeared and wrote upon the white plaster of the banquet hall wall, right where the lampstand light shone. The king's face turned deathly pale, and his knees knocked together so he could scarcely stand. When none of the court diviners could read those strange words, the queen mother remembered the wise sage Daniel.

**3.** Entering without seeking reward, Daniel calmly read aloud the writing upon the wall: *"MENE, MENE, TEKEL, UPHARSIN"*—meaning: the days of your reign have been numbered and brought to an end; you have been weighed in the balance and found wanting; your kingdom is divided and given to the Medes and Persians. That very night Babylon fell, ending Belshazzar's proud reign.

## Chapter IV: Footprints in the Ash on the Floor of Bel's Temple

**1.** When King Cyrus of Persia took rule over Babylon, the new monarch cherished Daniel's wisdom. One day the king asked Daniel why he refused to worship the clay-and-bronze idol Bel, which daily consumed measures of fine flour, forty sheep, and six large jars of wine set upon its altar.

**2.** Daniel smiled gently and replied that a lifeless image of clay and bronze could never eat or drink a morsel. Surprised and indignant, the king summoned Bel's seventy priests and laid down a test: if the banquet truly vanished overnight, Daniel would be held guilty of falsehood; if not, the priests themselves would face judgment.

**3.** That evening the priests set out the rich feast on the great altar inside the shrine and stepped outside so the king might seal the doors. Yet before the doors closed, Daniel secretly had his servants bring sieves and **scatter a fine layer of ash evenly across the entire stone floor of the temple**, unseen by the priests, after which the king pressed his signet ring firmly upon the sealed door.

**4.** At first light the next morning, the king and Daniel broke the seal and stepped inside. Seeing every dish of meat, bread, and wine completely gone, the king cried out in praise of mighty Bel! Yet Daniel held the king back and pointed down at the pavement: *"Look down upon the ash on the floor, O king, and see whose footprints are these!"* In the morning light, across the fine ash lay clear **footprints of men, women, and little children** leading to and from a hidden trapdoor beneath the altar table—every night the seventy priests and their families crept in through the secret tunnel to eat the offerings! At once the king exposed the fraud before the entire city.

---

## Chapter V: A Night in the Lions' Den and the Vision on the Clouds of Heaven

**1.** Under King Darius, envious of how Daniel was trusted above all the governors of the realm, rival officials laid a cunning snare: they persuaded the king to sign an unchangeable decree that for thirty days anyone who prayed to any god or mortal save the king alone should be cast into a **den of hungry lions**.

**2.** Though knowing full well the decree was signed, three times every day as had been his lifelong custom, Daniel went up to his upper chamber, opened the windows facing Jerusalem, knelt down, and gave thanks and prayed in peace. His enemies lay in wait, caught him in the act, and forced the king to uphold the law. Grieved to the heart and searching in vain until sundown for some way to rescue his friend, King Darius at last had to give the order to lower Daniel into the lions' den and sealed the stone over its mouth with his own hand.

**3.** All night the king fasted and could not sleep, allowing no music in the palace. At the first break of dawn, the king hurried straight to the lions' den and cried out in an anxious voice: *"O Daniel, faithful servant, has the One you serve been able to save you from the lions?"* Up from the deep pit rose Daniel's calm voice: *"An angel was sent down to **shut the lions' mouths**, so they have done me no harm, because I stand innocent before truth!"* Overjoyed, the king had Daniel lifted out, and not a single scratch was found upon his body.

**4.** In his final years in exile, Daniel beheld a wondrous vision of the tide of history: four fierce beasts emerging from a stormy sea, representing violent empires rising one after another over the earth; then the court of justice opened, and **one like a Son of Man came riding upon the clouds of heaven**, receiving everlasting glory and authority so that all peoples and languages might dwell together in a kingdom that shall never pass away.
