# Book of Deuteronomy

---

## Chapter I: Final Counsel on an Upright Heart

**1.** After forty years of leading the people across the vast desert, standing on the eastern bank of the River Jordan facing the green Promised Land, Moses gathered the whole assembly together to remind them of the deepest truths before his farewell.

**2.** He lifted his voice in earnest counsel: *"Hear, O whole people! You shall love with all your heart, with all your soul, and with all your strength the Most High who brought you forth out of bondage. These words I teach you today shall be engraved deep within your heart; teach them diligently to your children, when you sit in your house and when you walk by the way, when you lie down and when you rise up."*

**3.** Moses urged the people never to forget the lessons of those arduous wilderness years: that human beings live not by bread alone but by every word of truth; never to grow haughty once they dwell in fine houses and eat fruit from lush vineyards, forgetting the One who sustained them; and ever to deal justly with the helpless, shield the widow and the orphan, and love the stranger as themselves, remembering that they too were once strangers in the land of Egypt.

---

## Chapter II: The View from Mount Nebo

**1.** Knowing that his own hour was drawing near, Moses spoke words of courage to his young successor **Joshua** before all the people: *"Be strong and of good courage! Do not fear or be dismayed, for it is you who shall bring this people into the fertile land promised to their ancestors."*

**2.** Having written down the book of the law and entrusted it to the elders to keep beside the Ark of the Covenant, Moses sang his final song calling heaven and earth to witness to justice and mercy, and blessed each of the twelve tribes of Israel in turn.

**3.** From the plains of Moab, Moses climbed alone to the summit of **Mount Nebo**, to the lofty peak of **Pisgah** overlooking Jericho. From that windswept height, the eyes of the hundred-and-twenty-year-old leader remained clear and undimmed; he swept his gaze across the entire Promised Land stretching from the northern hills of Gilead down to the plain of Jericho—the city of palm trees—and all the way to the distant Western Sea at the horizon.

**4.** The Most High spoke to him: *"This is the land I swore to give to the descendants of Abraham, Isaac, and Jacob. I have let you look upon it with your own eyes, yet here you shall enter into rest, and hand over the crossing of the river to the rising generation."*

**5.** There in the land of Moab, Moses gently closed his eyes in peace. He was buried in a quiet valley opposite Beth-Peor, and to this day no human being knows the site of his grave. All the people wept and mourned for him thirty days before rising to turn their eyes toward the River Jordan flowing before them.
