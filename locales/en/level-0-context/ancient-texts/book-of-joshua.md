# Book of Joshua

---

## Chapter I: The Scarlet Cord at the Window of Jericho

**1.** After the death of Moses, **Joshua** took up the calling to lead the people across the River Jordan. From the camp beside the river, Joshua secretly sent two scouts across to reconnoiter the land ahead, and especially the fortified city of **Jericho**.

**2.** The two men entered the city and took lodging in the house of a woman named **Rahab**, built right into the city wall. Learning that the king of Jericho had sent soldiers to seize them, Rahab swiftly led the two men up to the roof and hid them beneath stalks of flax drying in the sun, then misdirected the pursuers out beyond the city gate.

**3.** When the soldiers had gone, Rahab climbed up to the roof and told her two guests that all the inhabitants of the city had heard of the wondrous parting of the Red Sea and of their righteousness; she asked the two men to swear to spare the lives of her father, mother, brothers, sisters, and all her household when the city should fall.

**4.** The two scouts pledged safety for her entire family under one unmistakable sign: she was to lower them down the outside of the city wall by a rope through her window, and then tie that **scarlet cord** fast in the very same window so that all would see it and spare the house on the day of assault.

---

## Chapter II: Crossing the River Jordan on Dry Ground

**1.** On the day of departure, the entire multitude drew up in orderly ranks along the bank of the River Jordan, then overflowing its banks at flood season. Joshua bade the priests carrying the **Ark of the Covenant** march a distance ahead of the people and step straight into the water's edge.

**2.** And lo, the moment the soles of the priests' feet carrying the Ark touched the rushing current, the waters flowing down from upstream halted and rose up like a distant wall, while the waters downstream drained away into the Dead Sea, leaving the riverbed dry.

**3.** The priests bearing the Ark of the Covenant stood firm in the middle of the dry riverbed until the whole nation had crossed safely to the far shore. Joshua appointed twelve men, one from each of the twelve tribes, to gather twelve large stones from the middle of the riverbed and raise a memorial monument at Gilgal, so that children in ages to come might remember the day the river opened a way.

---

## Chapter III: Seven Days Around the Walls of Jericho

**1.** Jericho kept its heavy iron gates tightly barred; none went out and none came in. Joshua commanded the entire people to keep a singular siege in complete silence: once each day for six days, armed warriors marched in front, followed by seven priests blowing seven ram's-horn trumpets directly before the Ark of the Covenant, while all the people walked quietly behind once around the city walls and returned to camp—not a single shout or spoken word was permitted.

**2.** On the seventh day, the entire host rose at dawn and marched around the city walls seven times in the same manner. At the seventh circuit, when the seven priests blew one long, resounding blast echoing across the valley, Joshua cried aloud: *"Shout!"*

**3.** With one voice all the people raised a mighty shout that shook earth and sky; in that very instant, the thick stone walls of Jericho collapsed flat where they stood. The warriors marched straight into the city without meeting resistance.

**4.** Only the house built upon the section of wall with the **scarlet cord** hanging from its window remained untouched. The two scouts of earlier ran straight there and brought Rahab, her father and mother, her brothers and sisters, and all her kindred safely outside the camp, keeping their oath of honor in full.
