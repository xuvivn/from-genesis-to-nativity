# The Life of Adam and Eve

---

## Chapter I: The First Night Outside the Gates of Paradise

**1.** When the radiant gates of Paradise closed behind them with the whirling sword of flame standing guard, **Adam** and **Eve** took their first barefoot steps upon the stony earth outside. A chill wind stung flesh that had never known frost or gale; hastily they gathered fallen branches, palm fronds, and forest leaves to build a frail shelter against wild beasts in the dark night.

**2.** The next morning as they walked in search of water and wild fruit, Adam's heavy foot stepped straight onto sharp thorns along the trail, drawing the **first drops of human blood** upon the earth; he bound strips of soft tree bark beneath his soles and gathered dry grass and soft moss to make their first bed. Finding a few ripe fruits to offer Eve in her hunger, she took one bite and tasted how faint and flat it seemed beside the pure fragrance of their lost garden; remembering Paradise, Eve broke down weeping aloud—shedding the **first human tears** into the parched soil.

**3.** As husband and wife sat holding each other in sorrow before their cold hut, a warm ray of light shone softly down from above together with comforting counsel from the angel **Michael**: humankind was not abandoned forever; an **invisible thread of light** still joined an upright soul to heaven through prayer. Michael taught Adam to arrange field stones into the first altar and kindle a sacred flame whose fragrant smoke rose straight toward heaven, carrying hope into an arduous earthly life.

---

## Chapter II: Seth's Journey and the Three Seeds of the Tree of Life

**1.** After nine hundred and thirty years of sweating labor upon the earth—tasting alike the joy of raising children and the heart-rending grief of Cain and Abel—at last Adam fell gravely ill and lay down beside his hut surrounded by his family. His gentle, devout third son, **Seth**, grieved deeply and sought how he might soothe his father's pain.

**2.** Remembering how his father often spoke of the sweet scent of the oil of mercy from the Tree of Life in their former garden, Seth and his mother Eve set out along the ancient paths until they stood right before the glowing gates of Paradise; kneeling with ashes sprinkled upon their heads, they earnestly implored for a single drop of oil from the **Tree of Life** to anoint and heal their father.

**3.** At the luminous threshold, the angel **Michael** stepped forth and answered gently: *"The hour has not yet come for the oil of mercy to flow forth for all creatures upon the earth; return and tell Adam that in only three days his spirit shall be set free from his body to enter rest. Yet do not despair!"* So saying, the angel reached out and placed in Seth's hand **three precious seeds taken from the Tree of Life** (some traditions tell of a small branch bearing three shoots of pine, cedar, and fragrant cypress), instructing him: *"When your father breathes his last, place these three seeds directly beneath his tongue as you lay him to rest!"*

---

## Chapter III: The Sacred Wood and the Tomb on Mount Golgotha

**1.** Tenderly Seth carried the three seeds back to his father's bedside; hearing his son recount the angel's promise of future restoration, Adam smiled peacefully for the last time and gently breathed out his life. Seth and the household did exactly as instructed, placing the **three seeds of the Tree of Life beneath Adam's tongue** before carefully covering his resting place with earth.

**2.** Before long, from that burial place sprang up **three flourishing young shoots** of three noble woods—cedar, cypress, and olive—which gradually twisted and wove tightly together into one mighty sacred tree spreading cool shade through the ages, becoming the living thread linking the dawn of Creation to the Temple and the redeeming cross many millennia later.

**3.** Ancient tradition preserves its memory across the Great Flood: before the floodwaters drowned the earth, Noah reverently bore the remains of forefather Adam aboard and laid them in the very center of the cedarwood ark. After the waters subsided and the ark rested on Mount Ararat, Noah's eldest son **Shem**, following angelic guidance, carried Adam's bones westward to the land of promise and buried them solemnly upon a limestone hill shaped like a brow, called **Golgotha**—chosen as the sacred meeting point of salvation history for ages to come.
