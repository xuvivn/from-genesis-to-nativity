# Book of Tobit

---

## Chapter I: The Righteousness of Old Tobit in Exile

**1.** Carried away into exile in the city of Nineveh in Assyria, **Tobit** of the tribe of Naphtali kept faithfully to an upright life and compassionate care amid troubled times. Whenever he saw fellow exiles poor and destitute, he gladly shared his bread and clothing; whenever he found the body of an unfortunate soul cast out beyond the city walls whom none dared bury for fear of the king's decree, under cover of night he quietly dug a grave and gave the dead an honorable burial.

**2.** One night, exhausted from doing such a deed of mercy, Tobit lay down to sleep outdoors in the courtyard beside the wall rather than go inside, unaware that sparrows nesting above dropped warm droppings straight into both his eyes. Despite every remedy, white films thickened over his eyes until he became completely blind. Even living in darkness and poverty, sustained by the tireless weaving of his wife Anna, Tobit kept his trust unbroken and never complained against his lot.

**3.** Remembering that years earlier he had left ten talents of silver in trust with a dependable friend named Gabael in distant Rages in Media, old Tobit called his only son, young **Tobias**, and instructed him: find a trustworthy guide to journey to Media and reclaim the silver so the household might live, and above all, throughout his life love the poor, shun deceit, and deal fairly with every human being.

---

## Chapter II: Setting Out with the Companion Azariah and the Little Dog

**1.** Young Tobias went out into the street to look for someone who knew the roads to Media, and right away met a gentle-mannered young man waiting there who gave his name as **Azariah**, claiming kinship and deep familiarity with the mountain trails. In truth, Azariah was none other than the healing angel **Raphael**, veiled in human form to protect Tobit's family.

**2.** After agreeing upon fair wages and receiving the blessing of peace from his father and mother, Tobias slung his pack over his shoulder and set out beside Azariah, while the family's **loyal little dog** trotted happily behind the two travelers along the long road.

---

## Chapter III: The Great Fish of the River Tigris

**1.** At the close of the first day, the two travelers stopped to camp for the night beside the swift waters of the **Tigris**. Stepping down to the river's edge to wash his feet, Tobias was startled when a huge fish leaped up from the water trying to swallow his foot!

**2.** Without flinching, Azariah called out to Tobias to seize the fish firmly by the gills and haul it onto dry land. Once ashore, Azariah instructed his young friend to cut open the fish and **carefully keep the heart, the liver, and the gall**, for they were precious medicines: the heart and liver, burned as incense, drive away every dark dread, while the **fish gall** serves as a salve to cure white blindness of the eyes. They roasted the rest of the fish as provisions for the road and pressed onward into Media.

---

## Chapter IV: Delivering Sarah from the Shadow of Sorrow

**1.** Reaching the city of Ecbatana, Azariah led Tobias to stay at the home of their close kinsman Raguel, who had an only daughter named **Sarah**. Bitter tragedy hung over Sarah's life: seven times she had been given in marriage, yet each time on the wedding night a dark evil spirit struck down her bridegroom, leaving her brokenhearted in despair and praying only for deliverance.

**2.** Strengthened by the wise counsel and guidance of his companion Azariah, Tobias asked courageously and with deep reverence for Sarah's hand in marriage. That night in the bridal chamber, rather than rush into merriment, the newlyweds knelt together in earnest prayer asking for peace and mercy to shield their new home. At the same time, Tobias laid the fish's heart and liver upon glowing coals; the rising fragrant smoke drove away the dark shadow forever, bringing lasting peace to the entire household.

**3.** During the days of the wedding feast, Azariah traveled on alone to Rages, recovered the full sum of silver Tobit had entrusted years before, and placed it squarely into Tobias's hands amid the rejoicing of both families.

---

## Chapter V: Sight Restored and the Angel Revealed

**1.** On the homeward road to Nineveh, Azariah urged Tobias to hurry on ahead with the **little dog** trotting joyfully beside him to bring the glad tidings, keeping the vial of fish gall ready in his hand. Anna, who watched along the lane every day hoping for her son's return, caught sight of him from afar and ran to throw her arms around him weeping for joy, while old Tobit stumbled slowly out to the doorway to greet his boy.

**2.** Tobias ran to embrace his father, gently blew upon his eyes, and rubbed the **fish gall** across Tobit's tightly shut lids. Waiting a moment as the eyes stung lightly, the young man delicately peeled away the thick white scales from the corners of his father's eyes: instantly **old Tobit's sight blazed clear again**; gazing clearly upon the handsome face of his son, he embraced him and wept aloud. The whole house rejoiced and held a festive welcome for the new bride Sarah amid bright blessings.

**3.** When the feast ended, father and son agreed to offer fully half of all the wealth they had brought back to repay Azariah for his boundless kindness to their family. Calling Azariah aside to offer this reward, the young traveler smiled warmly and bade them bless the Most High and devote their lives to doing good in the world; then he unveiled his true identity: *"I am the angel **Raphael**, one of the seven who stand before the throne of the Holy One, sent down to walk beside you and heal both father and son!"*

**4.** Having spoken those parting words, the angel dissolved into a stream of light and vanished from their sight. Father and son fell down upon the ground in silent gratitude for a long while, then rose to live all the rest of their days in serenity and joy.
