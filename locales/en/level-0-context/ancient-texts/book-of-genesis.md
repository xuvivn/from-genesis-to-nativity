# Book of Genesis

---

## Chapter I: The Seven Days of Creation of Heaven and Earth, All Living Things, and Humankind

**1.** In the beginning, the Most High created the heavens and the earth. The earth was then wild and empty, darkness covered the face of the deep abyss, and the Spirit of the Most High hovered over the waters.

**2.** The Most High spoke, saying: *"Let there be light."* And at once there was light. He saw that the light was good, and He separated the light from the darkness. He called the light Day, and the darkness He called Night. An evening passed and then a morning: that was the first day.

**3.** The Most High spoke, saying: *"Let there be a high vault amidst the waters to divide the waters above from the waters below."* He fashioned the vault of the sky, separating the waters beneath the vault from the waters above it. He called that vault Heaven. An evening passed and then a morning: that was the second day.

**4.** The Most High spoke, saying: *"Let the waters under heaven be gathered into one place, so that the dry land may appear."* He called the dry land Earth, and the gathering place of the waters He called Sea. Then He spoke: *"Let the earth bring forth herbs, seed-bearing grasses, and fruit trees yielding fruit after their own kind upon the face of the earth."* At once the earth sprouted carpets of green grass, seed-bearing plants, and trees bearing sweet fruit each according to its kind. He saw that all was good. An evening passed and then a morning: that was the third day.

**5.** The Most High spoke, saying: *"Let there be lights in the vault of heaven to divide the day from the night, and let them serve as signs for seasons, for days, and for years."* He set two great lights: the greater light to govern the day, which is the sun, and the lesser light to govern the night, which is the moon, together with countless twinkling stars across the vault of heaven to shed light upon the earth. He saw that it was very good. An evening passed and then a morning: that was the fourth day.

**6.** The Most High spoke, saying: *"Let the waters teem abundantly with living creatures, and let birds fly above the earth across the vault of heaven."* He created the great sea monsters and every kind of fish swimming in the seas, and every winged bird flying beneath the sky. He blessed them, saying: *"Be fruitful and multiply and fill the seas, and let birds increase upon the earth."* An evening passed and then a morning: that was the fifth day.

**7.** The Most High spoke, saying: *"Let the earth bring forth living creatures after their kind: cattle, creeping things, and beasts of the forest."* He made every kind of wild beast, cattle, and every small creature creeping upon the ground. He saw that every kind was good.

**8.** Last of all, the Most High spoke: *"Let Us make humankind in Our image, after Our likeness, that they may tend and watch over the fish of the sea, the birds of the sky, the cattle, all the earth, and every living thing upon the earth."* The Most High shaped the human form from fine dust of the earth, and breathed the breath of life into his nostrils, and the human became a living soul. He named the first man Adam.

**9.** The Most High gazed upon everything He had made, and behold, it was all very good. An evening passed and then a morning: that was the sixth day.

**10.** Thus the heavens and the earth and all their host were finished. On the seventh day, the Most High completed His work and entered into a day of rest. He blessed the seventh day and made it holy, for on that day He rested from all His work of creation.

---

## Chapter II: The Garden of Eden, the Four Rivers, and the Making of Woman

**1.** The Most High planted a wondrous garden in the East, called the Garden of Eden, and placed within it the human being He had formed. From the soil He caused every tree to grow that is lovely to look upon and good for food, together with the **Tree of Life** standing in the midst of the garden and the **Tree of the Knowledge of Good and Evil**.

**2.** A river flowed out of Eden to water and refresh the garden, and from there it divided into four great streams: the first branch was Pishon, winding around the land rich in pure gold and precious stones; the second branch was Gihon, winding around the land of Cush; the third branch was the Tigris, flowing east of Assyria; and the fourth branch was the river Euphrates.

**3.** The Most High placed Adam in the Garden of Eden to till it and keep watch over it. He instructed him, saying: *"Of every tree in the garden you may freely eat, but of the Tree of the Knowledge of Good and Evil you shall not eat, for in the day that you eat of its fruit, you shall surely die."*

**4.** The Most High said: *"It is not good for the man to be alone; I will make a fitting companion for him."* He brought every beast of the field and every bird of the sky before Adam to see what he would name each one; and whatever Adam called each living creature, that became its name. Adam gave names to all cattle, to the birds of the air, and to every beast of the forest, yet among all creatures there was still not found a companion fitting for him.

**5.** Then the Most High caused a deep sleep to fall upon Adam, took one of the ribs from his side, and closed up the flesh in its place. From that rib, the Most High fashioned the woman and brought her to Adam.

**6.** Seeing her, Adam cried out in joy: *"This at last is bone of my bones and flesh of my flesh!"* Adam named his wife **Eve**, meaning the mother of all living. And the two dwelt in the garden, naked and unashamed.

---

## Chapter III: The Serpent's Temptation, the Judgment, and Stepping Out of Eden

**1.** Now of all the wild creatures that the Most High had made, the serpent was the most cunning. It came and spoke to the woman, saying: *"Has the Most High truly forbidden you two from eating the fruit of every tree in the garden?"*

**2.** The woman answered: *"We may eat of the fruit of the trees in the garden; only of the fruit of the tree in the midst of the garden did the Most High warn us not to eat, nor even to touch it, lest we die."*

**3.** Then the serpent tempted her: *"You shall not die at all! For the Most High knows that in the day you eat of that fruit, your eyes will be opened and you will become like divine beings, knowing both good and evil."*

**4.** The woman looked up at the fruit and saw that it was good for food, pleasing to the eyes, and desirable to open the mind to wisdom; so she plucked and ate a bite, and gave some to her husband beside her, and he ate as well. At once the eyes of both were opened; they realized they were naked, and sewed fig leaves together to cover themselves.

**5.** In the cool of the evening, hearing the sound of the Most High walking in the garden, Adam and his wife hurried to hide themselves among the trees. The Most High called to Adam: *"Where are you?"* Adam answered: *"I heard Your voice in the garden, and I was afraid and hid because I am naked."* He asked: *"Who told you that you were naked? Have you eaten of the tree from which I commanded you not to eat?"*

**6.** Adam answered that the woman whom He had placed beside him had given him the fruit to eat; and the woman answered that the serpent had deceived her. Then the Most High declared to the serpent that henceforth it should crawl upon its belly and eat dust all the days of its life, and that between the woman's offspring and the serpent there would forever be enmity, until at last the woman's offspring should crush its head.

**7.** To the woman He spoke that she would bear many hardships in bringing forth children. And to Adam He spoke: *"Because you listened to the voice of your wife and ate of the fruit I forbade you, the ground shall henceforth bring forth thorns and weeds; by the sweat of your brow you shall eat bread all the days of your life, until the day you return to the dust from which you were formed."*

**8.** The Most High made garments of animal skins and clothed Adam and Eve, then sent them forth from the Garden of Eden to till the earth outside. At the east of the garden He stationed the Cherubim angels and a whirling sword of flame to guard the way leading to the Tree of Life.

---

## Chapter IV: Cain and Abel, the First Line of Civilization, and the Birth of Seth

**1.** After leaving the garden, Adam and Eve bore their firstborn son and named him **Cain**, and afterward bore a younger brother named **Abel**. As they grew, Abel became a gentle keeper of sheep, while Cain became a tiller of the soil.

**2.** At harvest time, Cain brought an offering of the fruits of the ground to the Most High; while Abel chose the finest, fattest firstborn lambs of his flock to offer. The Most High looked with favor upon Abel and his pure offering, yet He did not look with favor upon Cain's offering.

**3.** Cain's face darkened with anger. The Most High gently counseled him: *"Why is your face darkened with anger? If you do what is upright, will you not lift up your head? Yet if you do what is ill, sin crouches at the threshold, and you must master it."*

**4.** Yet Cain could not restrain the envy in his heart; he called his brother out into the open field and fell upon Abel and slew him. When the Most High asked: *"Where is your brother Abel?"*, Cain answered defiantly: *"I do not know! Am I my brother's keeper?"* The cry of Abel's blood rose from the earth to high heaven; Cain was forced to leave the fertile ground and become a wanderer east of Eden, settling in the land of Nod.

**5.** The descendants of Cain founded the first cities and crafts of humankind: Cain built a city and named it after his son Enoch. Many generations later, his descendant Lamech fathered three sons who opened three great arts: **Jabal** became the father of those who dwell in tents and herd livestock; **Jubal** became the father of all who play the harp and pipe the flute; and **Tubal-Cain** became the first smith, forging every tool of bronze and iron. Tubal-Cain's sister was **Naamah**. Yet alongside the iron blade, Lamech also sang a song boasting of vengeance and violent might.

**6.** Meanwhile, Adam and Eve bore another son in place of Abel, and named him **Seth**. Seth fathered a son named Enos; from that time people began to call upon the name of the Most High, though some strayed and wandered far from the light.

---

## Chapter V: The Lineage Through Seth to Enoch and Noah

**1.** The line from Adam through Seth passed down through many long-lived generations: Adam lived nine hundred and thirty years and died. After Seth came Enos, Kenan, Mahalalel, and Jared.

**2.** Jared fathered **Enoch**. Unlike the corrupt ways of the world around him, Enoch walked uprightly with the Most High for three hundred years after fathering Methuselah. All the days of Enoch upon the earth were three hundred and sixty-five years. Because Enoch walked faithfully with the Most High, one day he was seen no more, for the Most High took him away.

**3.** Enoch's son was **Methuselah**, who lived nine hundred and sixty-nine years. Methuselah fathered **Lamech**, and Lamech fathered a son whom he named **Noah**, declaring in hope: *"This child shall bring us comfort from the heavy toil of our hands upon the earth."*

---

## Chapter VI–IX: Noah's Ark, the Great Flood, and the Rainbow of the Covenant

**1.** As humankind multiplied upon the earth, the hearts of people inclined ever more toward evil, and everywhere the world was filled with violence and corruption. The Most High grieved to see the earth utterly ruined, yet **Noah** alone was a righteous man, blameless in his generation, walking ever with the Most High. Noah had three sons: **Shem**, **Ham**, and **Japheth**.

**2.** The Most High commanded Noah: *"Build for yourself an ark of cypress wood; divide the ark into many rooms and coat it tightly with pitch inside and out. Let its length be three hundred cubits, its width fifty cubits, and its height thirty cubits, built with three decks, a clear window opening near the top, and a door in its side. For I am about to bring a great flood of waters upon the earth to wash away all corruption."*

**3.** Noah obeyed and did everything just as he had been commanded. When the ark was finished, the Most High told Noah to enter the ark with his wife, his three sons, and his three daughters-in-law, bringing with them pairs—male and female—of every bird of the sky, every beast, and every creature of the earth, together with ample provisions to sustain them all. Then the Most High Himself shut the door of the ark behind them.

**4.** Rain poured down in torrents for forty days and forty nights; all the fountains of the great deep burst open, and the waters rose higher and higher until they covered even the tallest mountain peaks upon the earth. Noah's ark floated safely upon the vast expanse of waters.

**5.** After one hundred and fifty days, the waters began to recede; in the seventh month, the ark came to rest upon the mountains of **Ararat**. Waiting another forty days, Noah opened the window of the ark and sent forth a raven, which flew to and fro until the waters dried up from the earth. Then Noah sent forth a dove to see whether the waters had subsided, but finding nowhere to set her foot, the dove returned to the ark.

**6.** Waiting seven more days, Noah sent out the dove a second time; toward evening she returned to him carrying in her beak a **fresh green olive leaf**. Then Noah knew that the waters had receded from the earth. Waiting yet another seven days, he sent her forth a third time, and she returned to him no more.

**7.** Noah's family and all the beasts and birds stepped forth from the ark onto dry ground. At once Noah built an altar of stones and offered a sacrifice of thanksgiving. The Most High was pleased and established an everlasting covenant with every living creature on earth: He set the **seven-colored rainbow** in the clouds of heaven as a sign that never again would a great flood destroy the earth.

**8.** After the flood, Noah began to till the soil and planted a vineyard upon the mountain slope. From his three sons—Shem, Ham, and Japheth—their descendants spread forth to dwell across the entire earth.

---

## Chapter X–XI: The Kingdom of Nimrod and the Tower of Babel

**1.** Among the descendants after the flood rose **Nimrod**, a man of extraordinary strength, who became a mighty hunter and the first king to establish a great kingdom at Babel and in the land of Shinar.

**2.** At that time all humankind spoke one language and used the same words. Moving eastward to the plain of Shinar, they said to one another: *"Come, let us mold bricks and bake them thoroughly in fire, so that we may build a city and a towering spire reaching up to heaven, to make a name for ourselves forever lest we be scattered over all the earth."*

**3.** The Most High came down to see the city and the tower the people were building; seeing their united pride, He confused their speech so that one could no longer understand the words of another. Thus they had to leave off building the city and scattered to the four corners of the earth. Therefore that place was called **Babel**, which means confusion.

---

## Chapter XII–XVIII: The Call of Abraham and the Three Guests Beneath the Oak Trees

**1.** From the city of Ur in Mesopotamia, the Most High called **Abram**, saying: *"Leave your homeland, your kindred, and your father's house, and go to the land I will show you. I will make of you a great nation, and through you all the families of the earth shall be blessed."* Abram obeyed and set out with his wife Sarai and his nephew Lot, journeying into the land of Canaan.

**2.** One night the Most High led Abram outside his tent and bade him look up at the sky: *"Gaze into the heavens and count the stars, if you are able to number them. So countless shall your descendants be!"* Abram put his whole trust in that promise. Afterward, the Most High changed his name to **Abraham** and his wife's name to **Sarah**, meaning father and mother of many nations.

**3.** In the heat of midday beside the grove of oak trees at Mamre, Abraham sat at the entrance of his tent and looked up to see three strangers standing before him. He ran out swiftly to bow in welcome, fetched water to wash their feet, asked his wife to knead fine flour and bake bread, chose the tenderest calf to prepare a feast beneath the ancient trees, and stood attentively beside the guests as they ate.

**4.** The three guests brought tidings that at this very season the following year, though Abraham and his wife were well advanced in age, Sarah would bear a son. Listening from inside the tent doorway, Sarah smiled quietly to herself at the thought of such joy in old age, yet the angel assured them that nothing is beyond the power of the Most High.

---

## Chapter XXII & XXIV: The Trial on Mount Moriah and Rebekah at the Well

**1.** True to the promise, Sarah bore Abraham a son whom they named **Isaac**, meaning laughter. When the boy grew older, the Most High wished to test Abraham's faith and spoke to him: *"Take your beloved, your only son Isaac, and go to a mountain in the land of Moriah to offer him there."*

**2.** Early the next morning, Abraham split firewood, saddled his donkey, and took Isaac and two servants on a three-day journey to the foot of the mountain. Father and son climbed toward the summit, Isaac carrying the bundle of wood while Abraham carried the fire and the knife. Along the way Isaac asked: *"My father, here are the fire and the wood, but where is the lamb for the offering?"* Abraham answered gently: *"My son, the Most High Himself will provide the lamb for us."*

**3.** Upon the summit of Mount Moriah, when Abraham had arranged the stone altar and raised the knife in proof of his complete obedience, the voice of an angel rang down from heaven: *"Abraham! Do not lay your hand upon the boy, for now I know that you revere the Most High with all your heart!"* Looking up, Abraham saw right behind a thicket a **ram caught by its horns in the brambles**; he took the ram and offered it in place of his son, naming that place *"The Most High provides."*

**4.** When Abraham grew old and frail, he sent his most faithful steward with ten camels laden with gifts back to his ancestral homeland to seek a wife for Isaac. Arriving at a well outside the city at evening, the steward prayed silently for a sign: whichever maiden gladly offered him water to drink and willingly drew water until all his camels were satisfied would be the chosen bride.

**5.** Scarcely had his prayer ended when a maiden named **Rebekah** came out carrying a water jar upon her shoulder. Not only did she tilt her jar to let the stranger drink, she ran swiftly back to the well and drew water to fill the trough until all ten camels had drunk their fill. The steward bowed his head in thanksgiving and asked for Rebekah's hand in marriage to Isaac, carrying onward the lineage of the covenant.

---

## Chapter XXV, XXVII–XXIX, XXXII–XXXIII: The Brothers Esau and Jacob and the Ladder Reaching to Heaven

**1.** Isaac and Rebekah bore twin sons of opposite natures: the elder, **Esau**, was red and hairy, loving to hunt across the open fields; the younger, **Jacob**, was smooth-skinned and quiet, preferring to dwell among the tents.

**2.** Over the matter of the birthright and the blind old father's blessing, Jacob feared his brother's wrath and quietly left home to seek refuge in a distant land to the East.

**3.** On the road one evening just as the sun went down, Jacob paused in the wilderness, took a flat stone for his pillow, and lay down to sleep. In his dream he beheld **a great ladder rising from the earth until its top reached high heaven**, and the angels of the Most High were ascending and descending upon it. From the top of the ladder came a promise of peace, protection, and safe return to his homeland. Waking at dawn, Jacob set up the stone as a memorial pillar, poured oil upon it, and named that place **Beth-El**, meaning the House of the Most High.

**4.** Journeying onward to the distant land, Jacob met a lovely maiden named **Rachel** leading her father's flock to the well. Jacob rolled away the heavy stone from the mouth of the well so she could water her sheep, and stayed on to labor as a shepherd for fourteen long years in order to win Rachel as his wife. In that foreign land, Jacob became the father of twelve sons, the fountainhead of the twelve tribes.

**5.** Many years later on the journey back to his homeland, hearing that his brother Esau was advancing to meet him with four hundred men, Jacob's heart was filled with dread. After a night of sacred wrestling beside the brook Jabbok, where he received the new name **Israel**, the next morning Jacob walked ahead and bowed seven times to the ground before his brother. Yet Esau ran to meet him, threw his arms around his neck, kissed his brow, and both brothers wept aloud together in the embrace of reconciliation.

---

## Chapter XXXVII, XXXIX–XLV: Joseph's Coat of Many Colors and the Tearful Reunion

**1.** Among Jacob's twelve sons, **Joseph** was the firstborn of Rachel, loved best by his father, who had a bright long-sleeved coat of many colors woven for him. Joseph also dreamed strange dreams—of his sheaf of grain standing upright in the field while his brothers' sheaves bowed round about it, and of the sun, the moon, and eleven stars bowing before him—which stirred his brothers to bitter jealousy.

**2.** One day their father sent Joseph out to the fields to check on his brothers tending the flocks; the brothers plotted together, stripped off his coat of many colors, threw him into a dry pit, and sold him to a passing caravan of camel merchants bound for Egypt as a slave, then dipped his coat in goat's blood and brought it home to deceive their father into believing a wild beast had devoured him.

**3.** In Egypt, holding fast to integrity even when falsely accused and cast into a dark dungeon, Joseph became renowned for rightly interpreting the dream of the king's cupbearer. Two years later, **Pharaoh** dreamed that seven sleek, fat cows came up out of the Nile, only to be swallowed by seven gaunt, thin cows; and again he saw seven plump, full ears of grain growing on a single stalk, swallowed completely by seven withered, blighted ears.

**4.** Summoned from the dungeon, Joseph explained that both dreams foretold one truth: Egypt was about to enjoy seven years of overflowing harvest and plenty, followed directly by seven years of terrible drought and famine. He advised the king to build storehouses to gather grain during the seven plentiful years so the people might be saved from starvation. Astounded by his wisdom, Pharaoh placed his signet ring upon Joseph's finger, clothed him in fine linen, and appointed him grand vizier to govern all the land of Egypt.

**5.** When the famine spread all the way to Canaan, Joseph's brothers had to travel to Egypt to buy grain, never recognizing their younger brother in the majestic robes and headdress of the grand vizier. To test whether his brothers' hearts had changed, Joseph secretly hid his **silver cup** in the grain sack of the youngest brother, **Benjamin**, and sent soldiers in pursuit to arrest him.

**6.** When the guards demanded to keep Benjamin as a slave, the brother Judah stepped forward with choked voice and pleaded to remain behind as a slave in his young brother's stead, unable to bear watching their aged father die of grief should he lose his youngest son as well. Hearing those words, Joseph could hold back his heart no longer; he ordered every attendant out of the hall and wept aloud, saying to his brothers: *"I am Joseph! Is our father still alive?"* All the brothers embraced in tears of forgiveness, and Joseph sent wagons to bring his aged father Jacob and their entire household safely down to Egypt to survive the famine.
