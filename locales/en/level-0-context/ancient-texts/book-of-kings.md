# Books of Kings

---

## Chapter I: The Listening Heart of King Solomon

**1.** Succeeding his father King David while still very young, King **Solomon** traveled to the high sanctuary at Gibeon to offer a solemn sacrifice. That night in a dream, the Most High appeared and said to the young king: *"Ask what I shall give you."*

**2.** Solomon answered humbly that he was like a little child who knew not how to go out or come in among a people countless as the sands of the sea, and prayed: *"Give Your servant a **listening heart** to judge Your people, and to discern between good and evil."* The Most High was deeply pleased that the young ruler had asked neither for long life, nor riches, nor the lives of his enemies, but only for wisdom to serve justice; He granted Solomon a wise and discerning mind beyond any before or after him, and added honor and wealth besides.

**3.** Soon after, two women sharing one house brought a newborn infant before his throne for judgment: both had given birth within three days of each other, and during the night one child died, whereupon the bereaved mother had swapped her dead baby for the other woman's living son. Both argued fiercely before the king, each swearing that the living infant was her own.

**4.** Having heard them out, King Solomon gave command: *"Bring me a sword! Divide the living child in two, and give half to the one and half to the other."* At once the true mother, her heart torn with love for her child, fell to her knees pleading: *"O my lord king, give her the living baby, and by no means slay him!"* While the other woman answered coldly: *"He shall be neither mine nor yours; divide him!"* Immediately King Solomon pronounced judgment: *"Give the living child to the first woman and do not slay him, for she is his real mother!"* Fame of the king's wise and upright judgment spread far and wide.

## Chapter II: Building the Temple of Jerusalem in Sacred Silence

**1.** To fulfill his father's sacred wish, King Solomon sent letters of friendship to King **Hiram** of Tyre requesting the finest cedar and cypress timber from the mountains of Lebanon, to be floated in rafts along the sea to the land of Israel. Tens of thousands of skilled stonecutters and master carpenters labored side by side with devotion.

**2.** The Temple of Jerusalem was raised upon the summit of Mount Moriah according to a singular rule of reverence: every great stone block was quarried, squared, and finished completely at the distant quarry before being brought to the mount, so that **throughout all seven years of building at the sacred site, not a sound of hammer, axe, or any iron tool was heard**.

**3.** The interior of the Temple was lined throughout with cedar carved with gourds and open flowers and overlaid with gleaming pure gold; inside the Most Holy Place stood two Cherubim sculpted of olive wood ten cubits tall, spreading their golden wings above the **Ark of the Covenant** beneath. On the day of dedication, when the priests set the Ark in its appointed place and withdrew, a radiant cloud of glory filled the entire house. Kneeling before the altar with hands outstretched toward heaven, King Solomon prayed that though no temple of stone could ever contain the Most High, He would hear the sincere prayer of every soul turning toward that place—even the stranger arriving from a distant land.

---

## Chapter III: The Trial by the Queen of the South and the Sorrow of Later Years

**1.** The renown of Solomon's clear wisdom and radiant court traveled far into the South. The **Queen of Sheba** crossed vast leagues with a caravan of camels laden with rare spices, pure gold, and precious stones to Jerusalem, eager to test the king directly with the hardest riddles.

**2.** King Solomon answered smoothly every knotty question she posed, from the secrets of plants and creatures to statecraft and discerning things outwardly alike—such as telling real blossoms from countless lifelike artificial flowers by opening a window so a bee might fly in and alight upon the nectar-bearing flower. Witnessing his luminous wisdom and the stately order of the Temple and palace, the Queen of Sheba confessed in astonishment that what her eyes beheld far surpassed every report she had heard in her own country.

**3.** Yet in his later years amid boundless wealth, King Solomon gradually drifted from the single-hearted devotion of his youth, taking many foreign wives and building shrines to their idols around Jerusalem to please them. Because of that divided heart, after his reign the united kingdom fractured into two realms, North and South.

## Chapter IV: The Prophet Elijah at the Brook Cherith and the Unfailing Jar of Flour

**1.** In the days of the later kings, when the northern court fell into idol worship and persecuted all who held to uprightness, a prophet clad in a mantle of hair with a leather belt around his waist named **Elijah** appeared, declaring that for years to come neither dew nor rain should fall upon the earth.

**2.** To escape the king's wrath, Elijah hid himself beside the brook **Cherith** east of the Jordan; each day he drank clear water from the stream while **ravens** brought him bread in the morning and bread in the evening. When fierce drought dried up the brook, he journeyed to the town of Zarephath and met a poor widow gathering a few sticks beside the town gate.

**3.** When Elijah asked for a sip of water and a small crust of bread, the widow answered with choked voice that she had not a single loaf left—only one handful of flour in a jar and a drop of oil at the bottom of a jug to bake one last meal for herself and her son before they starved. Elijah gently told her not to fear, to make a small cake for him first and then prepare for herself and her child, for heaven promised that the jar of flour and the jug of oil would not fail until the day rain returned. The widow trusted his word and shared her very last portion; sure enough, throughout all the years of famine that followed, **the jar of flour never ran empty and the jug of oil never ran dry**, and her household lived in peace.

## Chapter V: Fire on Mount Carmel and the Gentle Whisper of a Breeze

**1.** In the third year of the drought, Elijah challenged four hundred and fifty priests of the false gods to assemble with all the people atop **Mount Carmel** to prove where truth stood. Both sides built stone altars and laid wood and offerings upon them without lighting any fire: whichever side prayed and saw fire descend from heaven to consume the offering would reveal the truth.

**2.** From morning until past noon, the false priests danced and shouted themselves hoarse around their altar, yet neither spark nor answer came. Toward evening, Elijah repaired the ancient altar with twelve stones for the twelve tribes, dug a trench around its base, and ordered four jars brimming with water poured over both offering and wood—repeating this three times until water overflowed and filled the trench round about.

**3.** Calmly Elijah stepped forward and offered a brief, earnest prayer. Instantly a great fire swept down from heaven, consuming the offering, the wood, the stones of the altar, the very dust, and licking up all the water in the trench! All the people fell face down in awe, acclaiming the truth made plain, and that very evening dark clouds rolled in to pour drenching rain upon the parched earth.

**4.** Later, hunted by the court until weary and alone, Elijah journeyed forty days and forty nights all the way to a cave on **Mount Horeb**. A furious gale tore the mountain and shattered rocks before the cave—yet the Most High was not in the wind. After the wind came a shaking earthquake—yet He was not in the earthquake. After the earthquake blazed a roaring fire—yet He was not in the fire either. And after the fire came **the gentle whisper of a soft breeze**. Hearing that quiet whisper, Elijah wrapped his face in his mantle and stepped out to stand at the cave entrance, listening for his new mission.

---

## Chapter VI: The Chariot of Fire and General Naaman in the River Jordan

**1.** When the day came for his earthly work to end, Elijah walked with his faithful disciple **Elisha** along the River Jordan. Together master and disciple crossed the river on dry ground as the waters parted. Walking and speaking earnestly together on the far bank, suddenly **a chariot of fire with blazing horses of fire** swept between them, separating them, and Elijah was caught up into heaven in a whirlwind. As his master's mantle fell to the earth, Elisha picked it up, draped it over his shoulders, and walked onward in his master's spirit.

**2.** In Elisha's days befell the wondrous story of **Naaman**, the valiant commander of the army of Aram, highly honored by his king yet afflicted with incurable leprosy that no medicine could heal. Serving his wife in the household was a **young Israelite girl taken captive**. Seeing her master suffer so grievously, the child spoke simply to her mistress, saying that if only her master would seek the prophet in her homeland, he would surely be healed.

**3.** Listening to the little girl's words, General Naaman set forth with chariots and horses bearing rich gifts of gold, silver, and fine garments to Elisha's door. The prophet did not even step out in ceremony, but sent word through a messenger: *"Go and wash seven times in the River Jordan, and your flesh shall be restored and made clean."*

**4.** At first Naaman turned away in anger, scorning the muddy little River Jordan as far inferior to the clear rivers of his own land. Yet his servants gently reasoned with him: *"My lord, if the prophet had asked you to do some great and difficult feat, would you not have done it? How much more, then, when he only says to wash and be clean?"* Naaman relented, obeyed, and dipped himself seven times in the Jordan; rising from the river the seventh time, his flesh became completely smooth and healthy **like the skin of a little child**.
