# Gospel According to Luke

---

## Chapter I: The Annunciation in the Temple and the Visitation in the Judean Hills

**1.** During the reign of King Herod over Judea, there lived a devout elderly priest named **Zechariah** and his gentle wife **Elizabeth**; both walked blamelessly and uprightly, yet they were well advanced in years and had no child. One day, when it fell to Zechariah's turn to enter the sanctuary of the Temple in Jerusalem to offer incense while the people prayed outside, he suddenly beheld the angel **Gabriel** standing at the right side of the incense altar.

**2.** Seeing Zechariah startled and afraid, the angel Gabriel spoke tenderly to calm him: *"Do not be afraid, Zechariah! Your prayer has been heard: your wife Elizabeth shall bear you a son, and you shall name him **John**. The child will bring gladness to many, growing up in purity to make ready an upright people to welcome the new light."* Because of his great age, Zechariah hesitantly asked for a sign; the angel answered that he would remain **mute and unable to utter a word** until the day all this came to pass.

**3.** Six months later, that same angel Gabriel was sent to a quiet village in Galilee named **Nazareth**, to a gentle maiden betrothed to a carpenter named Joseph of the house of David—and her name was **Mary**. Greeting her with reverence, the angel brought tidings that she would bear a son and call his name **Jesus**, who would inherit the throne of his forefather David and build a kingdom without end. Mary listened serenely and humbly gave her consent.

**4.** A few days later, Mary set out in haste across the hill country of Judea to visit her older cousin Elizabeth. The instant Mary's warm greeting sounded at the threshold, the unborn child within Elizabeth leaped with joy; filled with luminous gladness, Elizabeth blessed Mary, while Mary poured forth a song of praise celebrating tender love that lifts up the humble and lowly.

**5.** After staying about three months to help her cousin, Mary returned home, and soon Elizabeth gave birth to a baby boy. On the day of naming, neighbors wanted to name him after his father Zechariah, yet Elizabeth insisted he be called **John**. Turning to ask Zechariah's wish, they handed him a small writing tablet, and he wrote clearly: *"His name is **John**."* In that very instant his mouth and tongue were loosed again, and he spoke aloud in praise of the dawn of light soon to shine upon all who sit in shadow.

## Chapter II: The Winter Night in Bethlehem and Song Over the Pastures

**1.** In those days, the Roman Emperor issued a decree that a census be taken throughout the entire empire. Everyone had to travel to his ancestral town to be registered. Therefore **Joseph** too journeyed from the village of Nazareth in Galilee over hills and valleys southward into Judea, to the city of his forefather David—**Bethlehem**—accompanied by his expectant wife **Mary**.

**2.** Scarcely had they arrived when the hour came for Mary to give birth, yet not a single room was left anywhere in the town inns. She brought forth her firstborn son in a humble shelter, wrapped the newborn warmly in swaddling cloths, and **laid him in a manger**.

**3.** That night out in the fields near Bethlehem, **poor shepherds** were keeping watch over their flocks beneath the cold winter sky. Suddenly an angel appeared in the darkness, and bright glory shone round about them, filling the shepherds with terror. Kindly the angel reassured them: *"Fear not! I bring you tidings of great joy for all peoples: today in the city of David a Savior has been born to you! And this shall be your sign: you will find a newborn babe wrapped in swaddling cloths and lying in a manger."*

**4.** As soon as the words were spoken, the night sky blazed with a vast host of angels singing together across the pastures: *"Glory in the highest heavens, and **peace on earth to people of upright heart**!"* When the angels faded back into heaven, the shepherds hurried eagerly to Bethlehem and found Mary, Joseph, and the infant lying in the manger; joyfully they told everything they had heard in the fields, then returned along their way singing and giving thanks.

## Chapter III: Presentation in the Temple and Meeting Simeon and Anna

**1.** When the days of purification arrived according to ancient custom, the parents carried their child up to the Temple in **Jerusalem** to offer the humble gift of the poor—a pair of turtledoves or two young pigeons.

**2.** Living in Jerusalem was a righteous, devout old man named **Simeon**, who had waited quietly all his life for consolation to dawn upon his people. Guided by clear inner light into the Temple courts at the very hour the parents brought the child in, old Simeon cradled the infant in his trembling arms and sang in serene peace: *"Now may Your servant depart in peace, for my own eyes have beheld the light that illuminates all nations and peoples!"* Turning softly to Mary, he also spoke quietly of the deep trials her heart would share in days to come.

**3.** At that same moment came **Anna**, an aged prophetess eighty-four years old who had lived as a widow for decades in ceaseless prayer within the Temple; drawing near to look upon the child, she gave heartfelt thanks and spoke of the baby to all who waited eagerly for the redemption of Jerusalem.

## Chapter IV: The Twelfth Year in the Temple and the Lineage of Nathan

**1.** Returning to **Nazareth** in Galilee, the child grew strong in body and rich in wisdom and kindness. Every year at the great spring festival, his parents went on pilgrimage to the Temple in Jerusalem. When he reached **twelve years old**, the family once more joined the caravan of neighbors traveling up to the capital.

**2.** After the week of festival days ended and the caravan set out northward for home, the boy stayed behind in Jerusalem while his parents assumed he was walking with relatives and young companions. Having traveled a full day's journey without finding him, they hurried back to Jerusalem in alarm, searching everywhere.

**3.** On the third day, they found him seated right inside the Temple, **in the midst of the circle of teachers of the law**, listening intently and asking profound questions that left everyone astounded at the clarity and wisdom of a twelve-year-old boy. When his mother gently asked why he had caused his parents three anxious days of searching, he looked up calmly and spoke of his duty toward his Father's house, then obediently returned home with them to live simply in Nazareth, honoring his parents daily and growing ever wiser in the eyes of all.

**4.** In the genealogy preserved backward generation by generation to the dawn of time, his ancestral stream also flowed through **Nathan—another son of King David**—reaching back unbroken through Enoch, Seth, and Adam as a golden thread uniting the whole human family.
