# Ancient Traditions of Nazareth and the Dead Sea Community

---

## Chapter I: The Two Families of David's Line in the Village of Nazareth

**1.** In olden memory preserving the quiet years before new light broke along the banks of the River Jordan, a touching story is told of **two families whose fathers were both named Joseph and whose mothers were both named Mary**, springing from the two ancient branches of King David's house like the twin branches in the king's vision of old.

**2.** The first household belonged to the royal branch of **King Solomon**: the father, **Joseph**, was a seasoned, quiet master builder, and the mother, **Mary**, was noble and serene; their infant was born in Bethlehem beneath the shining star that guided wise men from the East, and endured years of exile in Egypt before returning to settle in the peaceful hillside village of **Nazareth**.

**3.** The second household belonged to the gentle branch of **Nathan**: the young father, a simple, honest carpenter, was likewise named **Joseph**, and the tender-hearted mother was likewise named **Mary**; their child was born in a warm stable cave beside the gentle breath of ox and ass amid the pasture songs of poor shepherds near Bethlehem. Not long after presenting their child in the Temple, that humble carpenter's family also returned to live right in Nazareth.

**4.** One morning beside the village well of Nazareth, the two gentle mothers who shared the name Mary chanced to meet. With a single glance of deep unspoken understanding, both mothers recognized the sacred bond uniting their two homes and remained as close as true sisters for the rest of their lives.

---

## Chapter II: Childhood Companions and the Winter Uniting the Carpenter's Shop

**1.** The two boys who both bore the name **Jesus** grew up side by side day after day among wildflower hills and the fragrant wood shavings of Nazareth's workshops, close as two halves of a single soul. The boy of Solomon's line was thoughtful and still, with deep clear eyes that read straight through ancient scrolls; his companion of Nathan's line was radiant and guileless, tuned to hear the whispers of mountain wind, meadow flowers, and wild birds.

**2.** While playing once beside the porch in childhood, the two boys found a **fledgling sparrow fallen from its nest with a broken wing**, shivering on the stone paving. With warm hands and eyes brimming with tenderness, both boys gently warmed and nursed the tiny bird until its wounded wing mended and beat strong again, soaring back into the blue sky—while their two mothers looked on in quiet pride from the doorway.

**3.** In their twelfth year, both families traveled together up to the Temple in Jerusalem for the great feast. Throughout the three days that the wise boy sat deep in earnest inquiry amid the circle of aged scholars in the Temple court, his companion of the shepherd line stood quietly beside the colonnade listening in deep stillness. Returning home from that turning-point pilgrimage, both youths felt within their innermost hearts the wondrous harmony uniting their souls into one single wellspring of life.

**4.** Several years later, harsh winters one after another carried away the elder members of both households. In that quiet bereavement, those remaining drew together around a single hearth beneath the roof of **the carpenter's shop of Nazareth**. Young Jesus worked diligently day by day at his wooden plane, listening to the grain of fragrant timber to craft sturdy, needful things for poor neighbors in the village—such as a comfortable wooden armchair built for a bent old grandmother next door so she could sit warmly in the afternoon sun.

---

## Chapter III: The White-Robed Community Beside the Dead Sea

**1.** During those very same quiet years, out in the barren limestone desert beside the **Dead Sea**, in the cliffside caves of **Qumran**, dwelt a hidden brotherhood of ascetics clothed entirely in **robes of pure white linen**. They had withdrawn from bustling cities torn by struggles for power in order to preserve ancient sacred scrolls unharmed, labor honestly with their hands, bathe in pure spring water each morning before sunrise, and share plain meals in silent equality.

**2.** When aged Zechariah and Elizabeth passed away while **John** was still a young boy, he was entrusted into the care of that white-robed desert community beside the Dead Sea under the kindly guidance of the elder master **Hiskia**. In quiet rock caves overlooking the deep salt waters, John learned by heart the ancient prophecies of the voice preparing the way in the wilderness and forged a resolute spirit that would bend before no earthly power.

**3.** Upon reaching manhood, perceiving that strict seclusion within cave monasteries could not open the door of renewal to the multitudes beyond, John bade farewell to Master Hiskia and the white-robed community and stepped out to the windswept banks of the **River Jordan**—clad in coarse camel's hair, living on locusts and wild honey, lifting his resounding call to make ready for the hour when heaven would open bright above the Jordan's waters.
