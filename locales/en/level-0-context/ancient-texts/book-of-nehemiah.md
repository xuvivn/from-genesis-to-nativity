# Book of Nehemiah

---

## Chapter I: The Cupbearer's Tears at the Palace of Susa

**1.** Many years after a remnant of exiles had been allowed to return to their homeland, in the winter capital of Susa in the Persian Empire, **Nehemiah** served as trusted cupbearer to Emperor Artaxerxes. One day travelers newly arrived from Judea brought grievous news: the stone walls surrounding **Jerusalem** still lay broken in ruins, and its gates had been burned down to charred rubble, leaving the residents in distress and disgrace.

**2.** Hearing these tidings, Nehemiah sat down and wept for his homeland, fasting and praying for many days. When next he entered to serve wine before the queen and the king, his face wore a sorrow never seen there before. When King Artaxerxes asked kindly what weighed upon his heart, Nehemiah confessed honestly how deeply it pained him that the city holding his ancestors' graves still lay in desolate ashes. Touched by such loyal devotion, the king granted Nehemiah leave from court to return and rebuild the city, furnishing him with letters of safe passage and royal warrants for timber from the king's forest to craft gate beams and fortress walls.

---

## Chapter II: The Midnight Ride of Inspection

**1.** Arriving in Jerusalem, Nehemiah rested quietly for three days without disclosing to anyone the plan stirring in his heart. By night, taking only a few trusted companions and a single mount, he slipped out silently through the Valley Gate under dim moonlight to inspect the entire circuit of shattered walls with his own eyes.

**2.** He passed along the Fountain Gate to the King's Pool, where tumbled masonry lay so thick his horse could find no footing and had to pick its way along the ravine bed. Having examined every breach in silence and returned before daybreak, the next morning Nehemiah called together all the elders, priests, and townsfolk and sounded his rallying call: *"You see the trouble we stand in: Jerusalem lies in ruins and its gates are burned with fire! Come, let us rise up and **rebuild the walls of Jerusalem**, that we may no longer suffer reproach!"* With one accord the people answered: *"Let us rise up and build!"*

---

## Chapter III: A Trowel in One Hand, a Sword in the Other

**1.** Priests, goldsmiths, perfumers, merchant households, and daughters of leading citizens alike took charge of the stretch of wall right before their own doorways, clearing rubble, raising timber gates, and laying stone beside stone without distinction of rank.

**2.** Seeing the walls steadily rising to seal the ancient breaches, neighboring enemies gathered to jeer that a patched-up wall so frail would collapse if even a fox climbed upon it, while secretly plotting a surprise raid to wreck the work.

**3.** Unshaken, Nehemiah divided the workforce to stand guard together over the city: half stood ready behind every family line armed with spears, shields, bows, and armor, while the builders who hauled stone and laid masonry worked under remarkable discipline—**with one hand they labored carrying stone and wielding the trowel, while with the other hand they held a weapon ready**; every mason wore a sharp sword strapped at his side, working beside a trumpeter prepared to sound the alarm wherever an attack struck. Throughout those days, from first light until the night stars appeared, none took off their outer garments to sleep.

**4.** In just fifty-two days of tireless, unyielding labor, the entire circuit of Jerusalem's walls stood complete and unbroken, leaving surrounding foes astounded. On the day the walls were dedicated, two great choirs marched atop the new ramparts with cymbals, lyres, and harps amid joyful shouts echoing far across the hills.
