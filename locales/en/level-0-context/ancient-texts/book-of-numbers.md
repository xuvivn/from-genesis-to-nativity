# Book of Numbers

---

## Chapter I: Forty Years in the Wilderness and the Staff That Blossomed with Almonds

**1.** Throughout the forty long years of wandering across the vast desert before entering the Promised Land, the people endured many trials of faith and obedience. When some questioned the leadership of Moses and Aaron, the Most High commanded each tribe to bring a dry wooden staff inscribed with the name of its tribal leader and lay them before the Ark of the Covenant inside the Tent of Meeting overnight.

**2.** The next morning when Moses entered the tent to inspect them, behold a marvel: among those twelve dry, lifeless staffs, the staff bearing **Aaron's** name had not only sprouted green shoots, but **put forth buds, opened white blossoms, and yielded ripe, plump almonds**. That blossoming staff was laid up with honor before the Ark of the Covenant as an everlasting sign of life springing forth from faithful devotion.

---

## Chapter II: The Bronze Serpent of Healing

**1.** Along the arduous detour around the sun-scorched land of Edom, the people once more lost heart and grumbled for lack of bread and water, complaining of the plain wilderness fare. At once fiery, venomous serpents appeared throughout the camp and bit many, bringing them to the brink of death.

**2.** Confessing their fault, the people ran to implore Moses to pray for deliverance. Following guidance from above, Moses cast a **serpent of bronze** and lifted it high upon a pole in the center of the camp. Whoever had been bitten and looked up in trust toward the bronze serpent raised upon the pole was instantly spared and kept alive.

---

## Chapter III: The Seer Balaam and the Talking Donkey

**1.** When the host of Israel drew near the plains of Moab beside the River Jordan, King Balak of Moab grew terrified at their vast multitude and sent envoys bearing rich rewards to summon the famed eastern seer **Balaam** to lay a curse upon the people of Israel.

**2.** Though warned by the Most High never to curse a people already blessed, Balaam let the lure of silver and gold urge him on; at dawn he saddled his donkey and rode out with the princes of Moab.

**3.** Along the way, an angel of the Most High stood blocking the path with a drawn sword in hand. Balaam's mortal eyes saw nothing, yet the donkey beheld the armed angel and swerved sharply off the road into a field; irritated, Balaam struck the animal to turn her back onto the highway.

**4.** At a narrow passage walled between two stone vineyard fences, the angel stood once more in the path, causing the donkey to press hard against the wall and crush Balaam's foot; again he beat her furiously. At a tight mountain defile where there was no room to turn either right or left, the donkey sank down on all fours beneath him. Seized with rage, Balaam beat the creature mercilessly with his staff.

**5.** In that very instant, the donkey opened her mouth and spoke in human speech to her master: *"What have I done to you that you have struck me these three times? Am I not your faithful donkey upon whom you have ridden all these years?"*

**6.** Then Balaam's eyes were opened wide, and he beheld the angel with drawn sword standing towering before him. Sternly the angel declared that had the donkey not turned aside three times, the sword would have struck Balaam down. Falling face down in remorse, Balaam offered to turn back, yet the angel bade him continue with the Moabite nobles on one condition: that he speak only the exact words placed in his mouth by the Most High.

**7.** Standing upon the high mountain ridge gazing down at the orderly tents of Israel stretching out like fertile valleys, instead of uttering curses as King Balak desired, Balaam's lips poured forth a resounding poem of blessing: *"How fair are your tents, O Jacob! A star shall rise out of Jacob, and a scepter shall come forth out of Israel!"* Having spoken, Balaam departed for his homeland, leaving King Balak standing dismayed upon the mountaintop.
