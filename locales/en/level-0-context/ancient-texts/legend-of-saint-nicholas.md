# Chronicles of Saint Nicholas of Myra and Winter Legends

---

## Chapter I: The Young Man of Patara and the Three Pouches of Gold by Night

**1.** Toward the close of the third century along the deep blue Mediterranean shore, in the thriving port city of **Patara** in the land of Lycia, lived a kindhearted young man named **Nicholas**. His parents died early, leaving him an abundant fortune, yet rather than spend it on luxury for himself, Nicholas remembered ever the teaching of quiet giving: let not the left hand know what the right hand does.

**2.** In that same city lived a father who had once known comfort but fallen into bitter ruin, left with three grown daughters and not a coin for their dowries, sinking so deep into hunger that in despair he was nearly forced in tears to sell his daughters into servitude simply to keep them alive. Overhearing weeping and heavy sighs from that shabby house nearby, Nicholas's heart ached with pity.

**3.** Late that night, waiting until the whole neighborhood slept deeply beneath the moonlight, Nicholas wrapped himself closely in a dark cloak, clutched a tightly tied **pouch of gold coins**, and stole quietly up to a window left slightly ajar against the warm night air. Softly he tossed the pouch of gold through the window opening and slipped swiftly away into the shadows without leaving a trace. Inside the house, **wet stockings happened to be hanging to dry beside the glowing hearth**—and the pouch of gold dropped straight inside one of the stockings! Waking at dawn to find lifesaving gold fallen as if from heaven, the father wept in joyful gratitude and arranged an honorable wedding for his eldest daughter.

**4.** On the second night and again on the third night afterward, Nicholas returned silently and tossed two more pouches of gold through the window straight into the stockings hung by the hearth, rescuing both younger sisters as well. On that third night, the father stayed awake watching from the porch, dashed out to catch the hem of his young benefactor's cloak, and sank to his knees weeping in thanks; yet Nicholas gently lifted him up and urged him to keep the secret completely untold, so the joy of giving might remain pure and unboastful.

## Chapter II: The Bishop of Myra and the Miracle of the Grain Ships

**1.** After a distant pilgrimage, Nicholas arrived in the harbor city of **Myra** at the very hour the local congregation stood without a shepherd. Following a wise sign agreed upon by the elders meeting through the night—to choose the first man named Nicholas who crossed the church threshold at daybreak—the humble young man walked in at dawn wearing a **warm deep-red cloak** to say his morning prayers, and was joyfully acclaimed by all the people as **Bishop of Myra**.

**2.** Serving as spiritual father of that coastal town, Nicholas became renowned as a steadfast protector of sailors caught in fierce tempests at sea, a guardian of abandoned children, and a fearless advocate for the wrongly accused.

**3.** One year, severe famine struck the land of Lycia until granaries stood empty and old and young grew pale and weak with hunger. Just then a fleet of **imperial merchant ships laden to the brim with wheat** from Egypt put into Myra harbor for shelter on their voyage to the capital of **Constantinople**. Bishop Nicholas walked down onto the quay and earnestly begged the sailors to spare a portion of grain from each vessel to save the starving townsfolk.

**4.** Torn between pity for the hungry and terror for their lives, the sailors hesitated—every kernel had been strictly weighed and recorded by Egyptian officials, and any shortage upon unloading in Constantinople would cost the entire crew their heads! Calmly Nicholas pledged his word in deepest trust that mercy would never bring them loss. Trusting the gentle bishop, the sailors opened the hatches and shared out enough wheat to sustain all the region of Myra for two full years; and wondrously, when the ships later cast anchor at Constantinople and weighed their cargo once more, **the weight of wheat in the holds remained whole and undiminished down to a single grain!**

## Chapter III: The Winter Legend Journey Around the World

**1.** After Nicholas entered peaceful rest on **December 6**, tales of the Bishop in the red cloak who dropped pouches of gold silently into hearthside stockings and carried life-saving grain across the sea lived on in folk memory across the centuries. From Mediterranean harbors, seafarers bore the image of that merciful protector throughout wintry Europe.

**2.** In snowy northern lands and the coastal country of the Netherlands, the saint's affectionate name softly shifted into **Sinterklaas**: every early December, parents told children of the gentle white-bearded bishop wearing a tall miter and red cloak who sailed across the great sea and rode a white steed across snow-covered rooftops, dropping sweet hazelnuts and spiced gingerbread down into wooden shoes or **woolen stockings hung beside the hearth** for kind children who learned to share.

**3.** Later, carried aboard sailing ships across the Atlantic to the new world from the old port of **New Amsterdam**, those cherished stories blended with the warm glow of snowy Christmas Eve and candlelit evergreen trees, flowering into the beloved figure of **Santa Claus**—kindly, white-bearded, dressed in warm red trimmed with white fur. Though centuries pass and his outer coat shifts in form from age to age, the warm flame from ancient Myra burns unchanged: **whenever a quiet hand gives away joy without asking return, the light of that first winter night shines bright anew within the human heart.**
