# Book of Exodus

---

## Chapter I–II: Bondage in Egypt and the Infant in the Reed Basket on the Nile

**1.** Many generations passed after the day Joseph's family came down into Egypt, and the descendants of Israel multiplied and grew mighty throughout the delta of the Nile. A new Pharaoh ascended the throne who remembered nothing of Joseph's deeds; growing fearful of their vast numbers, he forced the people of Israel into harsh slavery, kneading clay for bricks, building store cities, and toiling endlessly in the fields.

**2.** Not stopping there, the king issued a cruel decree that every newborn Hebrew boy must be cast into the Nile. At that time a woman bore a fair and radiant baby boy; she hid her child for three months, and when she could conceal him no longer, she wove a **basket of papyrus reeds**, coated it tightly with bitumen and pitch to keep out the water, laid the child inside, and set it among the reeds along the riverbank while the baby's older sister stood watching from afar.

**3.** Pharaoh's daughter came down to the river to bathe, spied the basket among the tall reeds, and sent her maidservant to fetch it. Opening the lid, she saw the infant weeping, and her heart was moved with compassion. Quickly the baby's sister ran up and asked if she should fetch a Hebrew nurse to feed the child, and brought the infant's own mother to care for him. The princess adopted the child as her son and named him **Moses**, meaning *"drawn out of the water."*

**4.** When Moses grew to manhood, though raised inside the Egyptian palace, he went out to visit his kindred enduring blows and bitter labor. After defending a slave who was being brutally beaten, Moses had to flee Egypt for the wilderness of **Midian**. At a well in that foreign land, he helped the seven daughters of the priest Jethro draw water for their flocks, was invited to stay, married Zipporah, and lived for many years as a quiet shepherd.

---

## Chapter III–IV: The Flame in the Burning Bush on Mount Horeb

**1.** One day, while leading his father-in-law's flock across the wilderness to the foot of **Mount Horeb**—the holy mountain—Moses looked up and beheld a wondrous sight: **a thornbush blazing fiercely with fire, yet its branches and leaves remained green and unconsumed**.

**2.** Moses told himself he would draw near to see why the bush did not burn away. Scarcely had he stepped closer when a voice called out from the midst of the bush: *"Moses! Moses! Come no nearer; take off your sandals from your feet, for the place where you stand is holy ground."* Moses hid his face in trembling awe.

**3.** The voice from the midst of the flame spoke on: *"I have surely seen the affliction of My people in Egypt and heard their cry beneath the lash of their oppressors. Now I send you to Pharaoh to bring My people out of Egypt, into a broad and fertile land flowing with milk and honey."*

**4.** When Moses asked hesitantly in whose name he should speak to the people, the Most High revealed the sacred name: *"I AM WHO I AM."* He bade Moses take up the shepherd's staff in his hand—the staff that turned into a serpent and back into a staff again—and appointed his elder brother **Aaron** as spokesman to accompany him back to Egypt.

---

## Chapter VII–XII: The Ten Plagues and the Night of Passover

**1.** Returning to Egypt, Moses and Aaron went many times before Pharaoh to ask that the people of Israel be allowed a three-day journey into the wilderness to offer sacrifice, yet Pharaoh hardened his heart and refused to listen, laying even heavier burdens of brickmaking upon the people.

**2.** One after another, ten plagues struck the land of Egypt: the waters of the Nile turned red as blood, frogs swarmed through the palaces, gnats, flies, pestilence upon livestock, boils, hail mingled with lightning fire, locusts darkening the sky, and three days of thick darkness where no one could see another's face—while in the dwellings of Israel there remained light.

**3.** Before the tenth plague, the Most High instructed each household to choose an unblemished lamb and brush its blood upon the two doorposts and the lintel of their house; that night the whole family ate the roasted lamb with unleavened bread and bitter herbs, with loins girded, sandals on their feet, and staff in hand, ready for departure.

**4.** At midnight the messenger passed through the land of Egypt; seeing the mark of blood upon the doorframe of a house, he **passed over** and spared those within, yet throughout Egypt from the king's palace to the dungeon rang the cry of mourning for the firstborn. That very night Pharaoh hastily summoned Moses and Aaron and ordered the entire people of Israel to gather their belongings and depart from Egypt at once. Carrying with them the bones of their ancestor Joseph according to his dying oath long before, the Israelites set out toward the sea.

---

## Chapter XIV–XV: The Parting of the Red Sea and the Song of Victory

**1.** Not long after Israel set forth, Pharaoh changed his mind and regretted losing his labor force, mustering six hundred chosen chariots and all his horsemen in pursuit. While the people were encamped beside the **Red Sea**, seeing deep waters before them and the dust cloud of Egyptian chariots behind them, the crowd cried out in terror.

**2.** Moses steadied the people: *"Fear not! Stand firm and behold the deliverance of this day."* Then, at the command from heaven, Moses stretched out his hand holding the staff over the sea; all night long a strong east wind blew, driving back the waters to either side and turning the seabed into dry ground.

**3.** All the people of Israel marched through the midst of the sea on dry land, with **two towering walls of water** standing to their right and to their left. When the Egyptian charioteers recklessly plunged down into the seabed in pursuit, their chariot wheels sank and clogged in the mire; and as soon as Israel had safely reached the far shore, Moses stretched out his hand once more over the sea, and the two walls of water crashed back into place, swallowing the entire host of chariots and horsemen.

**4.** Standing safely upon the shore as dawn broke, Moses and all the people lifted their voices in a song of thanksgiving, while the prophetess **Miriam**—sister of Aaron and Moses—took up a tambourine and led all the women in dance and antiphonal song praising the Deliverer.

---

## Chapter XVI–XVII: Manna from Heaven and Water from the Rock

**1.** Entering the barren wilderness short of provisions, the weary people looked back wistfully to the fleshpots of Egypt. At dusk the Most High sent flocks of quail settling around the camp, and early the next morning when the dew lifted, the desert floor lay covered with fine white flakes like frost, sweet as wafers made with honey. The people asked one another in wonder, *"Man hu?"* (meaning *"What is this?"*), and from then on called that bread of heaven **Manna**. Each household was to gather only enough for a single day's portion; whoever greedily hoarded it overnight found it spoiled, save on the sixth day when they gathered a double portion for the seventh day of rest, and it kept fresh and sweet.

**2.** At a parched stopping place where there was no water to drink, Moses did as instructed and struck the rock at Horeb with the very staff that had struck the Nile; immediately from the cleft in the hard rock gushed forth a cool, abundant stream enough for all the multitude and their flocks to drink their fill.

**3.** When the tribe of Amalek marched out to ambush the travelers in the valley of Rephidim, young **Joshua** led the warriors out to battle while Moses stood atop the hill holding the staff high in his hand. Whenever Moses raised his hands high, Israel prevailed; whenever his arms grew weary and drooped, the enemy pressed forward. Seeing this, Aaron and Hur placed a stone for Moses to sit upon, and stood one on either side holding his arms steady until sunset, securing complete victory for Joshua.

---

## Chapter XIX–XX, XXXII, XXXV–XL: The Ten Commandments on Mount Sinai and the Tabernacle

**1.** In the third month after leaving Egypt, the whole nation encamped at the foot of **Mount Sinai**. On the morning of the third day, thunder and lightning rolled across the summit, thick clouds shrouded the peak, a horn blast resounded louder and louder, and the mountain trembled in fire and smoke.

**2.** Moses ascended the peak for forty days and forty nights and received the two stone tablets inscribed with the **Ten Commandments**, teaching humankind to revere the one Most High alone, to fashion no graven idols, to honor father and mother, not to murder, not to steal, not to bear false witness, and not to covet what belongs to another.

**3.** While waiting so long for Moses to return from the mountain, the people below grew impatient, gathered their golden earrings, cast them into a **Golden Calf**, and held a festival of dancing and singing around the image. Descending the mountain with the two tablets in his arms and beholding the scene, Moses in fierce anger dashed the stone tablets to pieces at the foot of the mountain, melted down the golden idol, and restored order in the camp. Afterward he climbed the mountain again for forty days to plead forgiveness for the people and engrave two new tablets of the covenant.

**4.** Returning from the mountain with his face shining with light, Moses called upon skilled artisans to contribute gold and silver, blue, purple, and crimson yarn, fine linen, and acacia wood to build the portable **Tabernacle** according to the exact pattern shown to him on the mountain. Inside the Most Holy Place stood the **Ark of the Covenant** of acacia wood overlaid with pure gold, its lid sheltered by the outspread wings of two golden Cherubim, while outside stood the seven-branched golden lampstand and the altar of incense. When all the work was finished, the cloud of light of the Most High filled the Tabernacle, guiding the people throughout their desert journey: a pillar of cloud shading them by day, and a pillar of fire lighting their path by night.
