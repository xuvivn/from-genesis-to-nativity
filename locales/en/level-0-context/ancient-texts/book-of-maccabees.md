# Books of Maccabees

---

## Chapter I: The Prophet Jeremiah Hides the Ark on Mount Nebo

**1.** In the ancient records recounting history before the city's devastation and exile, it is written how the prophet **Jeremiah**, warned by clear revelation before the disaster, instructed attendants to carry the **Tent of Meeting**, the **Ark of the Covenant**, and the **altar of incense** out of the city with him before the flames of war consumed the Temple.

**2.** He led the party to the lofty mountain where Moses had once stood and looked upon the promised inheritance—**Mount Nebo**. Midway up the slopes, Jeremiah discovered a **deep, hidden cave**; inside he reverently placed the Ark of the Covenant, the Tent of Meeting, and the altar of incense, then sealed the cave entrance carefully.

**3.** When several companions who followed tried to mark the trail back, they could find no trace of the path again. Seeing this, Jeremiah solemnly declared that the sacred hiding place must remain unknown until the day the people are gathered once more in mercy and the bright light shines forth as of old.

## Chapter II: The Angelic Horseman and General Heliodorus at the Temple Treasury

**1.** Centuries later, after the Temple of Jerusalem had been rebuilt and dwelt in peace under the care of the gentle High Priest **Onias**, the northern royal court heard malicious rumors that the Temple treasury overflowed with untold wealth and dispatched the royal minister **Heliodorus** with troops to confiscate it all.

**2.** Arriving in Jerusalem, despite High Priest Onias's earnest explanation that the funds deposited in the Temple treasury were **savings held in trust to support widows and fatherless orphans**, along with private deposits of trusting citizens rather than ownerless spoils, Heliodorus stubbornly ordered his soldiers straight into the treasury. The entire city fell to its knees in stunned prayer through the streets.

**3.** The very moment Heliodorus and his bodyguard crossed the threshold of the treasury, there appeared before their eyes a **majestic rider clad in gleaming golden armor**, mounted upon a magnificently caparisoned charger that charged forward and struck the general flat to the ground with its front hooves, while two radiant young angels materialized on either side and lashed him relentlessly until he collapsed unconscious. His soldiers had to carry Heliodorus out on a stretcher, utterly speechless.

**4.** Fearing the emperor's wrath should word spread that royal officers were felled in Jerusalem, his aides rushed to beg High Priest Onias to pray for Heliodorus's life. Generously Onias offered prayers and sacrifice for the general's recovery. Upon reviving, Heliodorus immediately gave thanks and returned to tell the king plainly that a power guarding justice watched over that Temple which no earthly force could ever violate.

## Chapter III: The Uprising of Judas Maccabeus and the Eight-Day Flame

**1.** Not long afterward, the tyrant **Antiochus** seized the capital, plundered the Temple, outlawed every ancestral observance, burned the sacred scrolls, and sought to force the people to abandon the faith of their forebears. Amid that dark hour, in the village of Modein, the aged priest **Mattathias** and his five brave sons refused to yield, retreating into the rugged hills to raise the banner of revolt for freedom of conscience and uprightness.

**2.** Before his death, Mattathias passed command to his third son, **Judas**—surnamed **Maccabeus**, meaning *"The Iron Hammer."* With a small but fearless band, Judas Maccabeus and his comrades shattered one invading army after another, never flinching even before towering **columns of war elephants** bearing wooden turrets packed with archers on their backs.

**3.** Marching in to liberate Jerusalem, seeing the Temple desolate with weeds sprouting across the courtyards, gates blackened by fire, and the altar profaned, the fighters wept aloud in grief, then set to work at once clearing the sanctuary, building a new altar of unhewn stones untouched by iron, and forging fresh sacred vessels.

**4.** On the day the Temple was cleansed and rededicated, when they sought to relight the **seven-branched lampstand** according to ancient custom, the purified sacred oil found in the sanctuary was scarcely enough for a single day; yet wondrously the lampstand **blazed brightly without ceasing for eight full days and nights** amid joyful music and song. The entire people ordained an annual eight-day **Feast of Lights** to remember the undying flame of faithfulness.

---

## Chapter IV: The Bronze Tablet of Alliance with Rome

**1.** Perceiving that **Rome** in the West was rising in mighty power, keeping faith with its allies and checking the violence of cruel eastern kingdoms, Judas Maccabeus chose two wise envoys to sail across the sea to Rome to tell of their nation's struggle and seek a pact of friendship.

**2.** The Roman Senate received them warmly and agreed to establish a **treaty of equal friendship and mutual aid**, engraving the full terms upon **bronze tablets** sent back to be kept in Jerusalem—opening the first direct bond between the ancient holy land in the East and the power of Rome soon to enter the dawn of a new era.
