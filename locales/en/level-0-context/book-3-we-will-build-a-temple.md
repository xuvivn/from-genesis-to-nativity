# Level 0 — Book III: We Will Build a Temple (32 Chapters)

## 1. Cultural Atmosphere & Core Spirit

Book III opens at the brightest zenith of the united kingdom and passes through the fiercest tempest of ancient history: **from the day King Solomon dedicated the magnificent Temple until that Temple burned to ashes, the people were carried away into exile in Babylon, and slowly they returned to lay again every stone of the city walls**.

The deepest spiritual turning point of Book III lies in a single question: *When the outward Temple of gold and stone is gone, where do human beings keep the sacred flame?* The answer shines forth through one extraordinary life after another: **the wise heart** of Solomon, **the gentle whisper of wind on Mount Horeb** heard by Elijah, **the healing journey** of Tobias, **the burning coal** upon the lips of Isaiah, Jeremiah's rescue of the Ark of the Covenant, Daniel's steadfast courage in the fiery furnace and the lions' den, Jonah's mercy growing larger than his prejudice, Job's unyielding faith, and the rekindled flame of Judas Maccabeus.

---

## 2. Distinctive Storytelling Rhythm (Ages 9–10)

- **Radiant, Solemn Splendor (Chapters 1–6):** Like cedarwood shaped in deep silence on the sacred building site, culminating in the quiet clarity of King Solomon's judgment revealing a true mother's love.
- **Firestorm and Sacred Stillness (Chapters 7–9, 12–17):** Bold and decisive during the contest of fire on Mount Carmel and as Jeremiah rescues the Ark of the Covenant through the mist of Mount Nebo, woven together with hushed, whispered pauses as Elijah sits listening to *"the gentle whisper of wind."*
- **Warm Folktale Wandering and Keen Detective Wit (Chapters 10–11, 18–24):** Tobias's journey—with a faithful little dog trotting happily ahead—breathes the air of fairy-tale adventure; meanwhile, Daniel's exposure of the false feast of Bel by sifting fine ashes across the temple floor has a cleverness that 9- and 10-year-olds delight in.
- **Deep Inner Dialogue (Chapters 25–32):** Gentle humor with Jonah's gourd vine, deepening into quiet reflection on compassion; tragic grandeur as Job sits in the dark night and speaks his trust in the Redeemer; then rising into bright celebration at the relighting of the Temple lamps under Judas Maccabeus and the footsteps of the alliance with Rome.

---

## 3. Touchpoints & Metaphors for Ages 9–10

- **Key Touchpoint:** The courage to speak the truth and hold fast to what is right when the crowd goes astray. Solomon listens to two mothers claiming one child; Tobias stays honest and helps the poor even in danger; the three young men in the furnace and Daniel in the lions' den refuse to abandon their faith out of fear; Jonah learns to outgrow selfishness; and Job keeps hope alive in the depths of loss.
- **Core Metaphor:** When the Temple of brick and stone is torn down, those who keep faith with the promise (Jeremiah hiding the Ark, Nehemiah building with a trowel in one hand while holding a sword on watch with the other) become **living stones** rebuilding the house of peace.

## 4. Pronunciation Guide for Proper Names & Ancient Places

| Name in Text | Natural Read-Aloud Pronunciation (English) | Story Context |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Solomon – Hiram** | *SOL-uh-mun – HY-rum* | The wise king and the master bronze-smith who built the Temple (Chapters 1–6) |
| **Elijah – Elisha** | *ih-LY-juh – ih-LY-shuh* | The prophet of fire on Mount Carmel and the disciple who received his mantle (Chapters 7–9) |
| **Tobit – Tobias – Azariah** | *TOH-bit – toh-BY-us – az-uh-RY-uh* | The blind father in exile, his son, and the angel Raphael traveling in disguise (Chapters 10–11) |
| **Isaiah – Jeremiah** | *eye-ZAY-uh – jair-uh-MY-uh* | Two great prophets during the siege and fall of the Temple (Chapters 12–17) |
| **Nebuchadnezzar – Daniel** | *neb-yoo-kud-NEZ-ur – DAN-yul* | The Babylonian emperor and the young captive who interpreted dreams at court (Chapters 18–24) |
| **Belshazzar** | *bel-SHAZ-ur* | The king who saw the mysterious hand writing upon the stone wall (Chapter 21) |
| **Nehemiah – Jonah** | *nee-uh-MY-uh – JOH-nuh* | The rebuilder of Jerusalem's walls and the prophet inside the great fish (Chapters 25–27) |
| **Heliodorus – Job** | *hee-lee-uh-DOR-us – JOHB* | The commander sent to plunder the Temple treasury and the upright man of Uz (Chapters 28–30) |
| **Judas Maccabeus – Rome** | *JOO-dus mak-uh-BEE-us – ROHM* | The hero who restored the Temple and forged an alliance with Rome (Chapters 31–32) |
