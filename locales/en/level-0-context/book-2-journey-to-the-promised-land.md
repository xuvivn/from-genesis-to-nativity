# Level 0 — Book II: Journey to the Promised Land (56 Chapters)

## 1. Cultural Atmosphere & Core Spirit

After the Great Flood, humankind forgot the lesson of humility and gathered around proud King Nimrod beside the rivers of Mesopotamia, baking bricks to raise the **Tower of Babel** in an attempt to storm the heavens by their own strength. When the tower fell and seventy languages divided the peoples, history turned toward the pilgrimage of a wandering family who dared to leave walled cities and deep moats behind, pitching their tents in the open desert, lifting their eyes to the stars, and listening for the voice within conscience.

Book II walks through seven great stages—**Abraham, Isaac, Jacob, Joseph, Moses, Joshua, and Saul & David**—and closes with King David's wondrous dream, in which two branches of his descendants (Solomon and Nathan) weave together into **the World Tree** to welcome the light of the coming Savior.

---

## 2. Distinctive Storytelling Rhythm (Ages 9–10)

- **A Steady, Measured Traveler's Pace (Chapters 1–17):** Like camels stepping across the open grasslands; brief bursts of tension at Nimrod's fiery furnace and atop Mount Moriah, melting into serene peace beneath Abraham's spreading tree and Jacob's dream of the Ladder of Light at Beth-El.
- **Court Drama and Tears of Reunion (Chapters 18–24):** Moving from the coat of many colors, the snake-filled pit, Potiphar's household, the dungeon, and Pharaoh's dream of seven plump and seven withered ears of grain, to the moment Joseph can no longer hold back his tears as he embraces his youngest brother Benjamin and brings his aged father down into Egypt.
- **Heroic Epic Sweep (Chapters 25–44):** The infant Moses drifting in a reed basket, the trial of the burning coals, the flame of the Burning Bush, the Ten Plagues, the Red Sea standing upright like two walls of water, the seven heavens opened above Mount Sinai, Balaam's talking donkey, and the trumpet blasts bringing down the walls of Jericho.
- **Lyrical Music Mingled with Tragic Grandeur (Chapters 45–56):** Warm harp music soothing King Saul's dark melancholy; swift, breathless motion as the smooth stone strikes the giant Goliath's forehead; deep, solemn stillness in the cave as David bows in homage before his king; and a ringing hymn-like cadence as King David dreams of the World Tree.

---

## 3. Touchpoints & Metaphors for Ages 9–10

- **Key Touchpoint:** Every hero in Book II learns how to mend past mistakes and grow: Jacob once deceived his brother, yet turned back to seek reconciliation; Joseph's brothers repented before the silver cup; an impatient people who cast the Golden Calf had to learn forty years of discipline; and David refused another man's heavy armor so he could fight with the very skill he practiced every day.
- **Core Metaphor:** God's dwelling place is not merely a grand palace; it begins in a faithful heart (the movable Tabernacle carried across the desert) and in hands unstained by vengeful blood (David leaving the building of the Temple to the peaceful reign of his son).

## 4. Pronunciation Guide for Proper Names & Ancient Places

| Name in Text | Natural Read-Aloud Pronunciation (English) | Story Context |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Nimrod / Sandalphon** | *NIM-rod / san-DAL-fon* | The proud king who built the Tower of Babel and the dark spirit urging him on (Chapters 1–5) |
| **Abram / Abraham – Sarah** | *AY-brum / AY-bruh-ham – SAIR-uh* | The patriarch who stepped unharmed from the furnace and journeyed with his wife to Canaan (Chapters 3–11) |
| **Isaac – Rebekah** | *EYE-zuk – rih-BEK-uh* | Abraham's son and the bride who drew water at the desert well (Chapters 10–14) |
| **Esau – Jacob – Rachel** | *EE-saw – JAY-kub – RAY-chul* | The twin brothers and the bride for whom Jacob labored fourteen years (Chapters 13–17) |
| **Joseph – Benjamin** | *JOH-zef – BEN-juh-min* | The dreamer in the many-colored coat who became Egypt's governor, and his youngest brother (Chapters 18–24) |
| **Moses – Aaron – Miriam** | *MOH-ziz – AIR-un – MEER-ee-um* | The two brothers and their sister who led the Exodus through the Red Sea (Chapters 25–41) |
| **Balaam – Joshua** | *BAY-lum – JOSH-oo-uh* | The seer riding a talking donkey, and the leader who crossed the Jordan (Chapters 39–44) |
| **Samuel – Saul – David** | *SAM-yoo-ul – SAWL – DAY-vid* | The anointing prophet, Israel's first king, and the shepherd boy who played the harp (Chapters 45–56) |
| **Goliath – Jonathan** | *guh-LY-uth – JON-uh-thun* | The giant champion and the loyal prince who was David's dearest friend (Chapters 49–52) |
